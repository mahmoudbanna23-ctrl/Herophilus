## kill-pharos-dark

The great lighthouse of Alexandria seen from high in the night air beside it, looking down and
across the harbour toward the city.

The lighthouse stands in the LEFT third of the picture, large and near. It runs from below the
bottom edge of the picture almost to the top edge, so its foot is out of frame. It has three clear
tiers: a tall square lower tier of pale limestone blocks with a few small dark window slits, a
shorter eight-sided middle tier, and a small round top tier, an open ring of columns holding a
warm fire glow. We see the tower corner-on: two faces of the square tier, the right face catching
faint warm light, the left face in blue shadow. Its edges are straight and its sides vertical.

Behind and far below the tower: the wide curved harbour, deep blue water with long soft
reflections, a long stone causeway, a few anchored wooden ships with furled sails. Along the far
shore the city: low flat-roofed houses, long colonnades, one temple front with a triangular
pediment, the curved stone seating of an open theatre, a walled garden of palms and cypress, and
many small amber windows. Low mist lies on the water and between the buildings. The horizon sits
in the upper third; above it a deep blue night sky with thin drifting cloud and a few stars.

The space between the tower and the city is large and open: nothing touches or overlaps the
tower except sky, water and far shore.

## kill-pharos-dark-v2

The same view exactly, with ONE change: the lighthouse is repainted with far richer, older,
hand-painted stonework. Its outline, size, position, three tiers and fire glow stay exactly where
they are.

The stone is ancient and hand-laid: blocks of clearly uneven size and tone, some darker, a few
paler replacements, chipped and rounded corners, hairline cracks, dark soot streaks under each
window slit, pale salt stains near the top, green-black damp at the lowest courses, mortar lines
that wander. No regular grid, no repeated pattern, no clean computer-made surface. Carved
mouldings mark the ledge between tiers, worn and broken in places. Painted with confident visible
brushwork, like a film matte painting. The warm fire glow spills softly down the upper stone.

## city-dark

The whole harbour city of Alexandria at night, seen from the air at a three-quarter angle, about
forty degrees down, as if we had just flown in over the water and come to rest above it. This one
picture is the map of the place: FOUR landmark buildings, each standing clearly apart from the
others with open ground, water or low roofs between them, each lit by its own warm lamps so the eye
finds all four at once. They are about the same visual weight; none hides another.

1. THE LIGHTHOUSE, left of centre and furthest back, on a small rocky island at the harbour mouth,
   joined to the city by a long straight stone causeway. Three tiers: a tall square lower tier of
   pale limestone, a shorter eight-sided middle tier, a small round top tier with an open ring of
   columns holding a warm fire glow. Seen whole, from foot to fire, with dark water around it.
2. THE THEATRE, right side, middle distance: an open-air Greek theatre, a great half-circle of
   stepped stone seating cut into a low hill, facing a flat round floor and a low stage wall with
   columns. Small oil lamps along the top row and the stage. Empty.
3. THE TEMPLE, centre, on a broad raised stone platform reached by wide steps: a columned temple
   with a triangular pediment and a tiled roof, fire bowls burning at the foot of the steps.
4. THE GARDEN, lower left foreground: a walled garden with a gate, tall date palms and dark
   cypress, a small square pool reflecting lamplight, low flowering shrubs, a few hanging lamps.

Between and around them: low flat-roofed houses with small amber windows, long colonnades, narrow
paved streets, a curved quay with a few anchored wooden ships with furled sails. Thin low mist lies
on the water and in the streets. The horizon is high, in the top fifth of the picture; above it a
calm deep blue night sky with thin cloud and a few stars, and the open sea beyond the lighthouse.

LAYOUT. All four landmarks sit well inside the picture: nothing important in the outer tenth on
any side. The top fifth is quiet sky and sea. The lower right corner is a plain, calm stretch of
moonlit stone quay with nothing on it. Beside each landmark there is a small area of quiet, dark,
simple ground or water (room for a caption to be laid over the picture later; do not paint any
caption, sign or plaque).

## common-city

STYLE. The same painted world as the style reference image: smooth, glossy, soft-lit, hand-painted
3D finish, calm and cinematic, rich in small believable detail (worn stone edges, individual
blocks, roof tiles, ripples, rigging). Painterly, never photographic, never flat vector.

SETTING. Ancient Alexandria, about 300 BC, at night, seen from the air. Normal lens, no fisheye,
no tilt: vertical lines stay vertical and parallel.

LIGHT. Moonlight and small fires only. Cool night-blue shadows (deep blue, never brown, never pure
black, every surface keeps visible detail) with small warm amber points of lamp and fire light.

HARD RULES.
- No text of any kind: no letters, numerals, inscriptions, labels, signage or carved writing.
- No people, no figures, no statues of people, no faces, no hands, no living animals.
- No frame, no border, no vignette, no watermark, no signature, no neon, no lens flare, no light
  beam sweeping the sky.
- No modern objects: nothing electric, printed, plastic or machine-made.

## common-room-matte

STYLE. A hand-painted film matte painting in gouache and oil: MATTE, dry, chalky surfaces,
visible confident brushstrokes, soft broken edges, slightly uneven hand-mixed colour. NOT glossy,
NOT shiny, NOT wet-looking, NOT plastic, no specular highlights, no bloom, no glow halos, no
computer-game or 3D-render look, not photographic, not flat vector. Rich in small believable
detail (worn stone edges, wood grain, frayed papyrus), painted loosely rather than modelled.

SETTING. A room inside the great lighthouse of ancient Alexandria, about 300 BC, at night. Seen
from standing height, straight on. Normal lens, no fisheye, no tilt: vertical lines stay vertical.

LIGHT. Moonlight through the window and small oil lamps only. Cool night-blue shadows (deep blue,
never brown, never pure black, every surface keeps visible detail) with small warm amber pools of
lamp light, painted as soft dabs of warm colour, not as glowing bulbs.

HARD RULES.
- No text of any kind: no letters, numerals, inscriptions, labels, or writing on the scrolls or
  the papyrus sheet.
- No people, no figures, no statues of people, no faces, no hands, no living animals.
- No frame, no border, no vignette, no watermark, no signature, no neon, no lens flare.
- No modern objects: nothing electric, printed, plastic or machine-made. No books with spines:
  scrolls only.
- Exactly ONE shelf of scrolls, ONE desk, ONE sand-glass, ONE chest.
