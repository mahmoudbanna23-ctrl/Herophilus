# Amblyopia ( Lazy eye)
[spacing as printed — a space after the opening parenthesis]

Definition:

- It is **a neurodevelopmental** visual disorder with **unilateral** (or less commonly,
  bilateral) **reduction of best-corrected visual acuity** that cannot be corrected by glasses
  in an otherwise healthy eye.
- It is caused by an abnormal visual experience early in life and cannot be remedied
  immediately by spectacle glasses alone

Predisposing factors:

1. **Strabismus** (Approximately half of the cases mainly esotropia).
   [「esotropia」 printed in dark red]
2. **Anisometropia** (asymmetric refractive errors).
3. **Form deprivation and occlusion amblyopia** (Opacities in ocular media such as cataract,
   corneal scarring, or upper eyelid ptosis.
   [sic — the opening parenthesis is never closed]
4. **Bilateral uncorrected refractive errors:**
   (Amblyopia is more with high hypermetropia)
   [「high hypermetropia」 printed in dark red]

[Figure, right of the Predisposing factors list, uncaptioned apart from its two column
labels: two brow-and-eye pairs headed "Non-Amblyopic eye" and "Amblyopic eye". Beneath each,
a cone of light descends onto a drawing of a tree — the tree under the non-amblyopic eye is
rendered sharp and full, the tree under the amblyopic eye is rendered blurred and thin. A
schematic of degraded image quality, not a clinical photograph.]

Detection:

Amblyopia is diagnosed when the visual acuity is less than normal with **no** pathology seen
in the eye.
[「no」 printed in dark red]

- In older children:  Visual acuity is measured using charts
- In preverbal children: vision can be estimated by other methods that include:
  [「preverbal」 highlighted in yellow]
  - Observing fixation: Eyes with good acuity have central, steady and maintained fixation.
  - Resistance to occlusion of one eye.

Prognosis:

- Good prognosis if treated early in life.
- The older the child is, the less the response to treatment.

Treatment:

Amblyopia treatment is less successful after age 8-10 years.
[「less successful after age 8-10 years」 printed in dark red]

1. **Treatment of the cause:**
   - spectacles for anisometropia
   - treat media opacity or ptosis… etc.
2. **Occlusion Therapy**:
   Patching the **good** eye to force the brain to use the non-dominant eye and redevelop its
   vision
   [「good」 printed in dark red]
3. **Atropine cycloplegic drops** to impair accommodation and blur vision of the better-seeing
   eye

[Photograph, bottom right: a young girl wearing spectacles, the right lens covered by a
decorative fabric patch. Caption in bold blue: "spectacles + eyepatching"]

# The Red Reflex Test

[Two photographs at the top, side by side. Left: a close-up of one eye with a bright orange-red

  examination
- It should be done by paediatricians in all well-child visits
- **Importance :**
  It is vital for the early detection of vision- and potentially life-threatening abnormalities
  such as:
  [「such as:」 underlined]
  1. Cataracts
  2. Glaucoma
  3. Retinoblastoma
  4. retinal abnormalities
  5. systemic diseases with ocular manifestations
  6. high refractive errors.

  ophthalmologist.

# Retinopathy of prematurity

Definition:

- It is a **vasoproliferative retinopathy** that is a major cause of blindness in the
  developing world.
- Its incidence is **increasing** because of the improved survival of preterm infants.

Pathology:

- ROP occurs in **preterm** infants due to incomplete vascularization of the retina.
- ROP is a **two-phase disease:**
  1. beginning with delayed retinal vascular growth after premature birth
  2. followed by the growth of pathological new vessels that end up in fibrosis and retinal
     detachment.
     The growth of new vessels is stimulated by the secretion of vascular endothelial growth
     factors (VEGF)

**Risk Factors:**
[blue bold, centred over its list]

- Low birth weight of less than **1500 gm.**
- Gestational age less than **33 weeks.**
  [both figures printed bold in dark red]

[Diagram, right: a labelled cross-section of a globe split down the midline, headed
"Retinopathy of Prematurity - ROP". The left half is labelled "Normal" and shows an orderly
branching retinal vasculature; the right half is labelled "ROP Disease" and shows a cluster of
dark red beaded vessels at the periphery with an arrow labelled "Abnormal blood vessel growth".]

# Nasolacrimal system abnormalities

**Definition**:

Congenital obstruction of the nasolacrimal duct **(failure of canalization)** since birth, with
symptoms appearing in the first month of age.
[「since birth」 highlighted in yellow]

**Clinical picture:**

- Epiphora, crusting, discharge, recurrent conjunctivitis.
  [「Epiphora,」 highlighted in yellow]
- Reflux of mucopurulent material from lacrimal punctum when pressure is applied over lacrimal
  sac (positive regurge test).
  [「positive regurge test」 printed in dark red — sic, "regurge", not "regurgitation"]
- Lacrimal sac can be distended with mucus (mucocele) or become infected (lacrimal abscess).

**Treatment:**

- About 85% of cases resolve without surgical intervention before the first year. Therefore,
  surgery is not indicated **before the first year** of life.
- Massage over the lacrimal sac at the medial corner of the eyelid to increase pressure in the
  canal.
- Surgery: **Nasolacrimal duct probing** can be curative if persistent epiphora after the first
  year of life.
  [「Nasolacrimal duct probing」 printed bold in dark red]
- **Mucocele** needs probing upon diagnosis
- **Lacrimal abscess** needs incision and drainage.
