## printed p.253  (PDF p.255)  —  TRANSCRIBED IN FULL

[Banner heading, pale grey band, centred. The section number is set in a rounded display face,
the title in bold sans:]

# (11) Extraocular Malignancies

- Malignancies arising primarily from the eye;or its adnexa are rare.
  [sic — the semicolon and missing space, "eye;or", are as printed]
- Generally, the eye is more affected by metastases from cancers of other body organs or by the
  different forms of cancer therapy.
- In the United States, ocular and orbital metastases are found in 5% of cancer patients at
  autopsy.

**Ocular metastases from other primaries:**
[bold, underlined]

- ⇨ Most Commonly affect the choroid but occasionally can affect the optic nerve or the
  extraocular muscles.
  [「choroid」 underlined; "Most Commonly" capitalised as printed]
- ⇨ The original sites of the primaries are usually the **breast and the lung** in adults and
  **neuroblastoma** in children
  [「breast and the lung」 and 「neuroblastoma」 printed bold in dark red; 「in adults」 and
   「in children」 underlined]

**Systemic lymphoma:**
[bold, underlined]

- Affects the eye in 3% of patients
- infiltration of the orbit (causing proptosis or limitation of extraocular movement) or the
  conjunctiva is not uncommon in lymphomas.
- CNS lymphoma in the elderly can present as chronic, steroid-dependent,posierior/panuveitis
  **(Masquerade Syndrome).**
  [sic — "posierior" for "posterior", and the missing space after "steroid-dependent,".
   「(Masquerade Syndrome)」 printed bold in dark red]

**Childhood leukaemia:**
[bold, underlined]

- More than 75% of leukaemia patients are found to have ocular adnexal metastases at autopsy.
- Commonly leukemic patients present with intraretinal, or subconjunctival haemorrhages due to
  thrombocytopenia or anaemia.
  [sic — "leukaemia" in the heading and first bullet, "leukemic" in the second]

**Radiation of tumours near the eye may result in:**
[bold, underlined]

1. Cataract formation
2. Radiation retinopathy (similar to DR in appearance)
3. Optic neuropathy
4. Dry eye

**Chemotherapeutic agents can have ocular side effects:**
[bold, underlined]

- ⇨ Superficial keratitis (cytosine arabinoside)
- ⇨ Optic neuropathy (vincristine injections)

**Bone marrow transplantation to leukemic Patients can lead to:**
[bold, underlined; "Patients" capitalised as printed]

**Conjunctival mucosal damage and symblepharon** (partial or complete adhesion of the palpebral
conjunctiva to the bulbar conjunctiva) as the new bone marrow recognizes body tissues to be
foreign (Graft versus host disease).
[「Conjunctival mucosal damage and symblepharon」 printed bold in dark red]

[Photograph, bottom centre: an eye with the lower lid pulled down, showing a broad pale fibrous

[Second image, bottom right: a decorative graphic, not clinical — the words "Graft VS Host"

VERDICT — does chapter 19 pay the "Metastases from Extraocular Malignancies" row?
================================================================================

**YES. Chapter 17's cross-reference is a LIVE pointer, not a dead one.** Chapter 19's section
(11) is titled "Extraocular Malignancies" and is exactly what chapter 17 defers to. It is one
printed page, roughly 260 words, and it is the only place in the book that treats ocular
metastasis, ocular lymphoma or leukaemic ocular involvement.

What it delivers, item by item:

| Item asked for | Printed? | What the book says |
|---|---|---|
| Metastases to the eye — frequency | YES | 5 % of cancer patients at autopsy (US figure); and the general statement that the eye is more often affected by metastasis than by primary malignancy |
| Metastases — usual ocular site | YES | **Choroid** most commonly; occasionally optic nerve or extraocular muscles |
| Metastases — primary sites | YES | **Breast and lung in adults; neuroblastoma in children** |
| Metastases — presentation | **NO** — no symptoms, signs, fundus appearance or laterality are printed | — |
| Metastases — management | **NO** — nothing at all | — |
| Lymphoma | YES | Eye affected in 3 % of systemic lymphoma; orbital infiltration → proptosis or limited EOM; conjunctival infiltration "not uncommon"; **CNS lymphoma in the elderly as chronic steroid-dependent posterior/panuveitis = Masquerade Syndrome** |
| Lymphoma — management | **NO** | — |
| Leukaemia / leukaemic infiltrates | YES | **>75 % of leukaemia patients have ocular adnexal metastases at autopsy**; commonest presentation is intraretinal or subconjunctival haemorrhage **due to thrombocytopenia or anaemia** (i.e. the book attributes the ocular picture to the blood picture, not to direct infiltration) |
| Leukaemia — management | **NO** | — |
| Other tumours | YES, one — **Kaposi sarcoma of the eyelids** under AIDS on printed 252: multiple vascular skin malignancies, may involve palpebral or bulbar conjunctiva, **can be misdiagnosed as subconjunctival haemorrhage**. No management |
| Effects of cancer *therapy* on the eye | YES, and this is a third of the section — radiation (cataract, radiation retinopathy "similar to DR in appearance", optic neuropathy, dry eye), chemotherapy (superficial keratitis from cytosine arabinoside, optic neuropathy from vincristine), bone-marrow transplant (conjunctival mucosal damage and **symblepharon** as GVHD) |
| Retinoblastoma | **NO** — not mentioned in chapter 19 at all |
| Choroidal melanoma | **NO** — not mentioned in chapter 19 at all; it lives in chapter 17 |

**Absences worth naming:** the section prints **no management for any malignancy** — not for
metastasis, not for lymphoma, not for leukaemia, not for Kaposi sarcoma. It prints **no
work-up or imaging** for any of them. And it prints **no clinical presentation for ocular
metastasis** — only site and primary. A writer needing "how does a choroidal metastasis look
and what do you do about it" will not find it in this book.

**One structural surprise:** the section's own weight is inverted relative to its title. Three
of its six sub-headings are about the ocular toxicity of cancer *treatment* (radiation,
chemotherapy, bone marrow transplant) rather than about tumours. That third of the section is
material the register row did not ask for and nothing else in the book covers.
