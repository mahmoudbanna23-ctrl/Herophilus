Conjunctival Anatomy:

• The conjunctiva is a thin vascular mucous membrane that covers the surface of the
  eye globe except for the cornea (**Bulbar conjunctiva**) and the back surface of
  both upper and lower lids (**palpebral conjunctiva**).
• The **palpebral conjunctiva** is firmly adherent to the tarsal plates in the lids.
• The **bulbar conjunctiva** is loosely attached to the ocular surface (sclera and
  its overlying connective tissue [**Tenon's capsule**]) except at the limbus
  (corneoscleral junction) where it is firmly attached.
• The palpebral and the bulbar conjunctiva meet superiorly and inferiorly in the
  **superior fornix** and **inferior fornix** respectively.
• The potential dead space (leading into nowhere) between the bulbar and the
  palpebral conjunctiva when the lids are closed is called the **conjunctival sac**
  (Conjunctival cul-de-sac)
• The conjunctiva at the limbus hosts the **palisades of Vogt** which contain the
  stem cells that provide regenerative cells to the corneal epithelium. Damage to
  these structures results in corneal stem cell deficiency with corneal superficial
  vascularization and conjunctivalization of the corneal epithelium.

Conjunctival Histology:

• Transformation of the conjunctival non-keratinized epithelium (Composed of basal
  cuboidal cells and overlying polyhedral cells and Goblet cells) → into the
  non-Keratinized stratified squamous epithelium of the cornea takes place at the
  limbus.

  conjunctival epithelium into the keratinized stratified squamous epithelium of
  the skin.

[Banner:] Conjunctivitis

Definition:
• Inflammation of the conjunctiva is known as conjunctivitis and is characterized by
  dilation of the conjunctival vessels, resulting in hyperaemia and oedema of the
  conjunctiva, typically associated with discharge.
• The prevalence of conjunctivitis varies according to the underlying cause, which
  may be influenced by the patient's age, as well as the season of the year.

Aetiology:

  Non-infectious causes:
    a) Allergic:
       The **most frequent cause** of conjunctivitis, affecting **15-40%** of the
       population and is observed more frequently in the spring and summer.
    b) Toxic (chemical):
       - Some topical medications e.g., **Brimonidine**
       - Exposure to smoke, irradiation, chemicals, or fumes from the surrounding
         environment
    c) Secondary to other disorders:
       - Local: Dacryocystitis, dacryoadenitis, cellulitis.
       - systemic causes: Immune-mediated diseases and neoplastic processes

  Infectious causes:
       Bacterial, viral, chlamydial, fungal, parasitic
       (Viruses and bacteria are the most common infectious causes with viruses
       being the leading cause of infectious conjunctivitis.)

   • They have a fibrovascular core (in contrast to follicles) surrounded by

       1. Allergic conjunctivitis especially in vernal catarrh
       2. Prolonged contact lens wear

[Banner:] Bacterial Conjunctivitis

**1. Acute bacterial conjunctivitis:**

- It is caused by direct contact with infected secretions
- The most common organisms are **H. influenzae, S. pneumoniae, S. aureus and
  Moraxella catarrhalis.**
- Very few cases are caused by **N. gonorrhoea** while **N. meningitidis**
  conjunctivitis is rare and usually affects children.

Symptoms:
