## Q1 (marked page p.296)
The Meibomian glands secrete the outer oily layer of the tear film, which prevents tear evaporation. Goblet cells produce mucin, and lacrimal glands produce the aqueous layer.

## Q2 (marked page p.298)
Chronic dacryocystitis due to nasolacrimal duct obstruction is treated surgically by DCR, which creates a new drainage pathway between the lacrimal sac and nasal cavity.

## Q3 (marked page p.300)
The Schirmer test measures tear production using filter paper strips. It helps diagnose dry eye syndrome.

## Q4 (marked page p.302)
Chronic dacryocystitis due to nasolacrimal duct obstruction is treated surgically by DCR, which creates a new passage between the lacrimal sac and nasal cavity. Massage or probing is only for congenital or early cases.

## Q5 (marked page p.304)
Inner mucous layer of the tear film:
Secreted by the conjunctival goblet cells.
Helps maintain the adherence and stability of the tear film on the ocular surface, especially the cornea.

## Q6 (marked page p.306)
Treatment of nasolacrimal system abnormalities (congenital nasolacrimal duct obstruction):
A. About 85% of cases resolve without surgical intervention before the first year. Therefore, surgery is not indicated before the first year of life. Massage over the lacrimal sac at the medial corner of the eyelid to increase pressure in the canal.
B. Surgery: Nasolacrimal duct probing can be curative if persistent epiphora after the first year of life.
C. Mucocele needs probing upon diagnosis.
D. Lacrimal abscess needs incision and drainage.
