12. Which of the following causes jet black appearance of the pupillary
aperture?
A. Cataract
B. Pseudophakia
C, Posterior lens dislocation
D. All of the above
Pupil is jet-black in the newly born and starts to acquire a greyish tinge as the
person gets older due to the continuous addition of new lens fibers
throughout life causing light scattering; a process known as senile sclerosis. It
is to be noted that in the absence of the lens (surgically removed or posteriorly
dislocated), the pupil regains its jet-black color.
