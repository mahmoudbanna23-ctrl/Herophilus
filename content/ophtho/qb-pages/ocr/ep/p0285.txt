3. Epiphora
•Definition: Excessive tearing with overflow at the lid margin.
•Causes: Insufficient tear drainage or excessive tear production.
4. Dacryocystitis
•Definition: Acute or chronic infection of the lacrimal sac, usually secondary to nasolacrimal duct obstruction.
•Common organisms: Staphylococci or Streptococci.
•Clinical picture:
A. Acute dacryocystitis:
Symptoms: Pain, redness, swelling over lacrimal sac below medial canthus, epiphora.
Signs: Tender, tense swelling with erythema; may progress to abscess; pre-septal cellulitis possible.
•B. Chronic dacryocystitis:
Symptoms: Chronic epiphora, recurrent unilateral conjunctivitis, painless lacrimal sac swelling
(mucoce/e).
Signs: Pressure on lacrimal sac reflux of mucopurulent discharge through punctum (positive
regurge test).
tment:
Acute: Warm compresses, nasal decongestants, systemic and topical antibiotics, consider
dacryocystorhinostomy (DCR) after infection resolves.
Chronic : Dacryocystorhinostomy (DCR).
