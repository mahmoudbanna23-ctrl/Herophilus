Trachoma
•Causative agent: Chlamydia trachomatis serovars A, B, C
• Epidemiology:
Active trachoma: preschool children
Cicatricial trachoma: middle-aged adults (healing & fibrosis)
Clinical Features:
•Active trachoma: pannus formation (superior cornea)
•Cicatricial trachoma:
Scarring in upper tarsal plate
• Mild: linear or stellate
• Severe: Arlt's line can cause trichiasis, cicatricial entropion
Herbert's pits: residual from superior limbal follicles
Post-trachomatous concretions
Grading (WHO Simplified):
• — Follicular: 25 follicles (>0.5 mm) on superior tarsal plate
Management (SAFE Strategy):
•Surgery: for trichiasis
•Antibiotics: to affected and all family members
Drug of choice: Azithromycin 1 g single dose
• Alternatives: Erythromycin, Doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 1—2 weeks
•Facial cleanliness & Environmental improvement
