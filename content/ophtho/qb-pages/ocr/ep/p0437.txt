Allergic Conjunctivitis:
•Perennial: milder than seasonal
•Vernal (spring) catarrh:
Hot & dry climates (West Africa, Mediterranean, Middle East)
Age 5—25 yrs, avg 10—12 yrs
Spontaneous resolution in late 2nd decade
Symptoms: Morning misery, ropy mucous discharge
Signs:
• Tarsal giant papillae (cobblestone)
• Limbal Trantas dots (whitish degenerated eosinophils)
• Shield corneal ulcer (upper 1/3)
• Pseudogerontoxon (peripheral lipid deposition)
• Keratoconus (more common due to eye rubbing)
Recurrence: seasonal, but 23% perennial
•A topic keratoconjunctivitis (AKC):
Chronic, unremitting, perennial
Worse in winter, more severe than VKC
Associated with Staph. blepharitis
