Clinical Features
1. Ocular Manifestations
•Ptosis and diplopia are the presenting signs in N50% of patients.
•May mimic any ocular motor nerve palsy (3rd, 4th, or 6th) but pupils are never affected.
•Ptosis characteristics:
• Insidious onset, bilateral but asymmetric
• Worse at the end of the day
• Improves with rest
• Worsens on prolonged upgaze
• Positive ice test: improvement after 2 minutes of ice application diagnostic clue.
•Diplopia:
• May be horizontal or vertical
• Pattern can be bizarre or inconsistent (does not fit single nerve lesion).
