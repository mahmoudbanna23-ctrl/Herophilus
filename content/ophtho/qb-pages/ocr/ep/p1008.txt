Non-Arteritic Ischemic Optic Neuropathy (NAION)
Majority > SO years
Acute, painless, unilateral visual loss (Often noticed on
waking)
Usually one eye, Other rarely affected
Absent
Moderate—severe reduction
Swollen, pale optic disc
Local vascular risk factors (HTN, DM, hyperlipidemia,
nocturnal hypotension)
ESR and CRP normal (done to exclude arteritic cause)
NO definitive treatment; manage systemic vascular risk
factors and avoid nocturnal hypotension
May recover partially; risk of recurrence in same/other
eye
Arteritic Anterior Ischemic Optic Neuropathy
(A-AION)
Majority > 60 years
Sudden, severe unilateral visual loss with
periocular pain
Often bilateral, second eye involved within
days to weeks
Present — due to Giant cell Arteritis (GQ)
• Severe headache • Scalp tenderness
• Jaw claudication
•proximal myalgia (polymyalgia rheumatica)
• Malaise, weight loss
profound loss — only hand movements (HM)
or perception of light (PL)
Swollen, chalky-white optic disc
Signs of temporal arteritis (tender, thick
temporal artery, absent pulse)
ESR and CRP markedly elevated; Temporal
artery biopsy confirms GCA
Urgent high-dose systemic corticosteroids to
prevent blindness in the fellow eye
Medical emergency — vision loss often
permanent if untreated
