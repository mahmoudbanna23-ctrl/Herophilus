Blow-out Fracture
5.Definition & Mechanism
• Caused by sudden orbital pressure from an object larger than the orbital aperture (fist, tennis ball).
•Most commonly involves orbital floor; medial wall may also be fractured.
•Early: Proptosis from edema, emphysema, hematoma.
• Later: May develop enophthalmos.
6.Complications / Surgical Indications
• Muscle entrapment diplopia, limitation of ocular motility
• Large fractures globe displacement marked enophthalmos
• Surgery indicated for the above
7. Diagnosis
Periocular signs: Ecchymosis, edema, subcutaneous emphysema
Infraorbital nerve anesthesia: Lower lid, cheek, nose, upper lip, upper teeth/gums
Other signs: Diplopia, enophthalmos
Imaging: CT orbit (coronal) for floor fractures and muscle entrapment
8.Management
•Avoid nose blowing
Prophylactic antibiotics
•Surgical repair if indicated (muscle entrapment or large fractures)
