Age-Related Changes in the Eye and Its Adnexa
In ophthalmology, involutional (age-related) changes account for most clinic visits.
These changes can be classified into three major categories:
1. Extraocular Changes
•Ptosis:
The levator aponeurosis (the tendinous insertion of the levator palpebrae superioris muscle)
may partially detach from the superior tarsal plate, causing the upper eyelid to droop and
restrict the superior field of vision Involutional (senile or aponeurotic) ptosis.
•Entropion / Ectropion:
The lower eyelid suspensory ligaments lose firmness, causing the lid margin to rotate inward
(entropion) or outward (ectropion).
•Epiphora (chronic tearing):
Due to disorganization of eyelid—globe relationships dysfunction of the lacrimal pump
mechanism that normally propels tears toward the puncta.
•Dry Eye:
The conjunctiva loses accessory lacrimal glands and goblet cells over time reduced ar
production increased incidence of dry eye.
