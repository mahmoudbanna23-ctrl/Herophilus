2. Chambers of the Eye
The eyeball has three fluid-filled chambers:
Anterior Chamber (AO
a.
•Located between the cornea (anteriorly) and iris (posteriorly).
•Contains aqueous humour secreted by the ciliary body.
•Nourishes avascular structures (cornea, lens).
•Drains through trabecular meshwork Canal of Schlemm episcleral veins.
•Obstruction leads to intraocular pressure (IOP) glaucoma.
b. Posterior Chamber (PO
•Lies between the posterior surface of the iris and anterior surface of the lens.
•The ciliary sulcus (periphery) serves as an alternative site for posterior chamber
intraocular lens (IOL) fixation.
Vitreous Cavity
c.
•Filled with vitreous humour, a gel-like, avascular structure (98% water + collagen +
hyaluronic acid).
•Maintains globe shape and occupies two-thirds of the eye's volume.
