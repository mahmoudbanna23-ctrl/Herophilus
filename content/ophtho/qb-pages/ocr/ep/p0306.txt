6. A 6-month-old child presented with epiphora due to nasolacrimal duct
obstruction. Pressure over lacrimal sac caused reflux of mucus from lacrimal
punctum. What's the best management for that child?
A. Lacrimal sac massage only
B. Lacrimal sac massage and probing surgery if no response after I year of age
C. Immediate probing
D. Incision and drainage
Treatment of nasolacrimal system abnormalities (congenital nasolacrimal duct obstruction):
A. About 85% Of cases resolve without surgical intervention before the first year. Therefore, surgery is not
indicated before the first year Of life. Massage over the lacrimal sac at the medial corner Of the eyelid to
increase pressure in the canal.
B. Surgery: Nasolacrimal duct probing can be curative if persistent epiphora after the first year Of life.
C. Mucocele needs probing upon diagnosis.
D. Lacrimal abscess needs incision and drainage.
