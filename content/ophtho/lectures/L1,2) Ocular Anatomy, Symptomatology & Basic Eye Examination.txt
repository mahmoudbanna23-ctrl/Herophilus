Visual Acuity
It is a measurement of the smallest object a person can identify at a
          given distance from the eye compared to normal.



   Recorded as a ratio comparing patient’s performance with the VA of a normal person

               [6 meters (20 feet) considered equivalent to optical infinity]

1.   Ensure good illumination of the room and the chart.


2.   Position the patient (sitting or standing) at a distance of 6 meters from the chart.


3.   Test one eye one at a time
     (first without then with spectacles, if available)
     VA is reported for each eye (right eye first) with and without correction by spectaacles


4.   Ask the patient to cover one eye (left eye) with a plain occluder, card or palm of patient’s hand
     (They should not press on the eye)
5.   Ask the patient to designate the direction to which the strokes of the E point or the C letter’s
     opening or to read the letters on the Snellen chart


6.   Starting from the top of the chart (the 6/60 optotype) then shift downwards to the next line upon
     testing.


7.   The smallest line read is expressed as a fraction.
      e.g, 6/18.
     The upper number refers to the distance the chart is from the patient (6 meters)
     The lower number is the distance in meters at which a ‘normal’ eye is able to read that line of the
     chart.


8.   Repeat the procedure for the other eye.
✓   Record the VA for each eye in the patient‘s notes, stating whether it is with or without correction


✓   Example:
    ✓   V/A Without correction:
                                    Rt: 6/18    / Lt: 6/24
    ✓   V/A With correction:
                                    Rt: 6/6    / Lt: 6/12
                               Pinhole Test
If 6/6 is not achieved, repeat VA testing using a pinhole occluder
(with or without spectacles).
The use of the pinhole reduces the need to focus light entering the eye.
                     Pinhole Test
If VA improves, then the visual impairment is due to refractive error
(should be correctable with spectacles or a new prescription).


If VA worsens, another ocular pathology is causing the visual impairment
What If the patient cannot read the largest (top) letter at 6 meters?

1.   Reduce the distance between the patient and the chart, 1 meter at a time, until the top letter can be seen
     the VA will then be recorded as 5/60 or 4/60, etc.


2.   If the patient cannot read the largest (top) letter at one meter,
     Hold up 1 hand, extend 2 or more fingers, at varying distances of less than 1 meter and ask the patient to
     count the number of fingers.
     Record the distance at which counting fingers is done accurately (e.g., CF 50 cm).
What If the patient cannot read the largest (top) letter at 6 meters?

3.   If the patient cannot count fingers:
     Wave your hand and check if he/she can see this.
     This is recorded as hand movements (HM): VA = HM




4.   If the patient cannot detect hand motion:
     Shine a torch toward the eye and ask if they can see the light.
     If they can, record ‘perception of light’ (VA = PL).
     If they cannot, record ‘no perception of light’ (VA = NoPL).

Confrontation Visual Field Testing

                  Gross Detection of Significant visual field defects



The field of the examiner’s left eye is used as a reference in assessing the field of the
                          patient’s right eye and vice versa
     Confrontation Visual Field Testing
1.    Sit ONE meter in front of the patient with both eyes at same level.




2.    Ask the patient to cover the left eye with the palm of the left hand




3.    Close your right eye (preferably with the palm of the hand)
     Confrontation Visual Field Testing
4.    Ask the patient to:
      a)   Fixate on your left eye
      b)   Count your fingers placed midway
           between you and the patient in each of
           the 4 quadrants of the visual field.


5.    Repeat the test for the patient’s left eye

o   Results are recorded as:
