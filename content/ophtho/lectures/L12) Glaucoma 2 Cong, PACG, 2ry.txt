                  Acute Angle Closure Glaucoma

• Symptoms

  • Pain → ++++

  • Coloured haloes around light

  • Diminution of vision → rapid (over several hours)

  • Redness

  • Nausea / Vomiting (D.Dx: Acute Abdomen)
                   Acute Angle Closure Glaucoma
• Signs

   • Diminution of vision

   • IOP → ++++ (stony hard)

   • Corneal oedema
          • (Fundus examination, gonioscopy → usually not possible)

   • Ciliary injection

   • Mid-dilated vertically oval non-reactive pupil

   • Shallow anterior chamber / Iris bombe (forward bowing)

   • Eyelid oedema
                         Acute Angle Closure Glaucoma
• Treatment - Medical
    • Hospitalisation
    • IOP lowering Rx →
        • Systemic acetazolamide
        • Topical beta-blockers
        • Topical alpha agonists
        • Topical CAIs
        • Topical miotics (pilocarpine)

    • Hyperosmotic agents
        • IV mannitol
        • Oral hypertonic glycerol

    • Topical steroids

                                            Secondary Glaucoma
• Secondary Narrow angle glaucoma

    • A cause is seen for the gonioscopically narrow angle

• Mechanisms:

    • Anterior pulling

    • Posterior pushing

• Examples →
    • Neovascular Glaucoma

          • Neovascular membrane grows over the trabecular meshwork and contracts pulling the peripheral iris over the trabeculum

    • Iridocorneal endothelial Syndrome

          • A membrane grows over the trabeculum, iris and posterior cornea then contracts to pull the peripheral iris over the trabeculum

    • Intraocular tumours

          • Any space-occupying lesion in the posterior segment

    • Retinal Vein Occlusion

          • Congestion of the retinal veins causes forward rotation of the iris lens diaphragm

          Congenital / Developmental Glaucoma


• Epidemiology

  • In Western communities: rare (1 in 10,000 live births)

  • In communities with high consanguineous marriages: common

  • Affects males in 65% of cases, and is bilateral in 70% of cases
