Clinical Approach to the Cataract Patient
Structured examination framework for the OSCEs and clinical rounds


    1    History                                                       2   Visual Function Assessment

     •     Chief complaint: type and duration of visual loss           •    BCVA — both distance and near

     •     Glare, halos, monocular diplopia, reading difficulties      •    Pupils: RAPD (afferent defect = another pathology)

     •     Systemic: DM, steroids, autoimmune disease                  •    Visual fields: confrontation

     •     Ocular: prior uveitis, trauma, glaucoma drops               •    Light projection (all 4 quadrants) — dense cataract

     •     Medications: steroids, alpha-blockers (IFIS), amiodarone    •    Color discrimination — red vs green torch test



    3    Slit-Lamp Examination                                         4   Fundal Examination

     •     Lids, conjunctiva, cornea (Fuchs' endothelial dystrophy?)   •    Dilated fundus exam with +90D or indirect

     •     Anterior chamber: depth, cells, flare, synechiae            •    Macula: AMD, diabetic maculopathy, ERM

     •     Lens: morphology (nuclear/cortical/PSC), maturity           •    Disc: glaucomatous cupping?

     •     Pupil dilation essential — grade opacity properly           •    Periphery: retinal breaks, lattice degeneration

     •     Vitreous: any pigment? Schafer sign → retinal tear?         •    If not visible → B-scan ultrasound mandatory

Case 1 — Clinical Problem Solving
Apply your knowledge


   CASE A


      A 68-year-old man presents with a 6-month history of difficulty reading the newspaper and problems with near vision. He had been using reading glasses for
      10 years but now cannot read even with them. He also reports severe glare when driving at night. On examination: BCVA 6/9 dis tance, N10 near (with
      reading correction). Slit-lamp shows a granular brownish-yellow opacity just posterior to the lens capsule. Red reflex: dark plaque visible in the posterior pole.
      No RAPD. Fundus examination: normal macula.




    Questions:                                                                              Key Answers:

      1. What is the most likely diagnosis?                                                  1. PSC — Posterior SubCapsular Cataract

                                                                                             2. PSC is near nodal point → near tasks and miosis (reading) maximise the
      2. Why is near vision MORE affected than distance?
                                                                                             effect of the opacity

      3. What is the red reflex finding and what does it indicate?                           3. Dark plaque posteriorly on retroillumination — classic PSC pattern


      4. What investigations would you order?                                                4. Biometry, B-scan if needed; check for systemic steroids / DM


      5. What are the indications for surgery in this case?                                  5. Visual disability affecting daily life — functional indication met
