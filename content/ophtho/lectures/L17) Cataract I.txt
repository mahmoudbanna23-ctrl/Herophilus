• Clinical picture

    • Symptoms (“Gradual progressive painless diminution of vision”)

         • Diminution of vision (“foggy vision”)

         • Glare (“defective vision with bright illumination beside visual

           targets”)

         • Defective colour vision (colour desaturation / different

           perceptions)

         • Defective vision in different illumination conditions (dark / light)

         • in children

              • Poor visual attention

              • Squint (unilateral), nystagmus (bilateral)
                                                  Cataract
• Clinical picture

   • Signs

       • Decreased visual acuity (Chart)

             • Incipient, immature cortical cataract → 6/6 → HM

             • Mature cataract → HM

             • Never PL or No PL due to cataract or any media opacity
                                                                         Normal-Diffuse Illumination


       • Pupil colour change (grey, white, brown) (penlight)

       • Lens opacity (absent 3rd Purkinje image, slit lamp)

       • In children → leukocoria (“white pupil”) (D.Dx of leukocoria)

       • Tests → intact 2-point discrimination, normal pupil reaction

• Epidemiology

  • Senile cataract is the most common cause of reversible blindness in the world

  • Congenital / developmental cataract is a leading cause of childhood blindness and

    lifelong disability

  • Senile cataract starts from the 5th decade onwards, with no gender or laterality

    predilection

  • Chronic exposure to excessive ultraviolet light is an implicated predisposing factor

• Optical rehabilitation of aphakia (glasses, contact lenses, IOLs)
    • Glasses →
         • Binocular aphakia → suitable

         • Monocular aphakia → least suitable (image magnification ~ 30% → anisokonia → binocular diplopia)

         • Binocular aphakia → suitable

         • Monocular aphakia → suitable (image magnification ~ 7 – 10% → minimal anisokonia → no binocular diplopia)

• Take Home Message

   • The natural crystalline lens is responsible for one third of the dioptric power of the eye

   • Opacification of the natural crystalline lens may be age-related or secondary to local ocular or systemic

     conditions

   • Cataract is the most common cause of gradual progressive painless diminution of vision

   • Media opacities, including cataracts never cause vision worse than Hand Motion

   • Cataract maybe observed, corrected by glasses, or surgically removed and the patient maybe fitted by high

     plus lenses, contact lenses or implanted an intraocular lens
