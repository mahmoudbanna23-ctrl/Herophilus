⚠️ CONVENTIONS ADOPTED HERE.
  • The deck never states which eye is right and which is left in any
    photograph, so laterality is NEVER asserted below. Every deviation is
    reported as "the eye on the LEFT/RIGHT of the photograph", and the
    direction is given as IN (nasal, toward the nose) or OUT (temporal, away
    from the nose), which is recoverable from the picture and does not need
    the patient's side.
  • Where the deviation is read from the width of the white scleral crescent,
    that is said — a crescent of sclera on the TEMPORAL side means the eye is
    turned IN; on the NASAL side, turned OUT. That is this file's way of
    stating the evidence, not a caption from the slide.
  • Slides 4, 5, 6, 10, 11, 12, 13, 16, 18, 22, 23, 25, 27 and 30 were re-read
    from their enlarged single-slide renders because a four-up cell could not
    settle which iris sat where. Headings and bullets all read cleanly at

SLIDE 4 — Ocular Motility
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Heading, then a labelled direction DIAGRAM at the top right and a MONTAGE of
nine clinical photographs filling the rest of the slide. No body text.

DIAGRAM (top right, on a white panel) — a schematic face: two eyes with green
irides drawn either side of an outlined nose, and EIGHT ARROWS radiating from
between them. The four diagonal arrows are RED, the four cardinal arrows are
BLUE, and each carries a label:

     dextro-          direct elevation          laevo-
     version                                    elevation      (red, top row)
     ← dextro-version                laevo-version →           (blue, sides)
     dextro-          direct depression         laevo-
     depression                                 depression     (red, bottom)

⚠️ THE TOP-LEFT LABEL IS A DEFECT IN THE FIGURE AND IS TRANSCRIBED AS PRINTED.
It reads "dextro-version", the identical word already used for the horizontal
blue arrow immediately below it, where its mirror on the right reads
"laevo-elevation". The figure therefore prints "dextro-version" TWICE and
never prints "dextro-elevation" at all. Not corrected.

Because the diagram is drawn as a face seen from the front, DEXTRO is on the
OBSERVER'S LEFT — i.e. the patient's right. That is what fixes the reading of
the photographs below.

MONTAGE — nine photographs of one boy, arranged as a 3 × 3 grid with his full
face in the centre:

  CENTRE — full face, primary position: both eyes straight ahead, corneal
    reflections central and symmetrical.

  LEFT COLUMN (three wide eye crops). In ALL THREE, both pupils are displaced
  toward the LEFT of the photograph and the white sclera is exposed on the
  temporal side of the right-of-photograph eye — i.e. both eyes are turned to
  the patient's RIGHT: DEXTRO.
    • top    — both eyes right and UP      (dextro-elevation)
    • middle — both eyes right, level      (dextroversion)
    • bottom — both eyes right and DOWN    (dextro-depression); the lids are
      partly lowered over the down-turned eyes.

  RIGHT COLUMN (three wide eye crops). In ALL THREE, both pupils are displaced
  toward the RIGHT of the photograph — both eyes turned to the patient's LEFT:
  LAEVO.
    • top    — both eyes left and UP       (laevo-elevation)
    • middle — both eyes left, level       (laevoversion)
    • bottom — both eyes left and DOWN     (laevo-depression)

  TOP CENTRE — a single-eye crop, the eye looking straight UP: the iris is
    displaced upward under the upper lid and a wide band of white sclera is
    exposed BELOW it (direct elevation).

  BOTTOM CENTRE — a single-eye crop, the eye looking straight DOWN: the iris is
    displaced downward, the upper lid follows it, and white sclera is exposed
    ABOVE (direct depression).

⚠️ NO MUSCLE IS NAMED ANYWHERE ON THIS SLIDE. The nine positions are given as
directions of gaze only; which muscle acts in which position is not printed.

SLIDE 6 — [no heading printed; the three tropias]
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
One white-panelled DIAGRAM filling the slide: three drawn pairs of eyes,
stacked, each with its name printed to its left. These three words are the
only text on the slide.

  Esotropia
    → the eye on the LEFT of the picture is turned IN, toward the nose: its
      iris sits against the inner (nasal) corner and a wide white crescent of
      sclera is exposed on the TEMPORAL side. The eye on the right is
      straight, iris central.

  Exotropia
    → the eye on the LEFT of the picture is turned OUT, away from the nose:
      its iris sits against the outer (temporal) corner and the wide white
      crescent is on the NASAL side. The eye on the right is straight.

  Hypertropia
    → the eye on the LEFT of the picture is turned UP: its iris rides high
      under the upper lid and white sclera is exposed BELOW it. The eye on the
      right is straight, at the normal level.

So all three are drawn as a deviation of the SAME eye, changing only its
direction — in, out, up. Hypotropia is not drawn and not named.

SLIDE 15 — Apparent Squint (Nasal bridge and epicanthus)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Heading in yellow with the sub-heading in cyan beneath it:

  Apparent Squint
  (Nasal bridge and epicanthus)

Then TWO clinical PHOTOGRAPHS side by side, no captions.

PHOTOGRAPH 1 (left) — a girl with a BROAD, FLAT, LOW NASAL BRIDGE. In BOTH
eyes a crescent of white sclera is exposed on the NASAL side, between the iris
and the inner canthus, so the eyes give the impression of being turned
outward. Her corneal light reflexes are, however, symmetrical.
  = the broad-bridge / wide-set appearance: an apparent DIVERGENT squint.

PHOTOGRAPH 2 (right) — a toddler with a broad flat bridge and full EPICANTHAL
FOLDS. Here the folds cover the nasal sclera of BOTH eyes almost completely,
so that white sclera is visible only on the TEMPORAL side of each eye and the
eyes give the impression of being turned inward.
  = the epicanthus appearance: an apparent CONVERGENT squint.

The two photographs therefore print the two opposite illusions the sub-heading
names, and the slide leaves that unsaid.

SLIDE 16 — [no heading printed; the epicanthus demonstration]
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Two clinical PHOTOGRAPHS of THE SAME CHILD side by side, no text at all. This
is the deck's proof that an apparent squint is apparent, and it is delivered
entirely by a pair of pictures.

PHOTOGRAPH 1 (left) — the child at rest. Prominent EPICANTHAL FOLDS run down
from the upper lids across the inner canthi, hiding the nasal sclera of both

shown and never named; the words "pseudo-esotropia" and "pseudostrabismus"

TESTS THAT ARE NAMED NOWHERE
 1. HIRSCHBERG IS NOT NAMED. The corneal light reflex is visible and
    diagnostic in the photographs on slides 5, 11 and 18, and the displaced
    reflex of the squinting eye on slide 11 is the clearest single sign in the
    deck — but the word "Hirschberg", the term "corneal reflection test" and
    the millimetre-to-degree conversion appear on no slide.
 2. KRIMSKY IS NOT NAMED, anywhere.
 3. "PRISM COVER TEST" IS NOT NAMED, although slide 10 photographs it being
    performed. Nor is "alternate cover test": the deck's three cover-test
    slides (11–13) show only deviations, and slide 18's "Cover Uncover test"
    shows two straight eyes. THE STEPS OF NO COVER TEST ARE WRITTEN ANYWHERE —
    not what to cover, not what to watch, not what a movement means.
 4. THE MADDOX ROD IS NOT MENTIONED, and neither is the Maddox wing.
 5. WORTH'S FOUR-DOT TEST IS NOT MENTIONED. Nor is the Bagolini striated
    glass, the red glass test, or any stereopsis test (Titmus, TNO, Lang).
 6. THE SYNOPTOPHORE IS PHOTOGRAPHED (slide 33) AND NEVER NAMED. No knob, no
    scale and no slide of the instrument is labelled.
 7. NO UNIT OF DEVIATION IS EVER PRINTED. The prism on slide 8 is engraved
    "50" and that is the only number; "prism dioptre", the symbol Δ, and
    degrees of deviation appear nowhere. "Large angle" (slide 28) is the
    deck's only description of size.
 8. COVER TESTING AT NEAR VERSUS DISTANCE is not mentioned — no fixation
    distance is given for any test.

ANATOMY AND MECHANICS
 9. THE "EOM" OF THE TITLE IS ONE SLIDE. Slide 2 is an anatomical
    illustration and there is NO table of the six muscles, no origin, no
    insertion, no action, no innervation list and no field of action. The
    medial rectus, inferior rectus and inferior oblique are not labelled even
    on that illustration.
10. NO INDIVIDUAL DUCTION IS NAMED. Slide 3 prints "Ductions", "Versions" and
    "Vergences" and stops; adduction, abduction, elevation, depression,
    intorsion and extorsion appear on no slide, and convergence and divergence
    are never given as the two vergences.
11. NO MUSCLE IS LINKED TO ANY POSITION OF GAZE. Slide 4's nine positions are
    labelled by DIRECTION only; which muscle is tested in which position is
    not printed, so the diagnostic purpose of the nine positions is absent.
12. YOKE MUSCLES, HERING'S LAW AND SHERRINGTON'S LAW are not mentioned.

CLASSIFICATION AND CONDITIONS
13. PARALYTIC SQUINT IS LISTED ON SLIDE 14 AND NEVER RETURNED TO. Nothing in
    this deck describes an incomitant deviation, a nerve palsy, an abnormal
    head posture, diplopia charting or the Hess chart. (That is the deck's
    design — L30 is the paralytic lecture — but a reader searching this file
    for a third nerve palsy will not find one.)
14. NO CAUSE OF A SENSORY DEVIATION IS NAMED. "Sensory exotropia" and
    "Sensory esotropia" are listed (24, 27) and the deck never names cataract,
    corneal opacity, retinoblastoma, optic atrophy or any other cause, and
    never mentions leukocoria.
15. DUANE'S RETRACTION SYNDROME, BROWN'S SYNDROME, A AND V PATTERNS,

16. ABNORMAL RETINAL CORRESPONDENCE AND ECCENTRIC FIXATION are not mentioned,
    even though slides 22 and 23 are given over to sensory adaptations —
    suppression and amblyopia are the only two the deck lists.
17. HYPOTROPIA IS NEVER NAMED. Slide 6 draws esotropia, exotropia and
    hypertropia and stops; no vertical deviation appears anywhere else.
18. "ALTERNATING" versus "UNILATERAL" squint is never used as a term, although
