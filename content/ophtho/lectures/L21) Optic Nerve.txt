SLIDE 2 — Normal Optic Disc
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Heading only. One large FUNDUS PHOTOGRAPH fills the slide — the baseline
against which every later photograph is meant to be read.

FUNDUS PHOTOGRAPH — a close, tall crop centred on the disc.
  • Colour  — the disc is a warm orange-yellow with a distinctly PALER, almost
    cream-white central area: the physiological cup.
  • Margins — CRISP AND SHARP on all sides; the disc edge is a clean line
    against the orange-red retina, with no blurring of the surrounding nerve
    fibre layer.
  • Cup     — small-to-moderate, central, occupying well under half the disc,
    with a healthy pink-orange neuroretinal rim all the way round.
  • Vessels — the central retinal artery and vein emerge together from the cup
    and branch immediately into superior and inferior arcades; the arteries are
    narrower and brighter red than the veins, and the vessels are seen to DIP
    into the cup and climb out at its edge.
  • No haemorrhages, no exudates, no peripapillary pigment change.
  • Surrounding retina — uniform orange-red with the choroidal pattern faintly
    visible.

SLIDE 3 — Manifestatins of Optic Nerve Dysfunction
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
⚠️ The heading is printed exactly as "Manifestatins of Optic Nerve
Dysfunction" — the lecturer's own misspelling of "Manifestations". Kept.

The five items are printed each with its own icon rather than a bullet, and
the icons carry meaning:

  ↓ (large red DOWN ARROW)   Visual acuity
  ↓ (large red DOWN ARROW)   Colour vision
  ↓ (large red DOWN ARROW)   Contrast sensitivity
  ★ (red STAR)               Field defect
  ★ (red STAR)               RAPD

So the first three are marked as things that are REDUCED, and the last two are
starred — the deck's way of flagging the two signs that localise the lesion to
the optic nerve. No text explains the icons.

(This is the only typed bullet list in the entire deck.)

⚠️ THIS SLIDE IS A PHOTOGRAPH OF A HANDWRITTEN SHEET OF PAPER, in blue ink on
white, filling the whole slide. It maps FIVE DISEASES to FIVE FIELD-DEFECT
PATTERNS, each name written above a small hand-drawn sketch of the defect. It

Transcribed exactly as written, with the drawing under each described:

  Papilledema
  (enlarged blind spot)
    → sketch: a single small hatched CIRCLE, drawn to one side of centre —
      the blind spot, enlarged. A tiny dash is drawn to its left.

  Acute optic neuritis
  (central scotoma)
    → sketch: a hatched OVAL sitting at the centre, with a second, smaller
      hatched circle beside it to the right (the blind spot, normal size, for
      comparison).

  Chronic RB neuritis
  (centrocecal scotoma)
    → sketch: a long hatched OVAL running horizontally and JOINING a smaller
      hatched circle at its right end — i.e. the central scotoma and the blind
      spot fused into one continuous defect.
      ("RB" = retrobulbar; the deck never expands the abbreviation.)

  AION
  (ALtitudinal defect)
    → sketch: a broad hatched HALF-DISC occupying the LOWER half of the field,
      its upper border a straight horizontal line — an inferior altitudinal
      defect, the horizontal cut-off drawn deliberately flat.
      (The capital L in "ALtitudinal" is the lecturer's own.)

  Compression
  (Sector, wedge defects)
    → sketch: a small hatched circle (the blind spot) with a hatched WEDGE
      widening outwards from it, drawn as a triangle running up and to the
      right.

method, and no indication of which eye.

SLIDE 10 — Glaucoma
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Heading only, then ONE large FUNDUS PHOTOGRAPH.

FUNDUS PHOTOGRAPH
  • Colour  — the disc is dominated by a huge, brilliantly WHITE, deeply

SLIDE 14 — Optic Atrophy
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Heading, then FOUR fundus photographs in a 2 × 2 block, each with its TYPE
captioned in black inside the photograph. This one slide is the whole
classification, and the four captions are the only words on it:

PHOTOGRAPH 1 (top left) — caption:  Primary
  • Colour  — a CHALKY WHITE disc, dead white against a red fundus.
  • Margins — CLEARLY DEFINED AND SHARP, a clean edge all round; this is what
    separates primary from secondary.
  • Cup     — the pale disc is flat, with the cup not distinguishable from the
    surrounding pallor.
  • Vessels — normal in calibre, emerging from the centre.
  • Surrounding retina — normal red-orange, no peripapillary change.

PHOTOGRAPH 2 (top right) — caption:  Secondary
  • Colour  — a DIRTY GREY-WHITE disc, not chalky; the whole picture has a
    blue-grey cast.
  • Margins — INDISTINCT AND BLURRED, the disc edge fading into a fuzzy halo
    of surrounding tissue rather than ending in a line.
  • Cup     — filled in and not identifiable.
  • Vessels — pale pink-violet, drawn over and around the disc, appearing
    sheathed.
  • Surrounding retina — mottled and hazy immediately around the disc — the
    residue of the swelling that preceded the atrophy.

PHOTOGRAPH 3 (bottom left) — caption:  Consecutive
  • Colour  — a WAXY YELLOW disc, distinctly yellow rather than white.
  • Margins — reasonably defined.
  • Vessels — MARKEDLY ATTENUATED: the arterioles are thin, thread-like and
    fewer than normal, which is the striking feature of this photograph.
  • Surrounding retina — a pale, washed-out yellow-orange, quite unlike the
    healthy red of photograph 1 — the retinal disease that caused the atrophy
    is visible in the background.

PHOTOGRAPH 4 (bottom right) — caption:  Glaucomatous
  • Colour  — a bright white CUP with an orange-red rim around it.
  • Cup     — deep and large, occupying most of the disc, reaching the margin
    inferiorly.
  • Vessels — bend sharply at the cup edge.
  • Margins — sharp; the pallor is CONFINED TO THE CUP, not spread across the
    whole disc, which is what distinguishes glaucomatous atrophy from the
    other three in this block.

⚠️ The four type names are captions on photographs. NO DEFINITION, no cause
list and no distinguishing rule is written anywhere on this slide or in the
deck; the differences above are what the four pictures show.

re-reads of slides 4, 8, 9 and 19. Slides 3, 4 and 8 hold every word of prose
in the deck, so a negative here is easy to prove: if a thing is not in those
three slides, in one of the six typed headings, or burned into a photograph as

TREATMENT AND INVESTIGATION
 1. NO TREATMENT OF ANY KIND IS MENTIONED. Not one slide names a drug, a dose,
    a route or a duration. There is no steroid, no intravenous
    methylprednisolone, no acetazolamide, no anti-glaucoma medication and no
    surgery. The deck is entirely diagnostic.
 2. THE ONTT (Optic Neuritis Treatment Trial) IS NOT MENTIONED, and no trial,
    guideline, textbook or reference of any kind is cited anywhere. There is
