SLIDE 11 — Etiology of uveitis
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Etiology of uveitis
 • Idiopathic
 • Infectious
    • Bacterial: e.g.; TB, syphilis, leprosy, ….
    • Viral: e.g.; HSV, HZV, CMV,….
    • Fungal: e.g.; candidiasis,…
    • Parasitic: e.g.; toxoplasmosis,…
 • Non-infectious
    • Immune mediated: e.g.; Bechet's disease, Sarcoidosis, Multiple sclerosis,
      ankylosing spondylitis,…. etc.
    • Masquerade syndrome: malignancies such as leukemia, and lymphoma
    • Trauma
    • Ocular surgery
    • Drug induced

SLIDE 12 — Uveitis: anatomical classification
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Uveitis
Anatomically, uveitis can be classified into:

1. Anterior uveitis (iritis / iridocyclitis)

2. Intermediate uveitis:
   Inflammation predominantly involving the pars plana, vitreous, and the
   peripheral retina.

3. Posterior uveitis: (Choroiditis – Chorioretinitis)
   Inflammation of the choroid with or without inflammation of the retina.

4. Panuveitis
   Simultaneous inflammation of all three parts of the uveal tract

SLIDE 20 — Anterior uveitis: Treatment
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Anterior uveitis
Treatment include:
 ▪ Steroids (Topical ± periocular ± systemic)
 ▪ Cycloplegic-mydriatic topical medications:
    ➤ to decrease photophobia and pain from iritis and ciliary spasm
    ➤ To prevent and break recently formed posterior synechiae
 ▪ Antiglaucoma medications in case of increased IOP
 ▪ Control of the causative condition (after doing a systemic work up
   especially in recurrent cases to reach a definitive diagnosis of the cause of
   anterior uveitis)
 ▪ Treatment of secondary ocular complications

Blue sclera

SLIDE 30 — Episcleritis
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Episcleritis
 • Inflammation of the episcleral tissues
 • Relatively common, benign and self-limited cause of red eye
 • Usually unilateral
 • Common in female than male (3:1)
 • Presented with:
    ➤ Red eye
    ➤ Mild pain and/or discomfort
    ➤ Mild localised ocular tenderness
    ➤ No discharge (if present: minimal & watery)
