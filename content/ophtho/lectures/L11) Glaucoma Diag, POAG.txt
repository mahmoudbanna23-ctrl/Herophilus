• What is Glaucoma? (Definition)
                                          Glaucoma

• Definition

   • A triad of

      • Elevated intraocular pressure (IOP)

      • Optic nerve affection (cupping)

      • Visual field defects
Open Angle Glaucoma
• Definition
    • Glaucoma is a specific type of optic neuropathy in which intraocular pressure (IOP) is a significant risk factor.

• Comment
    • Optic neuropathy → optic nerve disease

    • Specific type → with special characteristics

    • Exceptions →
         • eyes with optic neuropathy and normal IOP (normal tension glaucoma)

         • eyes with normal optic nerves and elevated IOP (ocular hypertension)

• Mismatch between aqueous humour production and aqueous humour drainage

   • Aqueous humour drainage → trabecular meshwork → Schlemm’s canal→ collector channels

    • Pathology
        • Increased resistance to Aqueous humour drainage → trabecular meshwork →

    • Predisposing factors
        • Age
            • Older age (> 5th decade)
        • Gender
            • More common in females
        • Ethnicity
            • More common and more severe in dark races
        • Family history
            • 10-fold increased risk in first degree relatives of a glaucoma patient
        • Refractive state
            • More common in myopia

• How does glaucoma affect the eye? (Pathology)
Open Angle Glaucoma
    • Optic neuropathy

    • Neural rim thinning → structural changes
        • Focal → polar notch → superior, inferior

        • Circumferential → generalized thinning of the neural rim

    • Clinically → enlargement of cup/disc ratio > 0.3

    • Comment

    • Additional features →
        • Splinter haemorrhage

        • Total cupping with nasal shift of blood vessels
Open Angle Glaucoma
    • Neural rim thinning → functional changes
       • Disturbance of visual function →
          • Visual field changes →

              •   Paracentral scotomata in Bjerrum’s area (10 – 20 degrees of central field)
              •   Arcuate scotomata (superior, inferior)
              •   Nasal step
              •   Ring scotoma (double arcuate scotoma)
              •   Constriction of visual field (→ tubular field)
              •   Temporal island of vision
          • Visual acuity changes →
              • Diminution of central vision in end stage disease
Open Angle Glaucoma
    • Secondary open angle glaucoma
    • A cause is seen for the increased resistance of aqueous humour outflow
        • Examples →
    • Pseudoexfoliation syndrome
        • Pseudoexfoliative material (fibrillar basement membrane material) is
          deposited in the trabecular meshwork and all through the anterior segment)
    • Pigment dispersion syndrome
        • Pigment from the iris is chaffed out and deposited in the trabecular meshwork
          and all through the anterior segment
    • Steroid induced
        • Prolonged intake of potent steroids in a predisposed individual → decreased
          permeability of the trabecular meshwork to aqueous humour
    • Uveitic
        • Deposition of inflammatory cells and plasmoid (viscid) aqueous humour →
          decreased permeability of the trabecular meshwork to aqueous humour

• How is the patient with glaucoma diagnosed? (Clinical picture)
Open Angle Glaucoma
    • Presentation
        • Most commonly → asymptomatic

        • Less commonly → headache (early morning), frequent change of reading glasses

        • Advanced disease → peripheral visual field loss, diminution of vision

    • Examination
        • Optic neuropathy

        • Elevated IOP

        • Features of secondary glaucoma

• What is the treatment of glaucoma? (Treatment)
Open Angle Glaucoma
    • Treatment
       • Glaucoma is a potentially blinding disease, referral to an ophthalmologist is a
         must
       • Aim → preserve visual function to beyond the patient life expectancy at an
         affordable cost with the least side effects


    • Protocol
       • Medical treatment →
            • Decreasing aqueous humour production → beta-blockers, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, alpha
              agonists
            • Increasing aqueous humour drainage → prostaglandin analogues, miotics, alpha agonists
       • Laser treatment
            • Laser trabeculoplasty (Argon, Selective)
       • Surgery
            • Creation of a fistula between the anterior chamber and the subconjunctival space
              (trabeculectomy)
