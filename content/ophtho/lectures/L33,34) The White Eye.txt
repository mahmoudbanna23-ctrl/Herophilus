                              Take Home Message
• The white eye involves lesion of the cornea, pupillary membrane, the lens or the posterior segment

• Symptoms include variable degrees of diminution of vision, disfigurement and cause-related

• Treatment depends on the cause

• Corneal scars may follow healed inflammations or hydrops

• Pupillary membranes may be congenital or acquired (inflammatory)

• Cataracts may be congenital or acquired

• White pupil in a child may be a sign of retinoblastoma

The slide is a STACK OF FIVE OVERLAPPING CARDS (blue, yellow, red,
green, orange) sitting on a photo of a child's leukocoric eye. Only
the topmost card (orange, "Retinal Dysplasia") is visible in a
static render — the other four are physically covered and CANNOT be
read visually. They were instead recovered exactly by
`pdftotext -raw`, which preserves content-stream (draw) order and
so separates the cards cleanly. This is an extraction, not a
reconstruction. Draw order below = back card to front card.

1. Retinopathy of Prematurity
   Proliferative retinopathy in preterm and low birth weight
   infants with subsequent epiretinal fibrosis and tractional
   retinal detachment

2. Coats disease
   Peripheral retinal telangiectasia with extensive subretinal
   lipid exudation and exudative retinal detachment

3. Persistent Fetal Vasculature
   Persistence of the hyaloid system with variable extent
   posteriorly, lenticularly, or anteriorly

4. Toxoplasma, Toxocara
   Parasitic granulomas in the retina / choroid

5. Retinal Dysplasia
   Congenital malformation of the retina
   [the only card visible in the render — confirms the stack order]

CAUTION for anyone re-reading the interleaved block above: it
carries BOTH "piretinal" and "subretinal" fragments shuffled
together. The correct split is ROP = "epiretinal fibrosis",
Coats = "subretinal lipid exudation". A reconstruction from the
interleaved text alone can easily mis-assign these two.
