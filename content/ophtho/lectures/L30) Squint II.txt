SLIDE 4 — Limitation of ocular motility:
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Heading only — the first of the seven signs. SIX photographs in a block of
3 rows × 2 columns, of DIFFERENT patients, none captioned and none matched to
a muscle or a nerve.

ROW 1, LEFT — a fair child, eyes only. Both eyes are directed toward the RIGHT
  of the photograph. The eye on the RIGHT of the photograph reaches the outer
  canthus fully (a wide white crescent on its nasal side); the eye on the LEFT
  of the photograph lags — its iris sits only just past the midline instead of
  moving fully toward the nose. = limited ADDUCTION of the left-of-photograph
  eye.

ROW 1, RIGHT — a darker child, eyes only. Both eyes are directed toward the
  LEFT of the photograph and both reach well over; the eye on the LEFT of the
  photograph is fully abducted with a wide white crescent nasally, the eye on
  the right adducted. The two eyes are grossly dissociated in level: the eye
  on the RIGHT of the photograph sits noticeably higher in its fissure.

ROW 2, LEFT — an adult male in warm light, eyes only. Gaze is toward the RIGHT
  of the photograph: the eye on the LEFT of the photograph is fully adducted,
  its iris hard against the inner canthus with a broad white crescent on its
  temporal side, while the eye on the RIGHT of the photograph HAS NOT MOVED —
  its iris is still near the middle of the fissure. = limited ABDUCTION of the
  right-of-photograph eye, the clearest single frame on the slide.

ROW 2, RIGHT — a child, eyes only, with a bruised, swollen, purplish upper lid
  on the LEFT of the photograph. That eye's iris sits high and central; the eye
  on the RIGHT of the photograph is partly covered by its own lid and its iris
  is displaced downward and outward.

ROW 3, LEFT and ROW 3, RIGHT — two frames of the same patient with the
  EXAMINER'S FINGERS holding BOTH upper lids up out of the way, so that eyes
  which could not otherwise be seen can be photographed.
  • Row 3 left: the eye on the LEFT of the photograph is turned OUT and sits
    low; the eye on the RIGHT of the photograph is turned OUT and sits HIGH,
    with white sclera exposed below it.
  • Row 3 right: the eye on the LEFT of the photograph is turned IN and low;
    the eye on the RIGHT of the photograph is high and out, a wide band of
    white sclera below the iris.
  = a gross vertical as well as horizontal dissociation, the two eyes pointing
  in quite different directions in both frames.

SLIDE 5 — Ocular deviation (2ry angle > 1ry angle):
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The heading states the rule; THREE photographs demonstrate it, uncaptioned.

PHOTOGRAPH 1 (left) — a woman's face, eyes and nose. The eye on the LEFT of
the photograph is normally open, iris central. The eye on the RIGHT of the
photograph has a DROOPING upper lid covering part of the cornea, and its iris
is displaced OUT, toward the outer canthus, with white sclera exposed on the
nasal side. = ptosis with an outward deviation on the same side.

PHOTOGRAPHS 2 and 3 (right, stacked) — THE SAME young man twice, and this pair
is the whole point of the slide:
  • UPPER frame — the eye on the LEFT of the photograph is fixing, iris
    central; the eye on the RIGHT of the photograph is deviated by a SMALL
    amount, its iris just off centre toward the nose.
        = the PRIMARY angle: the sound eye fixes, the paretic eye deviates a
          little.
  • LOWER frame — the fixation has been swapped. Now the eye on the RIGHT of
    the photograph is fixing, iris central; and the eye on the LEFT of the
    photograph is deviated by a MUCH LARGER amount, its iris pulled well
    toward the nose with a broad white crescent on its temporal side.
        = the SECONDARY angle: the paretic eye fixes, and the sound eye
          overshoots.

The secondary deviation is visibly greater than the primary — which is exactly
what the heading claims and what separates a paralytic from a comitant squint.
Neither frame is labelled, and the words "primary" and "secondary" appear only
in the abbreviated heading.

SLIDE 6 — Diplopia:
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Heading, then TWO side-by-side panels, each with a one-word caption in white:

  Uncrossed          |          Crossed

EACH PANEL contains the identical figure: a red HEART drawn at the top (the
fixated object); below it TWO WHITE GLOBES side by side, each drawn in profile
with its optic nerve stub projecting from the lower, nasal side; and TWO CYAN
RAYS running from the heart down into the globes, each ending in an ARROWHEAD
on a small RED DOT on the retina. In both panels the red dot lies at the
lower, TEMPORAL side of each globe, and in both panels the two rays run
straight from the heart to their own globe WITHOUT crossing each other.

⚠️⚠️ THE TWO PANELS ARE DRAWN IDENTICALLY. Compared side by side at full
resolution, the globes are in the same positions, the rays have the same
geometry, and the red retinal dots are in the same places; the ONLY difference
between "Uncrossed" and "Crossed" is the word printed above the panel.
Whatever the figure was meant to contrast, as printed it does not contrast it,
and the deck therefore never actually shows what distinguishes crossed from
uncrossed diplopia.

⚠️ AND NEITHER TYPE IS ASSIGNED TO A DEVIATION. No text anywhere in the deck
says that uncrossed (homonymous) diplopia goes with a convergent deviation and
crossed (heteronymous) with a divergent one. The two words appear here and
nowhere else.

SLIDE 7 — Abnormal Head posture: (direction of action of paralysed muscle)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Heading in yellow with the cyan sub-heading beneath it, then the first of the
three postures in red-numbered white text:

  Abnormal Head posture:
  (direction of action of paralysed muscle)

  1. Face turn (Rt or Lt)

⚠️ The cyan sub-heading is the ONLY statement of the RULE in the deck: the
head moves in the DIRECTION OF ACTION OF THE PARALYSED MUSCLE. It is repeated
verbatim on slides 8, 9 and 10 and never expanded, and no muscle is ever named
against a posture.

PHOTOGRAPH 1 (left) — an adult's eyes and brows in warm light. The eye on the
LEFT of the photograph is turned IN toward the nose: its iris sits nasally
with a broad white crescent of sclera on its TEMPORAL side. The eye on the
RIGHT of the photograph is straight, iris central. = a convergent deviation of
the left-of-photograph eye.

PHOTOGRAPH 2 (right) — a young man in a pale skull cap, whole face. His FACE
IS TURNED toward the RIGHT of the photograph: the picture-left ear and cheek
are fully in view, the nose and chin point to the picture-right — while BOTH
EYES ARE DIRECTED BACK AT THE CAMERA, i.e. deviated toward the picture-left
within their fissures. That combination, a turned face with the eyes swung the
opposite way, is the compensatory posture the slide is naming.

SLIDE 11 — 3rd Nerve Paralysis
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Heading only, then a montage of SIX photographs in 2 rows × 3 columns of one
elderly patient in different gaze positions. No captions, no arrows, no
muscle names.

The finding that fixes the side is in the BOTTOM-CENTRE frame:
  • BOTTOM CENTRE — the eye on the LEFT of the photograph is open with its
    iris central; the eye on the RIGHT of the photograph is COMPLETELY CLOSED
    by a drooping upper lid. = COMPLETE PTOSIS on the right of the photograph.

The other five frames, read against that:
  • TOP LEFT — gaze toward the LEFT of the photograph. The eye on the left is
    fully ABDUCTED, its iris hard against the outer canthus with a wide white
    crescent nasally. The eye on the RIGHT of the photograph has NOT followed:
    its iris sits near the middle with white sclera on its NASAL side, i.e. it
    is still turned out. = FAILURE OF ADDUCTION of the right-of-photograph eye.
  • TOP CENTRE — gaze toward the RIGHT of the photograph. The eye on the LEFT
    adducts, the eye on the RIGHT abducts fully, its iris hard against the
    outer canthus. = ABDUCTION IS INTACT on the affected side (the lateral
    rectus is not supplied by the third nerve — the deck draws this and never
    says it).
  • TOP RIGHT — both eyes up and to the right; the eye on the LEFT is elevated
    with white sclera below its iris, the eye on the RIGHT elevates far less.
  • BOTTOM LEFT — both eyes depressed and to the left; white sclera exposed
    above both irides.
  • BOTTOM RIGHT — the EXAMINER'S FINGER (visible at the top of the frame)
    lifts the ptotic upper lid. The eye beneath is revealed sitting DOWN AND
    OUT: its iris is displaced toward the outer canthus and low in the
    fissure, with a large area of white sclera above and nasal to it.

= the three classic components of a complete third nerve palsy — ptosis, an
eye "down and out", and failure of adduction and elevation — all present in
one montage and none of them captioned.

SLIDE 13 — 3rd Nerve Paralysis
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Heading, then TWO column captions, TWO clinical photographs and one borrowed
teaching DIAGRAM. The captions are two-coloured — the sign in dark red, the
cause in green:

  Pupil involved (Compression)      |      Pupil spared (Vascular)

PHOTOGRAPH 1 (left, under "Pupil involved") — an elderly man's eyes. The eye
on the LEFT of the photograph has a heavily DROOPING upper lid covering the
upper half of the cornea, and the pupil visible beneath it is LARGE and black.
The eye on the RIGHT of the photograph is wide open and deviated OUT, its iris
carried toward the outer canthus with white sclera exposed nasally.

PHOTOGRAPH 2 (right, under "Pupil spared") — a face in strong red light, the
lids held apart by fingers at the upper edge of the frame. Both eyes are wide
open; the eye on the RIGHT of the photograph is deviated, with a broad band of
white sclera exposed on its outer side, and BOTH PUPILS ARE OF SIMILAR,
MODERATE SIZE — neither is dilated. That equality is the whole point of the
right-hand column.

DIAGRAM (centre bottom, cream panel) — a two-halved schematic, printed labels:
  LEFT HALF
   • posterior communicating artery   (to a solid RED circle)
   • aneurysm compresses pupillary fibres   (the red circle pressing on a grey
     stippled circle from outside)
   • III nerve   (to the grey stippled circle — the STIPPLING, i.e. the
     pupillary fibres, is drawn all round its OUTER rim)
   • pupillary dilation   (to a drawn eye with a blue iris and a WIDE black
     pupil)
  RIGHT HALF
   • vascular disease of vasa vasorum leading to infarct of central nerve
     fibres   (with an arrow to a nerve cross-section whose CENTRE is solid
     BLACK — the infarct — while the stippled outer rim is intact, and small
     red vessels are drawn around it)
   • pupil normal   (to a drawn eye with a blue iris and a SMALL pupil)

= the anatomical reason for the rule: the pupillary fibres run on the OUTSIDE
of the nerve, so external compression takes them first while an internal
infarct spares them. The diagram states it in labels; the slide adds no prose.

SLIDE 14 — 4th Nerve Paralysis
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Heading only, then a small inset photograph at the top right and a NINE-frame
gaze montage (3 × 3) of the same child below. No captions and no arrows.

INSET (top right) — the child's face. TWO findings, both visible:
  • the HEAD IS TILTED: the hairline, both eyebrows and the line joining the
    two eyes all slope down toward the LEFT of the picture;
  • the eye on the RIGHT of the picture sits HIGHER than its fellow and is
    carried slightly OUT, with white sclera showing on its nasal side.
  = a hyperdeviation with a compensatory head tilt — the two signs of a
  superior oblique palsy, printed together and never captioned.

MONTAGE — nine frames, the child's eyes only, arranged as the nine positions:
  • ROW 1 (up-gaze row): both eyes elevated in all three frames, white sclera
    below each iris; the two eyes stay close to the same level here.
  • ROW 2, LEFT (gaze to the LEFT of the photograph) — the eye on the LEFT is
    fully abducted; the eye on the RIGHT of the photograph is ADDUCTED and
    RIDES UP, sitting clearly HIGHER than its fellow with white sclera below
    its iris. = the hyperdeviation is WORST IN ADDUCTION, which is the frame
    the whole montage exists for.
  • ROW 2, CENTRE (primary position) — the eye on the RIGHT of the photograph
    is still slightly the higher of the two.
  • ROW 2, RIGHT (gaze to the RIGHT of the photograph) — the eye on the RIGHT
    is now abducted and the two eyes sit much closer to the same level. = the
    deviation LESSENS IN ABDUCTION.
  • ROW 3 (down-gaze row): the EXAMINER'S FINGERS hold the upper lids up so
    the depressed eyes can be seen; the irides are carried to the bottom of
    the fissures with wide white sclera above them.

⚠️ NO MUSCLE IS NAMED AGAINST THIS PALSY, and the words "hypertropia",
"torsion" and "excyclotorsion" appear nowhere in the deck. ("Superior oblique
muscle" does appear once in the whole deck — as an anatomical label on the
borrowed orbital figure of slide 2 — but it is never connected to the fourth
nerve or to this montage.)

Heading, one small line DIAGRAM, and a NINE-frame gaze montage (3 × 3) of an

  • ROW 2, LEFT (gaze toward the LEFT of the photograph) — the eye on the LEFT
    abducts fully, its iris hard against the outer canthus; the eye on the

= the whole sixth nerve picture — esotropia in the primary position, full
adduction, deficient abduction — carried by the montage alone. "Lateral
rectus" is not printed.

SLIDE 18 — Myasthenia Gravis: Ptosis
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Heading only, then FOUR photographs — one large above, three in a row below.

PHOTOGRAPH 1 (large, top) — an elderly man's whole face against a dark ground.
BOTH upper lids droop heavily, each covering most of the cornea so that only a

PHOTOGRAPHS 2–4 (the row below) — the same younger man's eyes three times,

  • Frame 1 — both eyes open, irides fully visible, lids in a normal position.
  • Frame 2 — both eyes are now directed UP; the irides have risen behind the

    come down.
  • Frame 3 — the lids have dropped further still, covering most of both

= the ptosis WORSENS as the position is held. Fatiguability is demonstrated by

⚠️ FIRST, THREE THINGS THAT ARE PRESENT AND MIGHT WRONGLY BE FILED AS GAPS:
SURGERY IS COVERED (slide 16: after 6 months, resection–recession for
incomplete, muscle transposition for complete). MYASTHENIA GRAVIS IS COVERED
IN FIVE SLIDES (17–21), including the ice-pack and Tensilon tests and the
thymus on chest films. NYSTAGMUS IS COVERED, in four bullets (slide 22). What
is missing from each is below.

TESTS AND MEASUREMENT
 1. THE HESS CHART IS NOT MENTIONED AND NOT SHOWN. No chart, screen, plot or
    grid of any kind appears in the deck; the only printed graphic besides
    photographs is the chest radiograph of slide 21.
 2. THE BIELSCHOWSKY HEAD-TILT TEST IS NOT NAMED. Slide 9 photographs a
    spontaneous compensatory head tilt and slide 14's inset shows another,
    but nowhere is the head tilted BY THE EXAMINER as a test, and the name
    appears on no slide.
 3. DIPLOPIA IS NEVER CHARTED OR TESTED. There is no red glass, no Maddox
    rod, no Lees screen and no instruction to ask which image is which. The
    two words "Uncrossed" and "Crossed" on slide 6 sit over two panels drawn
    identically (see that block), so the deck does not even illustrate the
    distinction, let alone assign it to a deviation.
 4. THE FORCED DUCTION TEST IS NOT MENTIONED, so paralytic weakness is never
    distinguished from mechanical restriction anywhere in the deck.
 5. NO DEVIATION IS MEASURED. No prism, no prism dioptre, no degree and no
    angle figure appears; slide 5 states "2ry angle > 1ry angle" as an
    inequality and gives no numbers.
 6. THE COVER TEST IS NOT PERFORMED OR MENTIONED HERE. It belongs to L29;
    this deck shows deviations directly and never covers an eye.

CAUSES, AND CONDITIONS NOT NAMED
 7. NO CAUSE IS GIVEN FOR ANY OF THE THREE NERVE PALSIES except the two on
    slide 13's diagram — a posterior communicating artery aneurysm compressing
    the third nerve, and vasa vasorum infarction of its centre. DIABETES,
    HYPERTENSION, TRAUMA, TUMOUR, RAISED INTRACRANIAL PRESSURE, CAVERNOUS
    SINUS DISEASE and MULTIPLE SCLEROSIS are named on no slide. The sixth
    nerve's classic false-localising role in raised ICP is absent.
 8. NO INVESTIGATION IS OFFERED FOR A NERVE PALSY. Slide 13's diagram names an
    aneurysm and the deck never mentions CT, MRI or angiography; the only
    imaging anywhere is the chest film for the thymus.
 9. ABERRANT REGENERATION of the third nerve is not mentioned.
10. DUANE'S RETRACTION SYNDROME and BROWN'S SYNDROME are absent entirely, as
    are congenital fibrosis, THYROID EYE DISEASE / restrictive myopathy,
    ORBITAL BLOW-OUT FRACTURE and INTERNUCLEAR OPHTHALMOPLEGIA. That last is
    worth naming explicitly: a deck that covers third, fourth and sixth nerve
    palsies plus myasthenia contains no supranuclear or internuclear lesion at
    all, and the word "gaze palsy" is printed nowhere.
11. THE FOURTH AND SIXTH NERVE SLIDES NAME NO MUSCLE. "Superior oblique
    muscle" and "Lateral rectus muscle" occur exactly once each in the deck —
    as anatomical labels on the borrowed orbital figure of slide 2 — and are
    never connected to slide 14 or slide 15. The medial rectus, inferior
    rectus and inferior oblique are not labelled even there.

RULES STATED BUT NOT EXPLAINED
12. THE HEAD-POSTURE RULE IS NEVER WORKED THROUGH. The cyan sub-heading

13. "FALSE ORIENTATION" (slide 10) IS NOT DEFINED — three symptoms are listed

14. PAST POINTING is not mentioned, although it is the classical partner of

15. HERING'S AND SHERRINGTON'S LAWS are not named, so the mechanism behind the

16. COMITANT VERSUS INCOMITANT is never contrasted in words in this deck. The

17. THE SURGERY IS THREE LINES. No muscle is named, no millimetres are given,

19. THE "AFTER 6 MONTHS" OF SLIDE 16 IS NOT EXPLAINED — the deck never says
