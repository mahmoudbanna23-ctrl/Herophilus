                       1. Rhegmatogenous Retinal
                       Detachment (RRD)
    Types of retinal   This is the most common type. It occurs
     detachment        when a physical tear or break develops in
                       the retina.
                          Mechanism: Vitreous fluid (the gel-like
                          substance in the eye) seeps through the
                          tear and collects in the subretinal space,
                          physically pushing the retina away from its
                          underlying blood supply.
                          Common Causes: Aging (vitreous
                          shrinkage), high myopia
                          (nearsightedness), or eye trauma.

                       2. Tractional Retinal Detachment
    Types of retinal   (TRD)
                       In this type, the retina is physically pulled
     detachment        away from the back of the eye by scar tissue
                       or membranes.
                           Mechanism: Scar tissue contracts on the
                           surface of the retina, creating "traction"
                           that lifts it. Unlike RRD, there is typically
                           no initial tear.
                           Common Causes: Advanced diabetic
                           retinopathy (the most common cause),
                           sickle cell disease, or previous ocular
                           trauma.

     Management and Surgical Options

       Treatment is tailored to either repairing a
       physical tear, removing pulling forces, or
       addressing an underlying medical cause.




13
     Management and Surgical Options
     1. Rhegmatogenous Detachment (Repairing
     Tears)
     Surgical intervention is almost always required
     to seal the retinal break:
         Vitrectomy: The surgeon removes the
         vitreous gel and replaces it with a gas or oil
         bubble to push the retina back.
         Scleral Buckling: A silicone band is placed
         around the eye to push the eye wall inward,
         helping the retina reattach.
         Pneumatic Retinopexy: A gas bubble is
         injected into the eye to seal the tear from the
         inside, often followed by laser or
         cryotherapy (freezing).




14
     Management and Surgical Options
     2. Tractional Detachment (Relieving
     Pull)
     Treatment focuses on removing the
     mechanical force pulling the retina
     away:
         Vitrectomy & Membrane Peeling:
         Surgeons remove the vitreous and
         carefully peel away fibrovascular
         membranes or scar tissue that is
         pulling on the retina.
         Underlying Disease Control:
         Long-term management involves
         strict control of the causative
         condition, most commonly diabetic
         retinopathy

     Management and Surgical Options
     3. Exudative Detachment (Treating the
     Cause)
     Surgery is rarely the first step; the focus is
     on stopping fluid leakage:
        Medical Therapy: Use of
        corticosteroids or immunosuppressants
        for inflammatory conditions like uveitis
        or VKH syndrome.
        Injections: Anti-VEGF drugs to stop
        leaking from abnormal blood vessels.
        Specific Treatments: Targeted therapy
        for infections, hypertension control, or
        radiation/surgery for ocular tumors.
