SLIDE 6 — Types of retinal detachment: 1. RRD   ⚠️ HIGH YIELD
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Types of retinal detachment
1. Rhegmatogenous Retinal Detachment (RRD)
This is the MOST COMMON type. It occurs when a physical TEAR OR BREAK develops in the
retina.
 • Mechanism: Vitreous fluid (the gel-like substance in the eye) seeps through the tear
   and collects in the subretinal space, physically pushing the retina away from its
   underlying blood supply.
 • Common Causes: Aging (vitreous shrinkage), high myopia (nearsightedness), or eye
   trauma.


─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 7 — Types of retinal detachment: 2. TRD   ⚠️ HIGH YIELD
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Types of retinal detachment
2. Tractional Retinal Detachment (TRD)
In this type, the retina is physically PULLED away from the back of the eye by scar
tissue or membranes.
 • Mechanism: Scar tissue contracts on the surface of the retina, creating "traction"
   that lifts it. UNLIKE RRD, THERE IS TYPICALLY NO INITIAL TEAR.
 • Common Causes: Advanced diabetic retinopathy (THE MOST COMMON CAUSE), sickle cell
   disease, or previous ocular trauma.


─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 8 — Types of retinal detachment: 3. ERD   ⚠️ HIGH YIELD
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Types of retinal detachment
3. Exudative (Serous) Retinal Detachment (ERD)
This type occurs when FLUID LEAKS and builds up behind the retina WITHOUT ANY TEARS OR
TRACTION.
 • Mechanism: Inflammatory or vascular conditions cause fluid from the choroid (the
   layer of blood vessels behind the retina) to seep into the subretinal space.
 • Common Causes: Inflammatory diseases (like uveitis), tumors (such as choroidal
   melanoma), or systemic conditions like severe hypertension.

SLIDE 14 — Management: 1. Rhegmatogenous Detachment (Repairing Tears)   ⚠️ HIGH YIELD
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Management and Surgical Options
1. Rhegmatogenous Detachment (Repairing Tears)
SURGICAL INTERVENTION IS ALMOST ALWAYS REQUIRED to seal the retinal break:
 • Vitrectomy: The surgeon removes the vitreous gel and replaces it with a GAS OR OIL
   BUBBLE to push the retina back.
 • Scleral Buckling: A SILICONE BAND is placed around the eye to push the eye wall
   inward, helping the retina reattach.
 • Pneumatic Retinopexy: A GAS BUBBLE is injected into the eye to seal the tear from the
   inside, often followed by LASER OR CRYOTHERAPY (FREEZING).

Management and Surgical Options
2. Tractional Detachment (Relieving Pull)
Treatment focuses on removing the mechanical force pulling the retina away:

SLIDE 16 — Management: 3. Exudative Detachment (Treating the Cause)   ⚠️ HIGH YIELD
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Management and Surgical Options
3. Exudative Detachment (Treating the Cause)
SURGERY IS RARELY THE FIRST STEP; the focus is on stopping fluid leakage:
 • Medical Therapy: Use of CORTICOSTEROIDS or IMMUNOSUPPRESSANTS for inflammatory
   conditions like UVEITIS or VKH SYNDROME.
 • Injections: ANTI-VEGF drugs to stop leaking from abnormal blood vessels.
 • Specific Treatments: Targeted therapy for infections, hypertension control, or
   radiation/surgery for ocular tumors.
