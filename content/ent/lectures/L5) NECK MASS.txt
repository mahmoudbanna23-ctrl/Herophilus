                     Neck Mass

• Neck mass is defined as an abnormal lesion
  (congenital or acquired) that is visible, palpable, or
  seen on an imaging study.
• The Guideline further qualified neck masses as any
  mass below the mandible, above the clavicle, and
  deep to the skin, although it may involve the
  overlying skin secondarily
                    Neck Mass

• Neck masses may develop from infectious,
  inflammatory, congenital, traumatic, benign, or
  malignant neoplastic processes.
• Evidence suggests that a neck mass in the adult
  patient should be considered malignant until
  proven otherwise.
                           Neck Mass

• Importantly, an asymptomatic lateral neck mass in an adult may be
  the initial or only clinically apparent manifestation of head and neck
  cancer, such as squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), lymphoma,
  thyroid, or salivary gland cancer.

• Indications for referral in children with neck mass
• High risk alarming symptoms for an adult neck mass
• Accurate diagnostic workup of an adult neck mass with specific goal
  to reduce delay in diagnosis of malignant disease
• Evidence-based algorithm for management of patient with neck mass

           General Consideration
                Rule of 80
• 80% of non-thyroid masses are neoplastic
• 80% of neoplastic masses occur in males
• 80% of neoplastic masses are malignant
• 80% of malignant masses are metastatic
• 80% of metastatic masses originate from supra-clavicular primary
            General Consideration
                 Rule of 7
• Swelling existed for 7days : Inflammatory
• Swelling existed for 7months: Neoplastic
• Swelling existed for 7years : Congenital /Developmental
            General Consideration
                 80:20 Rule
• Adult Neck Mass = 80 : 20 malignant : Benign
• Pediatric Neck Mass= 80 : 20 Benign : malignant
