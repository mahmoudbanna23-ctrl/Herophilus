How Sensitive the Ear to Sounds?

 ▪ The Human ear is sensitive to sounds over a
   wide range of:
      - Frequencies: 20 – 20,000 Hz
      - Amplitudes: 0.0002 – 200 dyne/cm2

 ▪ It can detect the difference between two
   sounds occurring 10 µsec apart in time.

Note: The wide sensitivity range of human hearing
necessitates the use of Logarithmic (Ratio) scales rather
than Linear (Numerical) scales.
 Logarithmic Scales
▪ Decibel (dB) scale for sound pressure levels:
     - 0 dB = 0.0002 dyne/cm2 (threshold)
     - 120 dB = 200 dyne/cm2 (limit)
     So, the range of hearing is reduced to only 120
     units of measurement (instead of (10)6 units).

▪ Octave scale for sound frequency in Hz:
     frequency range is measured in octave bands
     i.e. each frequency is double the previous one
     e.g. 250, 500, 1000, 2000, 4000 Hz, …etc.
   Human Auditory Field




Note: The human ear is not equally sensitive across frequency range
  Hearing Tests (Audiometry)

  measured in response to sounds.

Speech Audiometry
Measures hearing using Speech Stimuli:
    - Words.
    - Sentences.

Measures the ability of a patient to :
 ▪   Detect speech at the patient hearing threshold
     (Speech Reception Threshold = SRT)
 ▪   Discriminate speech at levels above threshold
     (Speech Discrimination Score = SDS)
 Speech Discrimination Scores

Helps to identify type of hearing loss:
     - Conductive HL           Excellent SDS
     - Sensory HL              Good SDS
     - Neural HL               Poor SDS


Provides an idea about the benefit of a hearing aid
to restore normal speech discrimination.

Auditory Brainstem Response (ABR)

Are electrical voltages recorded from the brain
   stem auditory pathway using electrodes placed
   on the skull.

Are used to measure the hearing thresholds in
   newborns, infants, mental retardation and
   malingerers.
Also used to measure the neural conduction in
   neurological disorders (i.e., space occupying
   lesions and MS).

Otoacoustic Emissions (OAEs)
Are low level sounds generated by the cochlear
   hair cells movements either spontaneously or in
   response to sound (Evoked).

Can be recorded putting a sensitive
  microphone in the EAC.
Are used to measure the hearing
   thresholds as well as the
   cochlear function in different
   otologic disorders.
