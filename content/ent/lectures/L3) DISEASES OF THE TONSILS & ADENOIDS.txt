          INFECTIOUS MONONUCLEOSIS

 Caused by: Epstein Barr virus (EBV).

 COMMON contagious illness.
 Manifests by: Extreme fatigue, sore throat &
  largely swollen neck lymph nodes.
  Rash + Swollen spleen or liver (less common)
 Primarily affects teenagers & young adults.
 Spread usually via saliva ‘Kissing disease’.

 Treatment: Supportive – Fever medications (Paracetamol) –
  Avoid contact sports to avoid spleen rupture.
                                                  Emad A. Magdy, M.D.
             DIPHTHERIA PHARYNGITIS
 Caused by: Corynebacterium diphtheriae.
 Contagious disease with spread by respiratory
  droplets (Coughing/Sneezing).
 Prevention by vaccination (DPT vaccine).
 Manifests by: Thick gray true membrane in
  the throat. Extreme bilateral cervical LN
  swelling (Bull neck). Bacteria exotoxin causes
  low grade fever, sore throat and breathing
  difficulties. Can cause skin sores & rash.
 Treatment: Patients require isolation & hospital care.
  Diphtheria antitoxin (start early) + Antibiotics.  Emad A. Magdy, M.D.
           ACUTE TONSILLITIS
➢ Common condition.
➢ Predominantly affecting CHILDREN (peak 5-7 yrs.) &
  young adults.
➢ Viral or Bacterial – Throat swab generally not helpful!
➢ Manifestations:
   • Acute sore throat, fever & malaise +/- referred otalgia.
   • Severe odynophagia may lead to dehydration.
   • Tender bilateral cervical lymphadenopathy.
   • Tonsils are red & inflamed +/- generalized pharyngitis.
➢ Recurrence can lead to CHRONIC TONSILLITIS.

     COMPLICATIONS OF ACUTE TONSILLITIS

1. Peritonsillar abscess (Quinsy).
2. Rheumatic Fever: primarily affects children 5-15 years.
   - Caused by: overreaction of immune system to group A Beta hemolytic
       streptococci (GABHS) – typically 2-3 weeks after a strept sore throat.
   - Symptoms include: Fever, polyarthritis, jerky movements (Chorea).
   - Prevention to prevent permanent heart damage (Rheumatic Ht. Disease).
3. Acute Glomerulonephritis:
   - Causes: Hematuria, Proteinuria, Oliguria, high BP & generalized oedema.
   - Most children recover fully in weeks, 30% of adults may progress to
       chronic kidney disease.
                                                         Emad A. Magdy, M.D.
       TREATMENT OF ACUTE TONSILLITIS


1. Rest & plenty of fluids.

2. Symptomatic treatment for viral tonsillitis.

3. Bacterial tonsillitis requires antibiotics (Penicillin V
   or Ampicillin/Clavulanic acid).

4. TONSILLECTOMY for recurrent acute tonsillitis
   (Chronic tonsillitis).

   INDICATIONS OF TONSILLECTOMY
1. Recurrent acute tonsillitis (Chronic tonsillitis):
   • 7 or more documented episodes in one year or:
   • 5 episodes or more for 2 years or:
   • 3 episodes or more for 3 years.
2. Peritonsillar abscess.
3. Tonsillitis causing febrile seizures.
4. Bilateral tonsillar hypertrophy (causing airway obstruction,
    difficulty in deglutition &/or interference with speech).
5. Tonsilloliths with unpleasant halitosis.
6. Suspicion of tonsillar malignancy (Unilateral enlarged tonsil –
    lymphoma or epidermoid carcinoma).

TYPES OF TONSILLECTOMY:
• EXTRACAPSULAR (Total): Entire tonsil and capsule are removed,
  exposing the superior constrictor muscle
• INTRACAPSULAR (Partial): Majority of tonsil is removed but capsule
  is left behind to protect underlying muscle, which reduces PO pain.

COMPLICATIONS OF TONSILLECTOMY
1. HEMORRHAGE (in less than 5% of cases) – Types:
   • Primary: during time of surgery.
   • Reactionary: within first 24-hours (dt. High BP or slipped ligature or
      dislodged clot).
   • Secondary: due to 2ry infection (usually 5-10 days PO).
2. Dehydration: due to poor fluid intake caused by PAIN.
3. Dental injury: from intubation or mouth gag insertion.
4. Lingual nerve palsy: caused by prolonged mouth gag tongue blade
    pressure (usually temporary).

5. Internal carotid artery injury: Catastrophic – due to deep tonsillar
    bed cautery, suturing or dissection.
                                                          Emad A. Magdy, M.D.
    COMPLICATIONS OF TONSILLECTOMY
6. Uvular Oedema : dt. bilateral obstruction of uvular veins - may cause respiratory
    obstruction.

7. Velopharyngeal incompetence: leading to regurgitation of fluid from nose &
    nasal tone of voice (usually temporary dt. palatal paresis but may be permanent due to
    palatal shortening from fibrosis).

8. Oropharyngeal stenosis: due to excessive cautery tissue injury, which is very
    difficult to treat.

9. Chest complications “including pneumonia”: from aspiration during or
    immediately after surgery.

10. Anesthesia complications & hemorrhage account for majority
    of deaths.

  INDICATIONS OF ADENOIDECTOMY

1. Obstructive sleep-disordered breathing (most common).

2. Recurrent/Chronic Otitis Media (5+ times in a year or 3+
   per year over two years).

3. Otitis Media with effusion (adenoidectomy + Ear tube
   insertion).

4. Chronic adenoiditis/sinusitis (symptoms lasting over 3
   months not responding to antibiotic therapy).

5. Orofacial/Dental changes (from persistent mouth breathing).

                                                   Emad A. Magdy, M.D.
    ADENOIDECTOMY TECHNIQUES
1. Conventional curettage (Adenoid Curette): This
   traditional technique uses a sharp, spoon-shaped instrument (curette)
   to scrape adenoid tissue from nasopharynx. Fast but relies on blind
   techniques or mirror visualization, which my leave residual tissue.

1. Conventional curettage (Adenoid Curette): This
   traditional technique uses a sharp, spoon-shaped instrument (curette)
   to scrape adenoid tissue from nasopharynx. Fast but relies on blind
   techniques or mirror visualization, which my leave residual tissue.




                                                      Emad A. Magdy, M.D.
         ADENOIDECTOMY TECHNIQUES
2. Suction Diathermy (Cautery): Uses specialized suction tool with
    an electric electrode to vaporize adenoid tissue, simultaneously removing
    it while controlling bleeding, which reduces intraoperative bleeding.

3. Coblation: Uses radiofrequency cold ablation - dissolves tissue at lower
    temp (40-70°C) compared to cautery (400°C) reducing thermal damage.

4. Microdebrider-assisted: Uses a powered rotating blade for precise
    thin-layer resection of tissue under direct visualization.

5. Endoscopic-guided removal: Uses a camera endoscope to directly
    visualize the nasopharynx (either transorally or transnasally) during any of
    the above techniques.

COMPLICATIONS OF ADENOIDECTOMY
1. HEMORRHAGE (most common) – as tonsillectomy could
   also be primary, reactionary or secondary. Causes:
     • Incomplete removal of adenoid tissue.
     • Blood disease e.g. hemophilia, leukemia.
     • Wrong diagnosis e.g. nasopharyngeal angiofibroma.
     • Injury to cervical vertebrae: dt. prominent atlas.
2.   Infection: local or descending.
3.   Dental injury: from intubation or mouth gag insertion.
4.   Eustachian tube injury.
5.   Recurrence: in very young children or incomplete removal
     (adenoids have no capsule).
6. Velopharyngeal insufficiency: especially if done in cases of
     unnoticed submucous cleft palate.               Emad A. Magdy, M.D.
