   and disadvantages of each tool
7. Formulate a basic management plan for patients with dysphagia, including referral pathways.
8. Apply clinical reasoning to select appropriate investigations for different patient scenarios.
9. Outline multidisciplinary approaches involving ENT, Phoniatrics and nutrition teams

1. Screening
• Swallowing screening is a pass/fail procedure its purpose is to
  identify:
   • The likelihood that dysphagia exists and
   • The need for further swallowing assessment
                                    (ASHA, 2009).
• Screening does not provide a detailed description of the patient's
  swallow function.
                                    (WHO, 2001).
1. Screening
Screening protocols may include
• The administration of an interview or a questionnaire that addresses the
  patient's perception of and/or concern with swallowing function.
• Observation
   • signs and symptoms of oropharyngeal swallowing dysfunction.
   • routine or planned mealtime situations, if indicated.
• Administration of the 3-oz water swallow test
                               (Depippo, Holas, & Reding, 1992)
• Recommendation for additional assessment.
2. Assessment
The purpose of assessment is to identify and describe
• Typical and atypical parameters of structures and functions affecting
  swallowing.
• Effects of swallowing impairments on the individual's activities .
• Determine whether the patient is an appropriate candidate for
  treatment and/or management:
   • Medical stability,
   • Cognitive status,
   • Nutritional status
   • Psychosocial, environmental, and behavioral factors
2. Assessment
Non-Instrumental Swallowing Assessment
• A case history.
• An oral mechanism exam, including one or more of the following:
   • Cranial nerve assessment.
   • Structural assessment of face, jaw, lips, tongue, hard and soft palate, oral
     pharynx, and oral mucosa.
   • Functional assessment of muscles and structures used in swallowing,
     including symmetry, sensation,strength, tone, range and rate of motion, and
     coordination of movement.
   • Observation of head–neck control, posture, oral reflexes, and involuntary
     movements.
2. Assessment
• Overall physical, social, behavioral, and cognitive /communicative
  status.
• Speech and vocal quality at baseline and any changes following bolus
  presentations.
• Monitoring heart rate and oxygen saturation using pulse oximetry.
• Alterations in bolus delivery and/or use of rehabilitative or
  compensatory techniques, as indicated.
2. Assessment
• Evaluation of the method (spoon, cup, self-fed, examiner-fed) and
  rate of bolus presentation.
• Secretion management skills.
• Labial seal and anterior spillage, and evidence of oral control.
• Signs and symptoms of penetration and/or aspiration, such as throat
  clearing or coughing before/during/after the swallow.
2. Assessment
• Consistency of skills across the feeding (fatigue on feeding/swallowing
  safety).
• Ability to clear the airway, and assessment of cough strength.
             (Martin-Harris et al., 2005)
2. Assessment

Instrumental Swallowing Assessment
• Use instrumental techniques to evaluate oral, pharyngeal, laryngeal,
  upper esophageal, and respiratory function as they apply to normal
  and abnormal swallowing.

• To determine the appropriateness and the effectiveness of a variety
  of treatment strategies.
2. Assessment
Indications :
• Concerns regarding the safety and efficiency of swallowing
   • Nutritional compromise
   • Pulmonary compromise
   • Concerns for airway safety (e.g., choking)
• Identify disordered swallowing physiology.
• Inconsistent signs and symptoms in the findings of a non-instrumental
  examination
• Chronic progressive degenerative conditions.
2. Assessment
Contraindications:
• Medically unstable.
• Unable to participate in an instrumental examination (cognitive ,
  alertness).
• If the instrumental assessment would not change the clinical
  management of the patient.

Videofluoroscopic Swallowing Study (VFSS)
• Is a radiographic procedure that provides a direct, dynamic
  view of oral, pharyngeal, and upper esophageal function
  (Logemann, 1986).
• Providing the patient with various consistencies of food and
  liquid mixed with barium, which allows the bolus to be
  visualized in real time on an x-ray during the swallow.
Videofluoroscopic Swallowing Study (VFSS)
• The VFSS is beneficial in
   • Identifying whether aspiration has occurred
   • Assessment of amount and timing of aspiration (silent aspiration)
   • Assessment of anatomy and pathophysiology of swallow function
     in the oral and pharyngeal phases.
   • Influence of compensatory strategies and diet changes
                              (Logemann, 2000).

The patient is asked to swallow three swallows of each of the following

High resolution impedance Manometry
• Is performed to assess motor function of the esophagus.
• A catheter with several electronic pressure probes is passed into the
  stomach to measure esophageal contractions and to define upper
  and lower esophageal responses to swallowing.
• Limitations:
    • No automatic analysis program
    • Improper placement of the manometric catheter
    • Could not detect aspiration
    • Could not represent the degree of pharyngeal residue.
