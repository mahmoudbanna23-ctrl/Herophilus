Definition
Snoring: Harsh, noisy sound produced by vibration of soft tissue of the

throat & upper airway due to partial airflow obstruction during sleep.

Sleep apnea: Cessation of airflow through the nose and mouth during

sleep > 10 seconds

   • Obstructive: 2ry to obstruction of the upper airway

   • Central: 2ry to cessation of thoracic & abdominal respiratory effort.

Incidence of snoring & obstructive OSA
• More common in old age. (30 Y = 30%, 40 Y = 40%, 50 Y = 60%)

• Male : Female = 2 : 1

• More common in obese persons with short neck.

• All patients with OSA are snorer but not all snorers have OSA

   • Primary snorers: Snoring without OSA (Occasional / Habitual)

   • Sleep-related breathing disorders: Snoring & OSA.
Pathophysiology of snoring & OSA
1. Partial obstruction of the upper airway 2ry to:

   • Space occupying lesion.

   • Hypotonia of palatal & pharyngeal muscle.

   Negative pressure and turbulent airflow during inspiration

   → vibration of the palate & Uvula – SNORING


2. Complete obstruction of the upper airway → OSA

→frequent arousal.
Risk factors
Obesity
   Deposition of fat narrows the upper airway lumen


Smoking
   Congestion of the upper airway mucosa leading to partial obstruction

Alcohol consumption and drugs e.g. Hypnotics, Sedatives
   Hypotonia of the palatal and pharyngeal muscles leading to their collapse.


Sleeping position
   Sleeping on the back causes the tongue to fall backward causing obstruction of
   the upper airway
Etiology
1. Nasal obstruction:
   DS, Hypertrophy of inferior turbinate, nasal polyp


2. Pharyngeal obstruction:
   Adenoid (most common cause in children), Tonsillar hypertrophy, bulky base of tongue, hypertrophy
   of lingual tonsils, receding mandible, macroglossia

3. Excessive length of the uvula and soft palate:
   Developmental, 2ry to mouth breathing.

4. Hypotonia of the palatal and pharyngeal muscles.
   Aging, Drug induced, Neurological disease
Presentation
Loud snoring (average 60 dB, can reach 85 dB)

Mouth breathing leads to dry mouth, dental cares, chronic sore throat

Witnessed attacks of sleep apnea & frequent arousal.

Morning headache.

Daytime sleepiness.

Frequent attacks of GERD.
Complications
Cardiovascular complications:
   • Hypertension, IHD, arrhythmia.

Cerebrovascular complications:
   • Decreased cerebral blood flow with OSA leads to high incidence of CVS.

Psychological complications:
   • Poor memory and loss of concentration.

   • Irritability and mood changes, Depression.

Social consequences
   • It can interfere with patients’ performance at work or school.

   • Disruption of the family life, diminished libido.
Assessment of patients with snoring /OSA
1. Clinical assessment
• Symptoms: Snoring, attacks of sleep apnea, frequent arousal.

• General examination: Obesity, BMI (Kg/M2>30), neck circumference (>17
 inches in male & >16 inches in females), receding mandible, macroglossia.

• ENT examination:
   • Nasal: deviated septum, polyp, hypertrophy of inferior turbinates
   • Oropharyngeal: bulky soft palate, long uvula, big tonsils
   • Indirect laryngoscopy: Bulky base of tongue, long folded epiglottis.

Management of snoring /OSA
1. Life style modification
Weight reduction.

Stop smoking / Alcohol consumption.

Avoid sleeping in supine position.

Elevate the head of the bed.

Avoid sleeping pills & sedatives.

Avoid high fat diet before sleep.
Management of snoring /OSA
2. Medications:
Intranasal corticosteroids / Antihistamines

Nonsteroidal nasal / oral sprays


3. Appliances:

Nasal dilators

Mandibular repositioning devices
Management of snoring /OSA
4. Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP)
Treatment of choice for moderate & severe OSA

Transmit air into the upper airway at positive pressure.

Limitations:
   Compliance in only 60% of cases.

   Aerophagia.

   Rhinitis (with nasal mask)

   Difficult to use in significant upper airway obstruction.

5. Surgical treatment
Indications:

• Snoring & mild OSA

• Moderate & severe OSA not compliant with CPAP

Surgical intervention according to site of obstruction:

• Nose / Nasopharynx.

• Oropharynx and soft palate .

• Base of tongue.

• Mandibular advancement surgeries.

   • Capping of the tube during day time

   • De-capping during sleep
Key points
• Sleep-related breathing disorder is a spectrum consisting of primary snoring at one end and severe
  OSA in the other end.

• Untreated OSA increases the risk of IHD, CVS, DM, psychological and social problems.

• Lifestyle changes are helpful and should be applied first for any patient suffering from snoring and
  OSA.

• CPAP is the treatment of choice for moderate or severe OSA.

• Surgical treatment may be helpful in snoring and mild OSA. Also could be done for patients with
  moderate and severe OSA if CPAP therapy fails.

• Surgical treatment should be tailored according the site of obstruction which is

  documented by DISE.
