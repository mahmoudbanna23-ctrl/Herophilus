NASAL OBSTRUCTION
Nasal Obstruction: Classification

  According to etiology:
  a. Mucosal
  b. Structural
  c. Mixed
Nasal Obstruction: Classification

  According to duration:
  a. Fluctuating (mucosal or mixed etiology)
  b. Constant (structural etiology)
Nasal Obstruction: Classification

  According to laterality:
  a. Unilateral nasal obstruction usually points to structural abnormality or uni-
     lateral pathology (antrochoanal polyp, odontogenic pathology)
  b. Bilateral nasal obstruction may be mucosal, structural, or mixed
  c. Alternating nasal obstruction is actually a variety of bilateral nasal obstruc-
     tion and is frequently position-related.
Nasal Obstruction: Classification

  According to position:
  a. Position dependent (allergy, rhinitis, rhinosinusitis)
  b. Non-position dependent (septal deviation, tumors)
Nasal Obstruction: Etiology

  Nasal Obstruction due to Mucosal Pathology:
  1.   All forms of rhinitis.
  2.   Nasal allergy.
  3.   Acute rhinosinusitis.
  4.   Chronic rhinosinusitis.
  5.   Fungal rhinosinusitis.
  6.   Mucosal pathology due to systemic disease
Nasal Obstruction: Etiology


   Nasal Obstruction due to structural pathology:
    A. Congenital
        1. Choanal atresia
        2. Pyriform aperature stenosis
Nasal Obstruction: Etiology

B. Acquired:
  1.   Adenoids
  2.   Trauma.
  3.   Septal deviation
  4.   Septal perforation
  5.   Hypertrophy of the turbinates
  6.   Nasal valves dysfunction
  7.   Nasal polyps
  8.   Sinonasal and nasopharyngeal tumors and cysts
Nasal Obstruction: Etiology

  Causes of nasal obstruction in children:
  1.   Choanal atresia/pyriform aperture stenosis
  2.   Congenital masses and cysts.
  3.   Foreign bodies.
  4.   Adenoids.
  5.   Rhinitis and allergy.
  6.   Nasal polyps (cystic fibrosis).
Nasal Obstruction: Practical Points


    Nasal allergy and viral infections are the commonest causes of nasal
   obstruction(in adults)
    Allergy and adenoids are common causes of nasal obstruction in
   children
Nasal Obstruction: Assessment

 Subjective:
  1. History taking (laterality, duration, other symptoms)
  2. Validated questionnaire (NOSE scale, SNOT 22)
Nasal Obstruction: Assessment

 Objective:
  1. Clinical examination and nasal endoscopy.
  2. Radiologic examination (CT scans, MRI).
  3. Rhinomanometry and acoustic rhinometry (mostly of academic interest)
Nasal Obstruction: Treatment

 Treatment:
  a. Treatment of The cause (main line)
  b. Decongestants (for temporary relief):
     1.   Topical (nasal drops) for not more than 5 days to avoid development of rhinitis
          medicamentosa (drug-induced rhinitis)
     2.   Oral (pseudoephedrine and phenylafrin) contraindicated in hypertension, hypertrophy
          of the prostate, and glaucoma
