• Malignant external otitis (necrotizing external otitis,
  skull base osteomyelitits)
   • Serious condition
   • Potentially fatal
   • Possibility of intracranial spread and cranial nerve
     palsies
   • Usually in immunocompromised (DM, renal failure, HIV,
     hematologic or immune deficiency diseases)
   • Causative organism usually Pseudomonas aeroginosa
• Clinical picture
   • Symptoms:
       • Pain (chronic, more at night, may become disabling)
       • Ear discharge
       • Symptoms related to cranial nerve palsies
       • Hearing loss
• Clinical signs
   • Granulation
   • Discharge
   • Cranial nerves
• Investigations:
   • CT scans
   • MRI
   • Isotope scanning (Tc99, Ga67)
   • Bacteriological testing
   • Biopsy
• Lines of treatment:
   • Control of the causative factors
   • Hospitalisation may be needed initially
   • Systemic antibiotics (against pseudomonas) for long
     periods of time (may take 3-6 months), a combination of
     IV antibiotics is usually needed
   • Debridement of the granulations in ear canal (and
     biopsy to rule out malignancy)
   • Hyperbaric oxygen therapy
