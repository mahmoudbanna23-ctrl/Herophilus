Infantile larynx
Anatomical:
• Small relative to body surface area.
• Higher in position.
• Funnel shaped →Narrow SG region (4.5 mm)
• Epiglottis: Long & folded on itself.
• Big arytenoids, Short VC.
• Loose submucosal tissue → edema formation.
Neurological:
• Premature neuromuscular control → VC spasm
• Premature cough reflex → FB aspiration

Laryngomalacia
          Laryngo = larynx, malacia = abnormal flaccidity.


Collapse of the supraglottic components during inspiration:
 • Epiglottis.
 • Aryepiglottic folds.
 • Arytenoids.
Laryngomalacia - Incidence

• Most common congenital laryngeal anomaly (60%).

• Most common cause of chronic stridor in infants.

• More common in males.

• Associated with GERD in 80 - 90% of cases.

• Synchronous airway anomalies in 15 - 20% of cases.
Laryngomalacia – Clinical presentation
• High pitched inspiratory stridor:

   Starts few weeks after birth → increase in severity over 6-9 months.

   Increased on supine, feeding & agitation. Decreased on neck extension & prone position.

   Resolves spontaneously 18 – 24 months in 80 - 90% of cases.


• Voice = normal.

• Feeding = normal (interrupted in severe cases).

• General condition = normal.
Laryngomalacia – Laryngoscopy

• Omega shaped epiglottis.

• Short AE fold.

• Redundant mucosa over arytenoids.

• Signs of GERD.
Laryngomalacia – Conservative management
• Assurance of parents.
• Anti reflux measures:
   Type of feeding.
   Position.
   Proton pump inhibitor.   Improvement at age of 2 years in
                                     90% of cases
  Prokinetic agents.

• Follow up:
  Monitor growth.
   Respiratory distress.
Laryngomalacia – Surgical intervention
Indications of surgical treatment:

• Severe stridor, cyanotic attacks.

• Weight loss, failure to thrive.

• Chest deformity.

• Pulmonary hypertension.

• Hypoxia, Hypercarbia.
Laryngomalacia – Surgical intervention

Supraglottoplasty:

  • Aryepiglottic fold lysis

  • Trimming of epiglottis.

  • Vaporization of arytenoid mucosa.

• Acute viral laryngitis.

• Acute epiglottitis = Supraglottitis.

Acute viral laryngitis
Etiology:
   • Rhinovirus, RSV, ParaInfluenzae virus, Adenovirus

Clinical picture:
   • Dysphonia, hoarseness of voice.
   • Stridor in infants < 1 year of age.
   • Low grade fever, cough, runny nose.

Treatment: Conservative (NO Antibiotic)
   • Antipyretics, humidification, decongestants, voice rest.
Acute Epiglottitis = Supraglottitis

Incidence:
  • Children 2 – 6 years (can occur at any age)

Etiology:
  • Haemophilus influenzae type B (90%)

  • Other (10%)

              HIB vaccine reduced the incidence > 90%
Acute Epiglottitis = Supraglottitis
Clinical picture
   Rapidly progressive course of:
   • Fever.
   • Severe Odynophagia.
   • Inspiratory Stridor.
   • Muffled voice.
   Examination:
   • Temp. > 38.5
   • Irritable, sitting position, drooling of saliva
Acute Epiglottitis = Supraglottitis
Diagnosis:
   • X-ray: "thumb print" or "cucumber" sign


                                     Laryngoscopy is contraindicated


                                           Airway obstruction
Acute Epiglottitis = Supraglottitis

Treatment
  • Steroids in massive dose.
  • Analgesics, Antipyretics, Fluids, Observation.

  • Antibiotic:
     • Ceftriaxone.

  • Severe stridor:
     • Endotracheal intubation.
     • Tracheostomy.
Acute Laryngotrcheobronchitis = Croup

Incidence:
  • Most common infectious cause of airway obstruction in children.
  • Ages: 6 months to 3 years.

Etiology:
  • Viral infection most commonly caused by the parainfluenzae virus.
Acute Laryngotrcheobronchitis = Croup
Clinical picture:
   Gradual progressive course of:
   • Low grade fever.

   • Barking cough.

   • Hoarseness of voice
   • Biphasic stridor.

Diagnosis
   • X-ray: “Steeple sign”
Acute Laryngotrcheobronchitis = Croup

Treatment:
   • Good hydration.
   • Analgesics, antipyretics.
   • Nebulized Racemic Epinephrine.
   • Steroids: systemic & nebulized.
   • Antibiotics.
   • Severe airway obstruction (rare):
       • Endotracheal intubation.
       • Tracheostomy.

FB Aspiration
Incidence of FB Aspiration
• The most common cause of death in home in children < 6 years.

• 1 – 3 years. (75%)

• M:F = 2:1

• Death rate:

   • Before endoscopy = 20%.

   • After endoscopy < 2%.
Why FB aspiration is common in children ?

• The larynx is higher in position.

• Children like to explore their environment with their mouth.

• They are unable to chew food properly.

• They have a propensity to play with eating.

• Premature cough reflex.

• They lack posterior dentition.
Site of impaction of FB aspiration

• Larynx (4%).


• Trachea (6%).


• Right bronchus (57%).


• Left bronchus (33%)
Clinical presentation of FB aspiration
• Acute phase:

   • Stridor, respiratory difficulties.

   • Pain in the root of the neck.

   • Change of voice, cough.

• Late phase: Neglected FB

   • Repeated or persistent chest infection not responding
     to medical treatment.

   • Hemoptysis. (organic neglected FB)
   • Chronic cough.
