 We must distinguish between viral infections,
which resolve without antibiotic treatment, and
    bacterial infections, which may require
            antimicrobial therapy.
          It is important to distinguish between viral
                  and bacterial rhinosinusitis.



Factors suggesting acute bacterial sinusitis are:
• Worsening of the symptoms after five days.
• Persistence of symptoms for more than 10 days.
• Presence of symptoms out of proportion to those typically associated with
viral URTI.
• Worsening of symptoms after initial improvement (“double-sickening”)
                  Antimicrobial Treatment

Resistance to antibiotics develops through several mechanisms:
 Bacterial production of enzymes such as beta-lactamase that
destroy the antibiotic.
 Membrane changes that render the bacterial outer membrane
impermeable to the antibiotic.
 Alterations in the microorganism’s RNA that affect the affinity
of binding sites for the antibiotic.
 Presence of efficient membrane efflux transporters for the
antibiotic within the infecting pathogen.
The misuse of antibiotics should not be
a replacement for spending time talking

patient’s family the differences between
      viral and bacterial infections.
