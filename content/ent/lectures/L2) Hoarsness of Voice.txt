BENIGN LARYNGEAL AND VOCAL CORD LESIONS
VOCAL CORD POLYPS
They are very common benign non neoplastic tumors
of the vocal cord, it is mainly a disease of males, most
patients are smokers, many use their voice
professionally. The age peak is between 30 and 50
years.
Types of polyps:
• Hemorrhagic
• Hyalinous
• Fibrous
• gelatinous
Symptoms:
Persistent hoarseness. In case of extreme growth,
  vocal fold polyps sometimes lead to dyspnea, stridor
  and even choking spells or intubation difficulties.
Signs:
- small pedunculated or sessile lesions
 -on the anterior third of the free edge of the true vocal
cord.
 -They are mobile during phonation when pedunculated.
-Polyps occur mostly singularly and unilaterally, but
bilateral cases have also been recorded.
Treatment:
Small polyps caused by irritating factors such as
smoking may be treated with smoking cessation alone.
 Other which are large in size theTherapy is surgical,
consists of suspension microlaryngoscopic surgery
under general anaesthesia, or indirect
microlaryngostroboscopic surgery under local
anaesthesia, both followed by 2 days of complete voice
rest. Results of either surgical modality are good.

  Contact Granulomas

-They are lobulated swelling on the vocal process
     of the arytenoid cartilage. usually pale,
     pedunculated masses
- unilateral or bilateral.
- more in males.
-superficial ulcers occur when the thin mucosa
  overlying the firm cartilage of the vocal process
  is crushed repetitively against the opposite side,
  causing a breakdown of the mucosa.
Symptoms:
Chronic or acute hoarseness of the
  voice, vocal fatigue, throat pain, a
  rough foreign body sensation and
  cough.
Treatment
-Resting the vocal cords If forceful
  speaking is the primary cause,
-Surgery is also done to remove the
  ulcers, but this is often considered a
  last resort as recurrence is common.
In cases where contact granuloma is
  caused by gastroesophageal reflux
  disease, treatment for it is often
  initiated.
Intubation Granuloma
• The cause of Intubation Granuloma is
  endotracheal intubation. It appears only in
  adults.
• The incidence is higher in women 4 to 1
  because the tube falls more to the posterior
  commissure and because the mucosa is thinner.
• It seen on the vocal process of the arytenoid.
Treatment:
• Excision when pedunculated.
• Attempts to remove during the the sessile stage
  are avoided, as recurrence may occur.
Microscopic laser excision appears to be more
  effective than surgical excision.
        Vocal fold cysts
• Vocal cord cysts are collections of fluid in
  sac-like formations on the vocal folds.
• Unilateral or bilateral smooth swelling on
  the membrenous vocal fold, the covering
  mucosa is normal.
• Females are more likely than males to
  develop vocal fold cysts and the menstrual
  cycle may alter the size of the cyst.
There are two types of vocal fold
  cysts:
• mucus retention
• epidermoid.
Treatment:
• vocal training and speech
  therapy along with medical
  interventions to decrease
  irritation of the cyst.
• In some cases, the cyst grows
  larger necessitating surgery to
  remove the cyst.
          Sulcus vocalis
Sulcus vocalis is a thinning or absence of a special
  layer of tissue, called the superficial lamina
  propria, which is the tissue covering the vocal
  cord requires to vibrate in order to produce
  sound.

Etiology
• Sulcus vocalis may be congenital or secondary to
   vocal trauma, infection, degeneration of benign
   lesions, or surgery. In addition, it may have
   relationship with ruptured congenital epidermoid
   cysts and also suggested that the disorder may
   demonstrate familial patterns. Typically, patients
   with congenital sulci have a lifelong history of
   disordered voice.
Clinical Presentation:
• hoarseness
• People with sulcus frequently must exert unusual effort
  to produce voicewhich lead to vocal fatigue .
• Because of the change in pitch which sometimes
  accompanies this disorder, men with sulcus sometimes
  complain that they are mistaken for women on the
  telephone.

Diagnosis:
Examination of the true vocal fold reveals a linear
  depression or an area of incomplete closure.
  Videostroboscopy reveals an area of decreased mucosal
  wave corresponding to the sulcus and more clearly
  demonstrates the associated incomplete closure.
    Reinke's edema
• Reinke's edema causes the vocal folds to bilaterally
   swell giving them an uneven, sac-like appearance.
• unilateral or bilateral bleach white swelling of the vocal
   fold, filled with fluid
• sessile and in advanced stages very mobile durind
   phonation. The lesion is bilateral in62-85% of cases.
• Commonly occur at the age of 40-60 years.
 Causes
Common causes of Reinke's edema include smoking,
   gastroesophageal reflux, hormonal changes such as
   hypothyroidism and chronic voice abuse.
Symptoms:
• persistant hoarseness is the initial symptom.
• The voice pitch is low: 80 Hz and lower for men and an
  average of 108 Hz for women. The pitch drops due to the
  increasing weight of the vibrating part of the vocal cord
  when edema develops.
• Pain is a very unusual symptom.
• In extreme cases dyspnea and even stidor can occur, for
  which at least one patient was reported to require a
  tracheotomy.
Treatment
-The first course of treatment is to remove the source of the
  irritant (e.g. smoking cessation, vocal rest, This can be
  effective if done soon after development of the edema.
-Surgery is also an option and can result in some restoration
  of the voice but is ineffective in complete restoration of the
  voice to its original state.
-Decortication of the vocal cords, i.e. removal of a strip of
  epithelium, is done first on one side and 3–4 weeks later on
  the other side.

  Congenital web
• Etiology and pathogenesis
   • Incomplete recanalization of the laryngotracheal
     tube.
• The most common site of development of
  laryngeal webs is at the level of the vocal folds
  anteriorly, although they may occur in the
  posterior interarytenoid or in the subglottic or
  supraglottic area.
Clinical presentation

 • Mild dysphonia
 • airway obstruction, depending
   on the size of the web.
 • Stridor is rare except in
   patients who have a posterior
   interarytenoid web.
