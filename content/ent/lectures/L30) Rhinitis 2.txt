Scleroma
• Chronic specific infection of the respiratory tract esp.
  the nose (rhinoscleroma)
• Klebsiella rhinoscleromatis ( gram negative )
• Mode of infection (unknown)
• Not uncommon in Egypt (endemic in Egypt )
• Teenagers especially in females (poor classes)
• Granulomatous tissue infiltrates the submucosa of the
  nose with accumulation of chronic inflammatory cells
  (plasma cells, lymphocytes and fibroblasts)
Scleroma- clinical features
 • Catarrhal
 • Hypertrophic: overgrowth of dense
   fibrous tissue
 • Atrophic : process of reabsorption
   and breakdown of tissues
 • Cicatricial: scar left by the
   formation of new connective tissue
   over a healing sore or wound.
Scleroma- pathology
    • The infiltration is charecterized by the
      presence of:
    • Miculicz cells (large foam cells
      containing the causative bacilli within its
      vacuoles)
    • Russel bodies (degenerated plasma cells
      with an eosinophil staining cytoplasm)

      Vasomotor Rhinitis (VMR)
• Vasomotor rhinitis is the most common type of non-allergic rhinitis
  especially in older patients.
• Vasomotor rhinitis results from changes in vascular tone
• activation the parasympathetic nerves that supply the nasal mucosal
• Symptoms of vasomotor rhinitis are similar to those of allergic rhinitis
     Vasomotor Rhinitis (VMR)
Etiology:
1.   Changes in humidity and temperature
2.   Environmental pollution.
3.   Active and passive smoking.
4.   Occupational irritants and pollutants
5.   Emotional factors.
6.   Endocrine factors: menstruation and pregnancy.
7.   Drugs: ACE inhibitors, β-blockers, and oral contraceptives.
      Vasomotor Rhinitis (VMR)
Treatment:
1- Intranasal steroids
2-Anticholinergic nasal sprays (ipratropium bromide) can decrease the
rhinorrhea.
3- oral/ local Decongestants
             NARES

Allergic-like rhinitis with eosinophilia in nasal secretions
           Allergy testing is typically negative.
   Treatment is similar to that of vasomotor rhinitis.
               Rhinitis medicamentosa

Etiology: Prolonged excessive use nasal decongestants
“rebound congestion (rebound phenomenon)”
Saline washes and topical steroids can help to reverse these changes.

1. Acute Invasive Fungal
   Sinusitis
• Most lethal form of fungal sinusitis – mortality 50-
  80%
• Two clinical populations
    • Poorly controlled Diabetics
    • Immunocompromised with severe
      neutropenia (chemotheraphy patients, BMT,
      organ transplants, AIDS) – Aspergillus accounts
      for 80% of infection in this group
  Acute Invasive Fungal Sinusitis
   Necrosis of nasal tissue and blackish appearance of the mucosa are
                                early signs.
 Involvement of the palate, orbit, and anterior cranial fossa is common.
  Meningitis, intracranial abscess, and cavernous sinus thrombosis may
                 develop along the course of the disease.
Treatment:
1. correction of immune suppression.
2. Aggressive surgical debridement.
3. Systemic anti-fungal medications: (mainly amphotericin B)
