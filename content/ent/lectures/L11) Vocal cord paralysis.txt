Vocal Cord Paralysis
• Immobility of the true vocal cords secondary to interruption of the

 nerve supply to the laryngeal musculature.

• It is a “Sign of another disease”

   • Neurological.

   • Iatrogenic / Traumatic

   • Inflammatory.

Anatomical considerations
Nerve supply of the larynx – Vagus nerve

1. Superior Laryngeal nerve (SLN):

   ❖External branch – Motor to Cricothyroid muscle

   ❖Internal branch - Sensory


2. Recurrent Laryngeal nerve (RLN):

   ❖Motor to all muscles of the larynx (Except Cricothyroid)

   ❖Sensory
Incidence
• Unilateral VCP > Bilateral VCP

• Bilateral VCP is more symptomatic

• Left VCP is more common (Longer course of left RLN)

• Vocal cord paralysis could be:

   • Abductor paralysis: Vocal cord fixed near the midline

   • Adductor paralysis: Vocal cord fixed away from the midline .
Pathophysiology of VC paralysis
Injury of the RLN:

• Paralysis of all intrinsic muscles EXCEPT Circothyroid ms.

• Paralyzed VC fixed in the Paramedian position.

Injury of the Vagus nerve:

• Paralysis of ALL intrinsic muscles of the larynx.

• Paralyzed VC fixed in the Cadaveric position.
Etiology of VCP
Idiopathic (? Viral)

Intracranial & Skull base lesions e.g. CVS, tumors, meningitis, trauma.

Cervical causes:
  • Iatrogenic: Thyroidectomy, parathyroidectomy, Zenker’s diverticulum, CP myotomy

  • Trauma: External trauma, intubation.

  • Tumors: Cancer thyroid, upper esophageal carcinoma.
Etiology of VCP
Thoracic causes (left side only)

  • Bronchogenic carcinoma / Mediastinal lymph node / Esophageal carcinoma

  • Apical fibrosis / TB

  • Iatrogenic: Cardiac, esophageal or lung surgery.
  • Dilated left atrium (Ortner’s syndrome)

Systemic causes: Peripheral neuritis / Myasthenia Gravis

Congenital vocal cord paralysis.

Diagnosis = Searching for the cause
History:
   • CVS, trauma, surgery, intubation, systemic diseases

Neck examination:
   • Thyroid swelling, lymph nodes, scar of previous surgery

Endoscopic examination + Biopsy from suspicious lesions:
   • larynx, pharynx, trachea and esophagus
Diagnosis = Searching for the cause
Radiological assessment:
   • X-ray chest
   • CT scan brain, skull base, neck and chest

Laryngeal Electromyography (1-6 months after the onset of paralysis):
   • Paresis or Paralysis.

   • Follow-up: Regeneration or Progressive degeneration.
Management of unilateral VC paralysis
1. Follow-up for 6 months for spontaneous recovery / Compensation.

2. Voice therapy.

3. Injection augmentation (Voice gap 1-3 mm)
   • Temporary: Autologous e.g. Fat, collagen, fascia or heterogeneous e.g. Hyaluronic acid

   • Permanent / Long lasting: Hydroxyapatite (Radiesse voice)

4. Framework surgery
   • Medialization Laryngoplasty = Type 1 Thyroplasty
