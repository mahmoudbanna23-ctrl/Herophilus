Age-Related Causes
•Infants: reflux, gastroenteritis, IJTI, pyloric stenosis, obstruction, sepsis
•Preschool: gastroenteritis, UTI, meningitis, appendicitis, obstruction, inborn errors
•School-age/adolescents: peptic ulcer/H. pylori, appendicitis, migraine, DKA, IBD, cyclic
vomiting, eating disorders, pregnancy
Clinical Assessment Pearls
• "Bile" described by parents may be stomach acid true green bile = obstruction.
•More proximal obstruction earlier and more prominent vomiting.
•Distal obstruction abdominal distension more pronounced.
•Small blood may be swallowed (e.g., cracked nipple, nosebleed); true hematemesis =
red flag.
•Distinguish vomiting after coughing (pertussis) from spontaneous vomiting.
•Always assess hydration and systemic illness.
