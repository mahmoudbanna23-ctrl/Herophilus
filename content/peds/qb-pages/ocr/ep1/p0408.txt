Malabsorption Disorders
•Features: abnormal stools, poor weight gain, nutrient deficiencies.
•True malabsorption stool = bulky, foul-smelling, hard to flush.
•Causes:
• Pan-malabsorption: e.g., cystic fibrosis.
• Specific defects: e.g., zinc malabsorption (acrodermatitis enteropathica).
Coeliac Disease
•Immune-mediated response to gluten in wheat, rye, barley.
•Diagnosis: genetic predisposition (DQ2/DQ8), antibodies (IgA anti-tTG, EMA), villous
atrophy on biopsy.
•Classical presentation (8—24 months): faltering growth, distension, abnormal stools,
irritability.
•Risk groups: type 1 diabetes, autoimmune thyroid disease, Down syndrome, first-degree
relatives.
•Management: lifelong gluten-free diet, dietitian support.
•Non-adherence nutrient deficiency, bowel malignancy risk.
