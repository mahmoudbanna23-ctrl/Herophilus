22. John, a 3-year-old boy, is referred to paediatric outpatients because of concern
about the development of pubic and axillary hair. His testes are 1.5 mL in size
(prepubertal). His blood pressure is 100/75 mmHg. Gonadotrophin levels are
undetectable (prepubertal), but his bone age is 5 years. What is the most likely
cause of his early pubertal development?
A. Adrenal tumor
B. Brain tumor
C. Idiopathic precocious puberty
D. Prader-Willi syndrome
E. Testicular tumor
Early pubic hair, advanced bone age, and undetectable
gonadotropins suggest peripheral (gonadotropin-independent)
precocious puberty. An adrenal tumor can produce androgens
causing early virilization without testicular enlargement.
