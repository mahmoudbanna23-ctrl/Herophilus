Tropical Infections
•Most febrile children after tropical travel have non-tropical infections (e.g., viral).
•Always consider malaria, typhoid fever, other tropical infections.
Typhoid (Salmonella typhi/paratyphi):
•Features: worsening fever, headache, cough, abdo pain, anorexia, myalgia;
Gl symptoms (constipation/diarrhea) usually appear in 2nd week; splenomegaly,
bradycardia, rose spots.
•Complications: Gl perforation, myocarditis, hepatitis, nephritis.
•Treatment: 3rd gen cephalosporin or azithromycin.
• Resistance common (esp. Indian subcontinent) older drugs (cotrimoxazole,
chloramphenicol, ampicillin) often ineffective.
