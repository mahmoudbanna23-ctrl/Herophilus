Fever with facial rash ("slapped cheek"): Parvovirus B19 (fifth disease) is the
most likely causative agent.
Early-onset neonatal sepsis organism: Group B Streptococcus is the most
common cause of early-onset neonatal sepsis and pneumonia.
Rapid-onset edema in a child: Sudden swelling of lips, eyelids, hands, and
genitalia without organ involvement suggests allergic angioedema.
Neonatal sepsis presentation: Early-onset sepsis is usually acquired from the
maternal genital tract during delivery; pneumonia is the most common
presentation.
Bacterial meningitis in children: Streptococcus pneumoniae is the most
common cause of bacterial meningitis after the neonatal period.
