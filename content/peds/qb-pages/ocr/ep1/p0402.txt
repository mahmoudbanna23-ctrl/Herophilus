Gastro-esophageal Reflux (GERI
•Definition: Involuntary passage of gastric contents into esophagus.
•Very common in infancy (immature LES, fluid diet, horizontal posture, short esophagus).
•Symptoms: Recurrent regurgitation/vomiting but normal growth & otherwise well.
•Course: Resolves spontaneously by 12 months.
•GERD (complicated reflux):
• More likely in neurodisability, prematurity, hiatus hernia, obesity, post-esophageal surgery.
• Complications: faltering growth, esophagitis (hematemesis, iron-deficiency anemia, feeding
pain), aspiration pneumonia/wheeze, Sandifer syndrome, BRUE (apnea).
•Diagnosis: Clinical. Tests (pH monitoring, impedance, endoscopy) only if atypical, complicated, or
non-responswe.
•Management:
• Uncomplicated: reassurance, feeding advice (smaller/frequentfeeds, thickeners, alginate).
• GERD: acid suppression (H2 blockers, PPIs). Avoid prokinetics. Consider cow's milk protein
allergy.
Refractory/complicated: Nissen fundoplication.
