This 2-year-old boy shows signs of virilization (penile growth, pubic hair) but prepubertal
testicular size (<2.5 cm), which suggests peripheral (gonadotropin-independent)
precocious puberty, rather than central (which would have testicular enlargement). The
history of paternal testosterone gel at home raises concern for exogenous androgen
exposure, which is a common cause of noncentral precocious puberty in young children.
Central activation of the HPG axis is unlikely given the very young age and small testes.
