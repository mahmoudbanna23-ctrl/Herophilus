17. A 3-day-old boy is having increasing abdominal distension and vomiting. He
is also noted not to have passed meconium since birth. Hirschsprung disease is
suspected. Which of the following investigations would be most helpful in
confirming the cause of obstruction?
A. Abdominal X-ray
B. Barium enema
C. Rectal biopsy
D. Rectal examination
E. Ultrasound scan
The gold standard for Hirschsprung disease is rectal biopsy,
which shows absence of ganglion cells. Imaging may help, but
biopsy is confirmatory.
