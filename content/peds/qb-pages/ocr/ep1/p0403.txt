Pyloric Stenosis
•Definition: Hypertrophy of pyloric muscle gastric outlet obstruction.
•Age: 2—8 weeks, more common in boys (4:1), esp. first-born, + family history.
•Clinical features:
• Non-bilious projectile vomiting (progressively forceful).
• Hungry after vomiting until dehydration sets in.
• Weight loss, dehydration.
• Visible gastric peristalsis, palpable "olive" mass (RUQ).
• Biochem: hypochloremic, hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis ± hyponatremia.
•Diagnosis: Ultrasound (standard).
•Management:
• Correct dehydration & electrolytes first (may take >24h).
• Surgical: Pyloromyotomy (open/laparoscopic).
• Rapid recovery: oral feeding within hours, discharge in 2—3 days.
