Frequent vomiting in thriving infant: Gastroesophageal reflux is common in
otherwise healthy, thriving infants; growth along the 50th percentile makes
pathology unlikely.
Early-onset neonatal infection: Symptoms in first 24 hours with lethargy and
respiratory distress suggest Group B Streptococcus infection.
Convulsions with rickets signs: Large head, widened fontanelles, and
prominent costochondral junctions suggest low serum calcium due to rickets.
Mild rash with posterior cervical lymphadenopathy: Self-limiting rash with
tender lymph nodes is typical of rubella.
Lactose intolerance in children: Often secondary to viral gastroenteritis;
congenital forms are rare and lactase supplements are not always needed
