I. A 14-year-old girl awakens with a mild sore throat, low-grade fever, and a
diffuse maculopapular rash. During the next 24 h, she develops tender swelling of
her wrists and redness of her eyes. In addition, her physician notes mild
tenderness and marked swelling of her posterior cervical and occipital lymph
nodes. Four days after the onset of her illness, the rash has vanished.
The most likely diagnosis of this girl's condition is:
a. Rubella
b. Roseola
c. Erythema infectiosum
d. Erythema multiforme
Low-grade fever, sore throat, maculopapular rash with
posterior cervical and occipital lymphadenopathy are classic.
Rash disappears within 3—4 days.
