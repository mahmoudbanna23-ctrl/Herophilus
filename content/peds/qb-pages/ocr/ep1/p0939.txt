Specific Bacterial Infections
Meningococcal Disease
1.
•Rapid, life-threatening.
•Rash: purpuric, non-blanching, irregular outline, necrotic center (>5 mm).
•Course: Septicemia shock DIC, limb loss, death within hours.
•Treatment: Immediate benzylpenicillin 1M / IV cephalosporin + urgent transfer.
•Prognosis: survivors usually recover well; least risk of long-term neuro sequelae.
Pneumococcal Infection
2.
•Carried in nasopharynx of healthy children.
•Causes: 0M, sinusitis, conjunctivitis invasive disease (pneumonia, sepsis, meningitis).
•High-risk: hyposplenism (e.g. sickle cell), nephrotic syndrome.
•Prevention: 13-valent pneumococcal vaccine (routine); prophylactic penicillin for
hyposplenic/asplenic.
