Infants commonly cross growth centiles in the first months due to catch-up
or catch-down growth; final height aligns with mid-parental height, and
deviations after age 5 may indicate pathology.
Edema is a key sign of protein-energy malnutrition, particularly in
kwashiorkor, due to low albumin and protein deficiency.
Exclusively breastfed infants are at risk of vitamin D deficiency, which can
lead to rickets if not supplemented.
A thriving 6-week-old with vomiting most likely has gastroesophageal
reflux; pyloric stenosis or gastroenteritis usually cause poor growth and
dehydration.
