If meconium is present during delivery, a decision whether to intubate and
suction the trachea will depend on the newborn's degree of vigor (vigorous IS
defined as strong respiratory efforts, good muscle tone, and a heart rate
greater than 100 beats/min). If the baby is nonvigorous as in this scenario,
direct suctioning of the trachea is indicated before many breaths occur which
will reduce the chance of forcing meconium into the lower airways and the
development of meconium aspiration syndrome (obstructed airways by
meconium, gas exchange impairment, and respiratory failure). When
meconium is present and the baby is vigorous, a simple bulb syringe or large-
bore suction catheter can be used to clear the airway and routine care can
proceed.
