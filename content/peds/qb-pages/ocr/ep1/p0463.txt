25. A 6-week-old male infant presents with a one-week history of worsening non-
bilious vomiting. His mother reports that the vomiting is projectile and seems to
be gradually worsening. He is eager to feed after vomiting but is having fewer
wet nappies than usual. On exammation, he is afebrile, appears hungry, and looks
mildly dehydrated. What is the most likely diagnosis?
A. Cow's milk protein intolerance
B. Gastroenteritis
C. Meningitis
D. Pyloric stenosis
Progressive non-bilious projectile vomiting with persistent
hunger and dehydration is typical of pyloric stenosis. Diagnosis
is clinical and confirmed by imaging.
E. Malrotation and volvulus
