CMV (most common congenital infection)
0.5-1/1000 live births.
•Incidence:
•Maternal infection usually mild/asymptomatic.
•90% infants normal, 5% symptomatic at birth (HSM, petechiae, CNS issues), 5% develop
late SNHL.
•Most common non-genetic cause of SNHL.
•Treatment: valganciclovir for symptomatic infants (SNHL/CNS involvement).
•No vaccine, not routinely screened.
Toxoplasmosis
•Source: raw/undercooked meat, cat feces.
•Transmission: during maternal primary infection (40% fetal infection).
•Most infants asymptomatic; 10% symptomatic with:
. Triad: retinopathy (chorioretinitis), intracranial calcifications, hydrocephalus.
•Treatment: pyrimethamine + sulfadiazine for 1 year.
•Often leads to long-term neurodisabilities.
