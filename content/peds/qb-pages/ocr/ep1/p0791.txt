Neonatal Infections
•Timing:
• Congenital (in utero): CMV, rubella, toxoplasmosis, syphilis, parvovirus, malaria, TB.
• Early-onset sepsis (<72h): usually vertical transmission (GBS E. coli, Klebsiella, Listeria).
• Late-onset sepsis (>72h): nosocomia//community (CONS, S. aureus, Enterococcus, Gram-
negatives, Candida).
Clinical features : nonspecific respiratory distress, temp instability, poor feeding, apnoea, lethargy,
seizures, jaundice, abdominal distension, shock.
Investigations: septic screen, CXR, FBC (neutropenia), cultures (blood ± CSF), CRP (serial).
Treatment:
•Early onset: benzylpenicillin/ampicillin + gentamicin.
•Late onset: flucloxacillin + gentamicin; modify if resistant (e.g., vancomycin for CONS).
•Stop antibiotics if cultures/CRP negative after 36—48h.
Meningitis: treat with ampicillin/penici//in + 3rd gen cephalosporin (e.g., cefotaxime).
•High mortality & neuro sequelae (cerebral palsy, hydrocephalus, hearing loss).
Prevention:
•GBS: intrapartum prophylaxis (risk-based in IJK; screening at 35—37 weeks in USA).
