Vitamin D Metabolism & Deficiency
•Sources:
80—90% skin synthesis WVB Vit D3).
10—20% diet (Vit D2/3: oily fish, egg yolk, fortified foods).
•Activation:
Liver 25-hydroxyvitamin D.
Kidney 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (active form).
• PTH stimulates activation when calcium is low.
•Functions:
• Regulates Ca & phosphate metabolism.
• Essential for bone health.
Role in immune modulation.
•Deficiency / Rickets:
Usually due to inadequate WB exposure, less often low dietary intake.
• Pathophysiology: Low Ca absorption TPTH bone demineralization + phosphate loss
growth plate abnormalities.
•Clinical Features:
• Rickets: leg pain, tiredness, bony deformity.
Hypocalcemia symptoms (esp. Q yrs, adolescence): seizures, tetany, stridor/apnea,
cardiomyopathy.
