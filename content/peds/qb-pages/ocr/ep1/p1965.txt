Gastroesophageal reflux in infants: Common in thriving babies with
occasional vomiting; red flags (projectile vomiting, poor growth) absent.
Breast milk jaundice: Benign, unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia; continue
breastfeeding and reassure.
Respiratory distress in preterm infant: Likely due to surfactant deficiency
(neonatal respiratory distress syndrome).
Jaundice in first 24 hours of life: Most important cause is hemolysis; early
physiological jaundice usually occurs after 24 hours.
Projectile vomiting in infant: Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis leads to loss of
gastric acid, causing metabolic alkalosis on blood gas.
Severe dehydration with shock: Prolonged vomiting and diarrhea causmg
hypotension and metabolic acidosis indicates hypovolemic shock.
