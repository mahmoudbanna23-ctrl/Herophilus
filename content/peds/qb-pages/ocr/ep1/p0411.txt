Hirschsprung Disease
•Absence of ganglion cells in distal bowel functional obstruction.
•Presentation:
• Neonates: delayed meconium, distension, bilious vomiting.
• Children: severe chronic constipation, distension, faltering growth.
•Diagnosis: suction rectal biopsy (no ganglion cells).
•Management: surgical resection and anastomosis.
•Thrush (oral Candida): common in neonates; treated with nystatin/azoles.
•Familial Mediterranean Fever (FMF): recurrent fever with serositis; risk of amyloidosis;
treated with lifelong colchicine.
•Aphthous ulcers: recurrent oral ulcers, self-limited.
•Herpetic gingivostomatitis: HSV in young children fever, vesicles, painful gums;
treated with fluids, analgesics, acyclovir if early.
