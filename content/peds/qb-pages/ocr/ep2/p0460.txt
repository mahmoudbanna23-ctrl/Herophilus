Pneumonia
•Infection of lung tissue; peaks in infancy and old age; high morbidity in children in low/middle-income
countries
•Pathogens:
Newborns: Maternal genital tract bacteria (Group B strep, gram-negative)
Infants/young children: RSV, Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, Staph. aureus
Children >5 yrs: Mycoplasma pneumoniae, Chlamydia pneumoniae, Streptococcus pneumoniae
•Clinical features: Fever, cough, tachypnoea, increased work of breathing, lethargy, poor feeding, localized
pain (pleural irritation)
•Signs: Crackles, bronchial breathing, dull percussion if consolidation or effusion, hypoxia
•Investigations: Chest X-ray if diagnosis uncertain; blood tests not reliably differentiating viral vs bacterial
•Management:
Mild/moderate: Oral antibiotics, supportive care (02 if hypoxic, analgesia, fluids)
Severe: IV antibiotics, oxygen, possible hospital admission
Complications: Pleural effusion, empyema may require chest drain or surgery
•Follow-up: Repeat X-ray only if persistent symptoms or lobar collapse
