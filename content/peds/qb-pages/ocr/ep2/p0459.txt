Bronchiolitis
•Most serious infant respiratory infection; 2—3% hospitalized annually
•Age: 1—9 months
•Causative organisms: RSV (80%), parainfluenza, rhinovirus, adenovirus, influenza,
human metapneumovirus
•Clinical features: Coryza cough, breathlessness, feeding difficulty, apnoea,
tachypnoea, tachycardia, chest recession, hyperinflation, crackles/wheeze
•High-risk infants: Premature, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, congenital heart disease,
underlying lung disease
•Investigations: Pulse oximetry; chest X-ray only if severe
•Hospital admission indications: Apnoea, 02 saturation <92%, poor intake, severe
distress
•Management: Supportive (02, hydration, monitor apnoea); no antibiotics,
bronchodilators, steroids, mist therapy, or nebulized hypertonic saline
•Prevention: Palivizumab for high-risk infants
