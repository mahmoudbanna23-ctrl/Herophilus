Most cerebral palsy cases result from prenatal brain injury rather than
perinatal or postnatal causes. Common factors include genetic abnormalities,
maternal infection, or intrauterine hypoxia.
'Spastic diplegia primarily affects the lower limbs, leading to increased tone,
scissoring, and toe-walking. It is commonly seen in premature infants due to
periventricular leukomalacia.
'Poor head control at 6 months indicates developmental delay, as normal
head control should be achieved by 4 months. This delay suggests a possible
motor developmental issue requiring evaluation.
• The most common cause of hematuria in children is urinary tract infection
(UTI). It typically presents with dysuria, frequency, or fever and resolves wi
appropriate antibiotic therapy.
