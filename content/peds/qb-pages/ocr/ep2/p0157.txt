2. Chromosomal Disorders
•General:
• Genes = DNA + histones -5 supercoiled —Y chromosomes
• Chromosomal abnormalities: Numerical or structural
• Prevalence: 1 in 150 live-born infants; 10% sperm, 25% oocytes
Down Syndrome (Trisomy 21)
•Incidence: 1 in 650 live births (increases with maternal age)
•Cytogenetics:
• Nondisjunction (94%) — maternal origin most common
• Translocation (5%) — Robertsonian (chromosome 21 + 14); parental carrier may increase
recurrence risk
• Mosaicism (1%) — some cells normal, some trisomy 21; milder phenotype possible
•Key Features:
• Facial: Up-slanting palpebral fissures, epicanthal folds, brushfield spots, flat nasal bridge
• Hands/Feet: Single palmar crease, short 5th finger, wide sandal gap
• Other: Hypotonia, pseudo-macroglossia, delayed motor milestones
