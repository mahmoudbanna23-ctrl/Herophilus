Leukemia is the most common malignant tumor in children. It presents with
pallor, bruising, recurrent infections, and bone marrow infiltration by malignant
cells.
• In a child from Kenya presenting with pallor and splenomegaly, malaria is the
most likely cause. It is highly prevalent in endemic regions and leads to chronic
anemia and spleen enlargement due to repeated parasitic infection.
The presence of red cell casts in urine strongly indicates glomerulonephritis.
Casts represent glomerular injury, as they are formed when red cells leak into the
renal tubules through damaged glomeruli.
'A child with coryza, cough, and mild fever but no respiratory distress likely
has an upper respiratory tract infection. These infections are viral and self-
limiting, requiring only supportive care.
