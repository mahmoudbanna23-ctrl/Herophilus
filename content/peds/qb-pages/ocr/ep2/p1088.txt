Congenital Hypothyroidism
"reening
•Detected by neonatal blood spot (Guthrie test) at day 5.
•Identifies raised TSH (primary hypothyroidism).
•Misses rare secondary hypothyroidism (low TSH).
•In some countries, T4 also measured.
•Incidence: 1 in 3000 births; preventable cause of severe learning disability.
Causes
1. Thyroid agenesis (absent gland, most common sporadic cause).
2. Thyroid maldescent (lingual or ectopic thyroid).
3. Dyshormonogenesis (enzyme defect, AR, —10%).
4. Maternal iodine deficiency (common worldwide, rare in 11K).
5 TSH deficiency (rare, part of pituitary dysfunction).
Clinical Features
•Often asymptomatic at birth, but later:
Poor feeding, faltering growth, prolonged jaundice.
Constipation, dry/cold skin, coarse facies, large tongue, hoarse cry.
Umbilical hernia, delayed development, goiter (sometimes).
Treatment
•Early thyroxine replacement (before 2 weeks) -5 prevents intellectual disability.
•Lifelong treatment, dose adjusted to maintain normal TSH/T4 and growth.
