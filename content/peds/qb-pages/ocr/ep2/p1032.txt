38. A 15-year-old girl presents with a generalised tonic-clonic seizure. There is a
history of dropping objects during breakfast-time and when she is tired. Her EEG
shows bilateral spike and polyspike and wave discharges. Which of the following
is the correct course of action?
a. Recommend antiepileptic drug treatment and wean if seizure-free after 2 years
b. Recommend antiepileptic drug treatment for life
c. Request a CT brain scan
d. Request an MRI brain scan
e. Start carbamazepine
This is juvenile myoclonic epilepsy (JME), characterized by morning
myoclonic jerks and tonic-clonic seizures. JME requires lifelong
treatment, as relapses are common if drugs are stopped.
