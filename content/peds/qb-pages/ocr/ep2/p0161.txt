1. Mendelian Inheritance
•Definition: Transmission of traits or diseases caused by variation in a single gene. Over 6,000
Mendelian disorders are known.
•Pedigree analysis: Essential for identifying inheritance patterns; symbols indicate
affected/unaffected individuals, carriers, consanguinity, twins, etc.
A. Autosomal Dominant (AD)
•Gene location: Autosomes (1—22).
•Inheritance: Only one mutated allele needed; males and females equally affected.
•Recurrence risk: Each child of an affected parent has a 50% chance of inheriting the mutation.
•Complicating factors:
• Homozygosity more severe phenotype (e.g., achondroplasia).
• Variable expression different severity among family members.
• Non-penetrance -+ mutation present but no symptoms (e.g., otosclerosis).
• De novo mutations new mutations in gametes; risk increases with paternal age.
