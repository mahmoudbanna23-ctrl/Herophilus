• In long-standing large VSD, pulmonary hypertension develops and causes
Eisenmenger syndrome. The left-to-right shunt reverses, leading to right-to-left
flow, cyanosis, and hypoxemia.
• Viral myocarditis causes acute inflammation of the heart muscle following a
viral infection. It presents with heart failure signs—tachycardia, gallop rhythm,
cardiomegalyand is often preceded by fever or rhinorrhea.
Coarctation of the aorta causes obstruction to blood flow beyond the left
subclavian artery. It leads to weak or absent femoral pulses, tachypnea, poor
feeding, and differential cyanosis in newborns.
A "boat-shaped" heart and unresponsive hypoxia to oxygen suggest
Tetralogy of Fallot. It presents with cyanotic (Tet) spells due to right-to-left
shunting through the VSD during exertion or crying.
