1. Hemolytic Anemia
•Definition: Increased red cell destruction.
•Red cell lifespan: Normally 120 days; in hemolysis, may fall to a few days.
•Compensation: Bone marrow increases RBC production; anemia develops when compensation fails.
• Types Of hemolysis:
• Intravascular: RBC destruction in circulation.
Extravascular: RBC destruction in spleen or liver.
Causes in Children
•Intrinsic RBC defects (most common):
• Red cell membrane disorders (e.g., hereditary spherocytosis)
• Red cell enzyme disorders (e. g. , G6PD deficiency)
• Hemoglobinopathies (e.g., 6-tha/assemia major, sickle cell disease)
Diagnostic Clues
•Anemia with normal WBC & platelets
•Splenomegaly/hepatomegaly (extramedullary hematopoiesis)
•Raised reticulocytes (polychromasia)
•Unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia, increased urinary urobilinogen
•Abnormal RBC morphology (spherocytes, sickle cells, hypochromic)
•Positive direct antiglobulin test (if immune)
•Increased RBC precursors in bone marrow
