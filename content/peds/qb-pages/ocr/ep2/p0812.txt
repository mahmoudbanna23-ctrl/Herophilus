Henoch—Schönlein Purpura (HSP)
•Epidemiology: 3—10 yrs, more common in boys, post-URTI, winter peak.
•Features:
• Rash (palpable purpura, symmetric, buttocks & legs)
• Arthralgia, periarticular edema
• Abdominal pain (colicky, risk of intussusception)
• Renal involvement (80% hematuria/proteinuria, rarely nephrotic syndrome)
•Pathophysiology: IgA immune complex vasculitis with complement activation.
•Prognosis: Most recover; risk of CKD if heavy proteinuria/HTN.
•Follow-up: At least 1 year to detect persistent renal disease.
