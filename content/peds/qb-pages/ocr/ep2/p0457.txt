Other Causes Of Stridor
Acute Stridor (Atypical Features / Poor Response to Treatment):
•Abrupt onset without infection consider anaphylaxis or inhaled foreign body.
•Usually due to structural problems:
• Intrinsic airway narrowing/collapse:
• Laryngomalacia (floppy larynx)
• Subglottic stenosis
• External compression:
• Vascular rings (e.g., double aortic arch)
• Lymph nodes, tumors
Laryngomalacia (Floppy Larynx):
•Congenital soft upper laryngeal cartilage collapses on inspiration -5 inspiratory stridor.
•Presents at N4 weeks, worsens with agitation, feeding, or supine position.
•Management: Usually none if thriving; resolves by 2 years.
Assessment of Acute Stridor:
•Look for cyanosis, oxygen saturation, level of consciousness, chest recession (mild vs sever
stridor characteristics (crying, rest, biphasic).
