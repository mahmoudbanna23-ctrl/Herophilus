Pathophysiology
•Shiga-like toxin endothelial injury (esp. kidney).
•Intravascular microthrombosis platelet consumption thrombocytopenia.
•RBCs damaged in occluded vessels microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (low Hb,
LDH, schistocytes).
•Clotting system normal (distinguishes from DIC).
•Other organs (brain, pancreas, heart) may also be involved.
Clinical Course
•Typical HI-JS (post-diarrheal):
• Good prognosis with early supportive care (dialysis if needed).
• Long-term: possible proteinuria, hypertension, progressive CKD.
•Atypical HIJS:
• Higher risk of relapse, CKD, hypertension, and mortality.
• Eculizumab (anti-C5 monoclonal antibody): major advance in treatment, but
expensive; duration of therapy uncertain.
