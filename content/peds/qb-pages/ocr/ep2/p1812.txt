27. A 4-year-old boy presents with pain in his abdomen and joints for the past 24
hours. He is afebrile. On examination, he has diffuse abdominal tenderness, but
there are no masses, no lymphadenopathy, and no hepatosplenomegaly. A
widespread purpuric rash is present over the legs and buttocks. A urine dip shows
2+ blood. Routine bloods are normal. What is the most likely diagnosis?
A. Idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura
B. Meningococcal sepsis
C. Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL)
D. Vitamin C deficiency (scurvy)
E. Henoch—Schönlein purpura
Classic HSP: purpura, abdominal pain, arthralgia, and renal
