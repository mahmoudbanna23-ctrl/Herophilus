54. A I O-month-old boy presented to the emergency room with convulsions. He
had a history of mild diarrhea for 3 days and was afebrile. He had no past history
of convulsions. Examination revealed a large head with a wide anterior
fontanelle, prominent costochondral junctions, and broad wrists. His eyes and
skin pinch were normal, and rectal temperature was 37.80C.
What investigation is most likely to reveal the cause of his convulsions?
A. CT brain
B. EEG
C. Coagulation profile
D. Serum calcium
E. Skull X-ray
Clinical signs of rickets (wide wrists, costochondral junction
prominence) suggest hypocalcemia; serum calcium is the key
investigation.
