5. A 5-year-old boy presents with marked eye puffiness and bilateral lower limb
pitting edema for a week. Two days before admission, the edema extended to the
abdominal wall and scrotum. History and clinical examination were otherwise
unremarkable. What is the most important complication of this boy's disease?
A. Hypertensive encephalopathy
B. I-lean failure
C. Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis
D. Malnutrition
This boy has manifestations suggestive Of nephrotic syndrome.
Nephrotic syndrome increases the risk for infection in general,
and spontaneous bacterial peritonitis may occur.
