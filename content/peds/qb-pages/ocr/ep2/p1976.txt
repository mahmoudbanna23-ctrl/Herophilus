A newborn with oligohydramnios, bilateral talipes, and failure to pass urine
likely has bilateral renal agenesis. This leads to Potter sequence, causing
pulmonary hypoplasia and is typically fatal.
A teenager returning from rural India with jaundice after diarrhea likely has
hepatitis A. The virus spreads via the fecal-oral route and is common in areas
with poor sanitation, preventable by vaccination.
Episodes of collapse and jerking movements triggered by anger in toddlers
are breath-holding spells. These are benign, emotion-induced events that may
cause brief hypoxia and transient loss of consciousness.
'A diabetic child with vomiting and dehydration but not in shock requires IV
normal saline. This corrects dehydration safely without immediate insulin
glucose until stability is achieved.
