2. A paediatrician is called to urgently review a 3-day-old baby on the postnatal
ward. On arrival, the baby is profoundly cyanotic, and saturations do not improve
with high-flow oxygen therapy. Examination reveals a single loud second heart
sound but no murmur. Intravenous access is secured, and the blood gas shows
severe metabolic acidosis. A chest X-ray shows increased pulmonary vasculature.
What is the most likely diagnosis?
A. Atrial septal defect
B. Transposition of the great arteries
C. Patent ductus arteriosus
D. Tetralogy of Fallot
E. Ventricular septal defect
Profound cyanosis unresponsive to oxygen with a single S2 and
increased pulmonary blood flow is classic for TGA. Immediate
recognition is essential for prostaglandin therapy.
