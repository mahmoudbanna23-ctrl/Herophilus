Whooping Cough (Pertussis)
Causative Organism:
•Bordetella pertussis produces pertussis toxin; highly contagious.
•Bordetella parapertussis — milder illness, no toxin.
Epidemiology: Endemic, with periodic epidemics.
Clinical Phases:
1.Catarrhal Phase (1 week): Coryza, mild catarrhal symptoms.
2. Paroxysmal Phase (up to 3 months):
Spasmodic cough with inspiratory whoop (infants may have apnoea instead).
• Vomiting after coughing, cyanosis or flushing, mucus discharge.
'Epistaxis, subconjunctival hemorrhage possible.
'Complications: pneumonia, seizures, bronchiectasis (uncommon).
3.Convalescent Phase: Symptoms gradually decrease but may persist for months.
Mortality: Highest in infants before completing primary vaccinations.
