34. A 4-year-old child is brought for review following a recent admission to
hospital with wheeze. He has a history of atopy, including eczema in infancy and
allergic rhinitis in the summer months. He is currently on inhaled salbutamol
PRN. His mother reports she usually gives him his inhaler on four or five days
per week. You discuss possible management options. His mother is strongly
opposed to using steroids. What is the next best step in management?
a. Refer to Respiratory Pediatrician
b. Long-acting beta-2 agonist (e.g. salmeterol)
c. Leukotriene receptor antagonist (Montelukast)
d. Oral theophylline Montelukast is an effective non-steroidal controller option,
especially in children with atopic features when steroids are
not preferred.
