17. A 5-year-old child presents with marked periorbital puffiness and pitting
edema of the lower limbs for a week. Two days prior to admission, the edema
spread to the abdominal wall and scrotum. History and clinical examination are
otherwise unremarkable.
What is the most important complication of this child 's condition?
a. Hypertensive encephalopathy
b. Heart failure
c. Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis
d. Malnutrition
This child likely has nephrotic syndrome, as suggested by
generalized edema. A serious and common complication Of
nephrotic syndrome is spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP),
due to loss Of immunoglobulins and complement in the urine.
