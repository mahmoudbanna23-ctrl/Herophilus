once the questions exist. `endpoint-p2-s12-reprint-map.md` states this in its own words under
"What this file does NOT settle".

## The rulings that are not obvious from the table

**1. The chapter follows the ANSWER, not the vignette's setting.** Same rule section 11 used. It
does real work three times here. n12 is a neonate with positive E. coli cultures and would look like
`neonatal-sepsis` from the stem alone; the printed key is **galactosaemia**, which `liv-8` teaches as
"the one metabolic cause the deck singles out", so the entry is `liver`. n40 is a list of five
haematological diseases and would look like `haematology`; what is asked is an inheritance pattern,
which is `gen-16`. n15's vignette is an accident, but what is tested is a unilateral chest
examination against four other causes of the same picture, which is a respiratory-differential
question.

**2. ⚠️ n50 and n51 are croup, and croup lives in `infection`, not in a respiratory chapter.**
Measured: the only place the corpus prints the croup organism is `inf-15`'s epiglottitis-versus-croup
table — **"Organism | Hib | Parainfluenza virus"** — which also prints the barking cough, the hoarse
voice, the coryzal prodrome and the "worse at night" course. `respiratory`'s `resp-14` carries a
one-row croup entry inside its asthma-mimic table (**"6 months – 3 years | INSPIRATORY stridor,
barking cough, hoarse | Viral, worse at night, responds to steroids"**) and names no organism. n51
asks for the organism and can be sourced only from `inf-15`; n50 asks for the expected examination
finding and both sources carry it. Both go to `infection` so that the pair stays in one chapter, and
each cites `resp-14` as the supporting source.

**3. ⚠️ n15 goes to `respiratory`, and the physical signs of an inhaled foreign body are a measured
gap.** Corpus-wide, "foreign body" appears in five places and **not one of them describes the
clinical picture**: `resp-1` lists it under aspiration as a cause of pneumonia, `resp-12` lists
"Foreign body aspiration" among the causes of infant wheeze, `resp-14` names it as an asthma mimic,
`acc-14` is button batteries (swallowed, not inhaled), and `emg-6` is the BLS airway algorithm. The
discriminating fact this question turns on — that an obstructing bronchial foreign body causes
**air trapping**, so the mediastinum and apex are pushed **away** from the affected side, which is
why an apex displaced to the RIGHT means a LEFT-sided foreign body — is nowhere in the material.
File to `respiratory` (`resp-12` is the closest shelf: the chapter that lists foreign-body
aspiration as a cause of the wheeze this child has). **Answer it from general medical knowledge and
tag that mechanical claim `not taken from the course material`.** Do not declare the gap and stop.

**4. ⚠️ n42, n47 and n39 each rest on something the corpus does not teach. Answer and tag; never
declare.**

- **n42 — the Automated Otoacoustic Emission screen.** `dp-8` is the section that exists for exactly
  this ("Hearing and vision — the two impairments screening exists to catch") and it teaches the
  hearing *dependency* and the vision *schedule* — three named vision checks — but prints **no
  hearing test and no screening programme at all**. Corpus-wide, "otoacoustic" appears in exactly one
  place: `nnj-10`, which says a kernicteric child shows **"a failed ABR with NORMAL otoacoustic
  emissions — auditory neuropathy / dyssynchrony"**. **That single line is a genuine source for this
  question's hardest distractor** — option [0] "It tests for auditory neuropathy" is false precisely
  because OAE stays normal in auditory neuropathy, which is what makes ABR, not OAE, the test that
  detects it. **⚠️ NOTE THE CHAPTER: n42 is filed to `dev-problems`, not to `normal-dev`,** because
  `dp-8` is the screening section; `nnj-10` is cited as a supporting source only. What OAE physically
  measures (a cochlear outer-hair-cell echo) is tagged `not taken from the course material`.
- **n47 — hyper-IgE syndrome.** "hyper-IgE", "hyperimmunoglobulin" and "Job syndrome" all return
  **zero hits corpus-wide.** `inf-48` names five primary immunodeficiencies (SCID, Wiskott–Aldrich,
  DiGeorge, Duncan and one more) and this is not among them. What the question actually asks is the
  inheritance pattern, and the answer is **autosomal dominant** — which is the option the bank
  prints. File to `infection-immunity` (`inf-48` is the primary-immunodeficiency section, the closest
  shelf), cite `inf-47` for the recurrent-infection work-up and `gen-14` for what autosomal dominant
  means, and tag the identification of the syndrome `not taken from the course material`.
  ⚠️ The stem says "their second male child", which reads like an X-linked pedigree and is a
  deliberate distractor; the drafting half must say why two affected males do not establish X-linkage.
- **n39 — the molecular lesion in sickle cell disease.** Sickle cell appears in eleven sections, but
  as a *disease with consequences* (stroke, haematuria, abdominal pain, a blood-film appearance, the
  asplenic risk) — **never as a molecule.** `haem-8` lists it under haemolysis; `haem-11` shows the
  film. The printed key, **"Amino acid replacement"**, is the valine-for-glutamate substitution at
  position 6 of the β-globin chain, and that fact is not in the corpus. File to `haematology`, answer
  it, tag the substitution `not taken from the course material`, and note that the four distractors
  each name a real mechanism the corpus DOES teach (enzyme deficiency = G6PD, `haem-8`; absent red
  cell precursors = red-cell aplasia, `haem-23`; membrane abnormality = spherocytosis, `haem-11`),
  which is what makes them good distractors.

**5. ⚠️ n41 sits on a contradiction inside the course material itself, and it is RECORDED, not
resolved.** The printed key is **10 months** for the mature pincer grasp. `nd-6`'s milestone spine
puts **"Immature pincer grasp developing"** at 9–10 months. `nd-8`'s fine-motor table puts
**"Transfers objects; pincer grasp"** at 6–9 months, with no immature/mature distinction. `nd-15`
lists a plain "Pincer grasp (thumb and index)" as normal in the reassurance row. So the corpus gives
the pincer grasp three different ages depending on which table is read, and only `nd-6` makes the
immature/mature split the bank's key depends on. **`answer` stays on the bank's printed option.**
The drafting half quotes `nd-6` and `nd-8` side by side in `explanation` and says plainly that the
two tables disagree — per the standing rule, a contradiction is recorded and never corrected.

**6. n28 and n41 print NO explanation box** — confirmed on the page images by the staging halves and
recorded in the reprint map. Both take the boxless marker
`Written for this bank — Pediatrics endpoint part2.pdf prints no explanation here.` as the final line
of `explanation`, and neither gets a `**The endpoint file prints this explanation:**` lead line. The
other fifty print a box and take the boxed shape.

**7. n70 is a cross-chapter question filed to `cardiac`.** The printed key is mitral stenosis, which
the corpus teaches only in `card-18` (`cardiac-acq`, rheumatic heart disease). But the question is
"which condition is associated with a **diastolic** murmur", and its four distractors — anaemia,
hyperthyroid hyperdynamic circulation, fever, aortic stenosis — are the flow murmurs and the systolic
lesion of `card-3`'s innocent/pathological checklist and `card-4`'s murmur grid. The topic is murmur
timing, so the chapter is `cardiac`; `card-18` is cited as the source for mitral stenosis itself.

**8. n71 stays in `cardiac`, not `cardiac-cyan`, even though the child is cyanosed.** Eisenmenger
physiology is taught inside `card-5` (VSD) and `card-7` (ASD) — the acyanotic lesions that turn
around — and `cardiac-cyan` (`card-10`–`card-14`) is about lesions that are cyanotic from birth. The
question is what happened to a known large VSD, so it belongs with the VSD.

**9. n80 needs `per-15` as well as `neuro-cp`.** The child is an ex-26-weeker at a chronological
3 years described as "10 months old", which is the bank writing corrected age loosely; `per-15`
("Long-term impact of preterm birth") is the section that makes spastic diplegia the preterm's
characteristic CP, and `nd-4` is where correcting for prematurity is taught. The chapter is
`neuro-cp` — the question asks for the CP type — with both cited.
⚠️ **The stem's own arithmetic is internally inconsistent** ("A 3-year-old boy … He is 10 months
old"). Transcribe it exactly as printed and note the inconsistency in `explanation`; do not repair
it and do not raise it as a question.

**10. Everything not covered by rulings 1–9 goes to the chapter that teaches the disease the key
names**, per the table.
