4.13 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which feature in a child with purpura most strongly suggests a diagnosis OTHER than immune
thrombocytopenic purpura?

 A. Recent viral illness
 B. Hepatosplenomegaly, lymphadenopathy and bone pain
 C. Petechiae over the trunk
 D. Normal haemoglobin
 E. Age 4 years

Answer: B.

Those features mean marrow infiltration — leukaemia. ITP is an isolated thrombocytopenia in an
otherwise well child; anything more requires bone marrow examination.




4.14 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A pale 10-month-old has haemoglobin 6 g/dL, a very low reticulocyte count, normal platelets and white
cells, and no hepatosplenomegaly. What does the low reticulocyte count indicate?

 A. Active haemolysis
 B. Failure of red cell production
 C. Acute blood loss
 D. Hypersplenism
 E. Iron overload

Answer: B.

The reticulocyte count is the single most useful discriminator: HIGH = haemolysis or blood loss; LOW =
reduced production (red cell aplasia, marrow failure, iron deficiency, chronic disease). This picture fits
pure red cell aplasia.
