6.16 · MANAGEMENT

An infant has a narrow complex tachycardia at 280 beats/min with poor perfusion. Vagal manoeuvres
have failed and IV access is in place. What is next?

 A. Intravenous adenosine
 B. Immediate DC shock at 4 J/kg
 C. Oral digoxin
D. Intravenous amiodarone as first line
 E. Observation

Answer: A.

Ladder: circulatory and respiratory support → vagal manoeuvres (successful in about 80%) → IV
adenosine in incrementally increasing doses → synchronised DC cardioversion 0.5–2 J/kg. Maintenance
is with flecainide or sotalol; digoxin only if there is no delta wave.




6.17 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which is a MINOR criterion in the Jones criteria for rheumatic fever?

 A. Carditis
 B. Migratory polyarthritis
 C. Prolonged P-R interval
D. Erythema marginatum
 E. Subcutaneous nodules

Answer: C.

Minor criteria are FRPP — Fever, Raised acute-phase reactants, Polyarthralgia, Prolonged P-R interval.
Diagnosis needs 2 major, or 1 major + 2 minor, PLUS evidence of preceding streptococcal infection.
Secondary prophylaxis is monthly benzathine penicillin for 10 years or until age 21, whichever is
longer.
