5.3 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which is NOT a sign of impending respiratory failure?

 A. Cyanosis or persistent grunting
 B. Reduced saturation despite oxygen
 C. Rising pCO₂
D. Exhaustion and reduced conscious level
 E. Audible wheeze

Answer: E.

Wheeze reflects airway narrowing, not failure — and a SILENT chest is the ominous sign. The four
warnings are cyanosis/grunting, desaturation despite oxygen, RISING pCO₂, and exhaustion or
confusion.




5.4 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A 2-year-old has a barking cough and inspiratory stridor that is worse at night, following two days of
coryza. He is alert with mild recession. What is the treatment?

 A. A single dose of oral dexamethasone
 B. Intravenous antibiotics
 C. Nebulised salbutamol
D. Immediate intubation
 E. Throat examination and swab

Answer: A.

Croup (laryngotracheobronchitis), 6 months–6 years, parainfluenza. Mild or moderate disease responds
to a single dose of oral dexamethasone; severe disease gets nebulised adrenaline with observation for 2–
3 hours because of rebound.
