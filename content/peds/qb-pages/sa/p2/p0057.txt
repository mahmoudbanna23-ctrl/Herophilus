8.10 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which of the following defines a COMPLEX febrile seizure?

 A. Generalised, lasting 5 minutes, single episode
 B. Focal, lasting 15 minutes or more, recurring within 24 hours, or followed by a focal deficit
 C. Occurring at a temperature above 40 °C
D. Occurring in a child under 12 months
 E. Any seizure with a fever

Answer: B.

Later epilepsy risk: 1–2% after a simple febrile seizure — the same as any child — versus 4–12% after
complex ones. Febrile seizures occur in 3% of children aged 6 months to 6 years and do not cause brain
damage.




8.11 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which statement about the management of febrile seizures is correct?

 A. Regular antiepileptic prophylaxis should be started after the second seizure
 B. Antipyretics have not been shown to prevent febrile seizures
 C. An EEG predicts recurrence
D. Lumbar puncture is never indicated
 E. Recurrence occurs in fewer than 5%

Answer: B.

30–40% have another febrile seizure. Under 18 months the signs of meningitis may be absent, so an
infection screen including lumbar puncture may be needed. Rescue buccal midazolam is provided if
seizures last more than 5 minutes.
