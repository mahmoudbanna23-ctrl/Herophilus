11.9 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

What proportion of infants who acquire hepatitis B perinatally become chronic carriers?

 A. Fewer than 10%
 B. About 50%
 C. At least 90%
D. None — perinatal infection always clears
 E. About 25%

Answer: C.

They are asymptomatic but become carriers, which is why vaccination (with immunoglobulin where
advised) at birth matters so much. 30–50% of carrier children develop chronic liver disease, about 10%
progressing to cirrhosis, with a long-term risk of hepatocellular carcinoma.




11.10 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A child's serology shows HBsAg negative, anti-HBs positive, anti-HBc positive. What does this mean?

 A. Successful vaccination only
 B. Past natural infection with recovery
 C. Acute infection
D. Chronic carrier with high infectivity
 E. No exposure to hepatitis B

Answer: B.

Anti-HBc is produced only by real infection, never by vaccination. Vaccination gives anti-HBs alone.
HBsAg = infectious now; HBeAg = highly transmissible; anti-HBc IgM = acute.
