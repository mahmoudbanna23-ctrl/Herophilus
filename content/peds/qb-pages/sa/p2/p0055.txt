8.5 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A 6-month-old has clusters of sudden flexion of the head and trunk followed by extension of the arms,
20–30 at a time, mostly on waking. He has stopped smiling at his mother. What is the diagnosis and
treatment?

 A. Colic — reassurance
 B. Infantile spasms (West syndrome) — vigabatrin or corticosteroids
 C. Benign myoclonus — no treatment
D. Reflux — anti-reflux medication
 E. Febrile seizures — antipyretics

Answer: B.

Loss of social interaction is the key marker and spasms are commonly misdiagnosed as colic. The EEG
shows hypsarrhythmia; there is a good initial response in about 70%, but relapse is common and most
develop learning disability.




8.6 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which epilepsy syndrome is characterised by brief staring episodes lasting under 30 seconds, provoked
by hyperventilation, with 3 Hz spike-and-wave on EEG?

 A. Juvenile myoclonic epilepsy
 B. Childhood absence epilepsy
 C. Rolandic epilepsy
D. Lennox–Gastaut syndrome
 E. Panayiotopoulos syndrome

Answer: B.

Onset 4–12 years, two-thirds female, 80% remit in adolescence, and it accounts for only about 2% of
childhood epilepsy. Provoke it in clinic by asking the child to blow on a windmill for 2–3 minutes.
