9.7 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which additional feature in a soiling child would point to an occult spinal abnormality?

 A. Urinary incontinence
 B. A history of an anal fissure
 C. Refusing to use the school toilet
D. A family history of constipation
 E. Recent house move

Answer: A.

Neuropathic bowel from an occult spinal lesion is usually accompanied by urinary incontinence.
Examine the back, perineal sensation, anal tone, leg reflexes and gait, and look for S2–S4 sensory loss.




9.8 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which is required for a diagnosis of Gilles de la Tourette syndrome?

 A. Coprolalia
 B. Multiple motor tics AND vocal tics present for over one year
 C. Onset before 4 years of age
D. An abnormal MRI
 E. Complete inability to suppress the tics

Answer: B.

Coprolalia occurs in only about 10%. Tics affect 1 in 10 children, more boys, onset around 8 years,
peaking at 11 years; they are worse when inactive, better when concentrating, and partially
suppressible. First-line treatment is cognitive behavioural therapy with habit reversal, with clonidine or
risperidone reserved for severe cases.
