11.3 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A 6-week-old has persistent jaundice, chalky white stools and dark urine, with hepatomegaly and
faltering growth. What is the diagnosis and why does timing matter?

 A. Breast-milk jaundice — no action needed
 B. Biliary atresia — a Kasai portoenterostomy before 60 days clears the jaundice in about 60%
 C. Neonatal hepatitis — steroids
D. Haemolysis — transfusion
 E. Hypothyroidism — thyroxine

Answer: B.

Biliary atresia occurs in 1 in 15 000; investigations show raised conjugated bilirubin and GGT with a
contracted or absent gallbladder. Without intervention, chronic liver failure and death follow within 2
years, and it is the commonest indication for paediatric liver transplantation.




11.4 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which triad of symptoms defines cholestasis?

 A. Dark urine, pale stools and pruritus
 B. Pale urine, dark stools and pruritus
 C. Vomiting, diarrhoea and fever
D. Splenomegaly, anaemia and jaundice
 E. Ascites, oedema and encephalopathy

Answer: A.

Conjugated bilirubin refluxes into blood and is excreted renally (dark urine); no stercobilinogen reaches
the gut (pale stool); bile acids deposit in skin (pruritus).
