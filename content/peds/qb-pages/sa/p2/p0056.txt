8.7 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 15-year-old drops her cereal bowl most mornings because of sudden jerks, and has had one
generalised seizure shortly after waking. Which is correct?

 A. Juvenile myoclonic epilepsy — treatment is usually effective but lifelong
 B. Childhood absence epilepsy — remission is usual
 C. Rolandic epilepsy — no treatment needed
D. Non-epileptic attack disorder
 E. Lennox–Gastaut syndrome

Answer: A.

Myoclonus shortly after waking is the signature. Learning is unimpaired, response to treatment is good,
but remission is unlikely — and carbamazepine would make it worse.




8.8 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which investigation is recommended in ALL children presenting with seizures?

 A. MRI brain
 B. 12-lead ECG
 C. Lumbar puncture
D. Metabolic screen
 E. Sleep-deprived EEG

Answer: B.

Because a missed long QT syndrome is an avoidable death. Imaging is not needed in clear-cut
generalised syndromes such as childhood absence or rolandic epilepsy.




8.9 · MANAGEMENT

A child has been convulsing for 6 minutes. There is no IV access. What is the first drug?

 A. Buccal midazolam or rectal diazepam
 B. IV phenytoin
 C. IM phenobarbitone
D. Thiopental
 E. Oral levetiracetam

Answer: A.

Treat at 5 minutes. With access: lorazepam. Without: buccal midazolam or rectal diazepam. Throughout
— airway, high-flow oxygen and "Don't Ever Forget Glucose".
