4.3 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which combination best distinguishes β-thalassaemia trait from iron deficiency?

 A. Both have low ferritin
 B. Thalassaemia trait: NORMAL or high ferritin, disproportionately low MCV for the degree of
    anaemia, raised HbA₂
 C. Iron deficiency has a raised HbA₂
 D. Thalassaemia trait responds to oral iron
 E. Both show target cells only in iron deficiency

Answer: B.

Giving iron to a child with thalassaemia trait is useless and potentially harmful. Check ferritin before
treating a microcytic anaemia, and confirm the trait with haemoglobin electrophoresis (raised HbA₂).




4.4 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A child with β-thalassaemia major on regular transfusions is at greatest long-term risk from which
complication?

 A. Iron overload affecting heart, liver and endocrine organs
 B. Vitamin C toxicity
 C. Folate excess
 D. Hypersplenism from transfusion
 E. Renal stones

Answer: A.

Regular transfusion prevents the anaemia and bone marrow expansion, but iron accumulates and causes
cardiac failure, cirrhosis, diabetes and pubertal failure. Iron chelation is therefore essential, and cardiac
disease is the usual cause of death when chelation fails.
