12.9 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A 10-year-old has headaches, vomiting, poor growth and cannot see the board at school. Examination
shows a bitemporal field defect. What is the most likely tumour?

 A. Medulloblastoma
 B. Craniopharyngioma
 C. Brainstem glioma
D. Ependymoma
 E. Glioblastoma multiforme

Answer: B.

A suprasellar tumour compressing the optic chiasm and pituitary: bitemporal hemianopia, growth
failure, diabetes insipidus, weight gain. It is not truly malignant but locally invasive — survival is good,
but at the cost of visual impairment and lifelong pituitary insufficiency.




12.10 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 3-year-old has morning vomiting, truncal ataxia and a convergent squint. Which statement is correct?

 A. A lumbar puncture should be performed immediately
 B. MRI is the investigation of choice; lumbar puncture is done later, for CSF staging, and never
   before imaging
 C. Papilloedema must be present to justify imaging
D. Seizures are the usual presenting feature of posterior fossa tumours
 E. Medulloblastoma never metastasises

Answer: B.

A posterior fossa tumour, most likely medulloblastoma — which seeds through the CSF, with up to 20%
having spinal metastases at diagnosis and a 5-year survival of about 50%. Papilloedema is a late sign
and its absence never excludes raised pressure.
