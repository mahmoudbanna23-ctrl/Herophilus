4.9 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 5-year-old has a 2-day history of widespread petechiae and bruising, 2 weeks after a viral illness. He
is otherwise well. Platelets 12 × 10⁹/L, haemoglobin and white cells normal. What is the most likely
diagnosis?

 A. Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia
 B. Immune thrombocytopenic purpura
 C. Haemophilia A
D. Henoch–Schönlein purpura
 E. Disseminated intravascular coagulation

Answer: B.

ITP: isolated thrombocytopenia in a well child after a viral illness. Most cases are self-limiting and
managed with observation; treatment is reserved for significant bleeding. Anaemia, neutropenia, bone
pain or hepatosplenomegaly would point to leukaemia and demand a marrow.




4.10 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A boy has a prolonged APTT with a normal prothrombin time and normal platelets, and bled into a
knee joint after a minor injury. What is the most likely diagnosis?

 A. Von Willebrand disease
 B. Haemophilia A or B
 C. Vitamin K deficiency
D. Immune thrombocytopenic purpura
 E. Liver failure

Answer: B.

Isolated APTT prolongation with haemarthrosis in a boy = haemophilia (factor VIII or IX deficiency),
X-linked recessive. Treatment is factor replacement; avoid intramuscular injections, aspirin and
NSAIDs. Von Willebrand disease more typically causes mucocutaneous bleeding.
