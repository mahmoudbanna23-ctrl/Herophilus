7.18 · MANAGEMENT

A child with acute kidney injury has a potassium of 7.4 mmol/L with peaked T waves on the ECG.
What is given FIRST?

 A. Calcium gluconate
 B. Salbutamol nebuliser
 C. Glucose and insulin
D. Calcium exchange resin
 E. Dialysis

Answer: A.

Calcium gluconate protects the myocardium when there are ECG changes — it does not lower the
potassium. Then salbutamol and glucose/insulin shift potassium into cells, and resin or dialysis
removes it. Prerenal failure is the commonest AKI in children; HUS and acute tubular necrosis are the
commonest renal causes.
