6.11 · MANAGEMENT

A 9-month-old with tetralogy of Fallot has a hypercyanotic spell lasting 20 minutes. Which treatment
combination is correct?

 A. Morphine, IV propranolol, IV volume and bicarbonate
 B. Nebulised salbutamol and oral prednisolone
 C. Adenosine and cardioversion
D. Furosemide and captopril
 E. Immediate arterial switch operation

Answer: A.

Spells are usually self-limiting and followed by sleep, but beyond about 15 minutes they must be treated
— untreated they cause myocardial infarction, stroke and death. Note that the murmur becomes quieter
or disappears during a spell.




6.12 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 12-year-old with a large unrepaired VSD is now cyanosed with no murmur and signs of right heart
failure. What has happened?

 A. The defect has enlarged
 B. Eisenmenger syndrome — pulmonary vascular disease has reversed the shunt
 C. Infective endocarditis
D. Rheumatic fever
 E. Aortic stenosis has developed

Answer: B.

Years of high pulmonary blood flow thicken the pulmonary arteries; the shunt reverses at about 10–15
years. Prevention — early closure of large shunts — is the only real treatment.
