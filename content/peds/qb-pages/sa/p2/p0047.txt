7.8 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A 7-month-old has a fever of 39 °C with vomiting and no focus on examination. What is the single most
important investigation?

 A. Chest X-ray
 B. Urine sample for microscopy and culture
 C. Full blood count only
 D. Abdominal ultrasound
 E. Lumbar puncture in all cases

Answer: B.

Test the urine of any infant with an unexplained fever above 38 °C. The commonest error in childhood
UTI is failing to establish the diagnosis properly in the first place — and with it the chance to prevent
renal damage.




7.9 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which urine result is diagnostic of infection regardless of colony count?

 A. Any growth from a suprapubic aspirate
 B. 10³ CFU/mL from a bag sample
 C. Leucocytes on microscopy
 D. Nitrites alone on a bag sample
 E. 10⁴ CFU/mL from a clean catch

Answer: A.

For a clean-catch or midstream sample the threshold is >10⁵ CFU/mL of a single organism (90%
probability, 95% if repeated). Urinary white cells are not reliable — they lyse on storage and occur in
other illness.
