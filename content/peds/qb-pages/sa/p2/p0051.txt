7.16 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 7-year-old has smoky brown urine, periorbital oedema, a blood pressure of 138/92 and reduced urine
output 3 weeks after a sore throat. Which result would you expect?

 A. Low C3 that returns to normal in 3–4 weeks
 B. Low C3 and low C4 persisting
 C. Normal complement with raised IgA
D. Positive ANCA
 E. Low platelets and fragmented red cells

Answer: A.

Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis — a type III immune-complex disease. Over 95% recover
completely; persistently low complement is an indication for biopsy, and both C3 and C4 low suggests
SLE.




7.17 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which statement about Henoch–Schönlein purpura is correct?

 A. The platelet count is low
 B. The purpura is palpable, distributed over the buttocks and extensor surfaces with sparing of the
   trunk, and over 80% have haematuria or proteinuria
 C. It is commoner in girls
D. The rash is the last feature to appear
 E. Renal involvement never requires follow-up

Answer: B.

HSP is the commonest childhood systemic vasculitis: age 3–10 years, twice as common in boys, peaks in
winter, often after a URTI. Watch for intussusception with abdominal pain, and follow up the urine and
blood pressure.
