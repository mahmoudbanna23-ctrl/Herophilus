6.9 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A neonate is deeply cyanosed on day 2 with NO murmur. The chest X-ray shows an "egg on side" with
a narrow mediastinum. What is the diagnosis and immediate management?

 A. Tetralogy of Fallot — beta blocker
 B. Transposition of the great arteries — prostaglandin and consideration of balloon atrial
    septostomy
 C. Coarctation — surgery
 D. Total anomalous pulmonary venous drainage — diuretics
 E. Persistent pulmonary hypertension — nitric oxide alone

Answer: B.

In TGA the circulations run in parallel, so survival depends on mixing. Prostaglandin keeps the duct
open; balloon atrial septostomy is needed in about 20%; the arterial switch is performed in the neonatal
period.




6.10 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which is NOT one of the four features of tetralogy of Fallot?

 A. Large ventricular septal defect
 B. Overriding aorta
 C. Subpulmonary stenosis
 D. Right ventricular hypertrophy
 E. Atrial septal defect

Answer: E.

The right ventricular outflow obstruction is the driver: it forces blood right to left across the VSD. The
chest X-ray shows a small boot-shaped heart with oligaemic lung fields.
