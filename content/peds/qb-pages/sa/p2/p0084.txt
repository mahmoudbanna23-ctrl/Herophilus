  13.4

  A 6-year-old has an ejection systolic murmur maximal at the upper RIGHT sternal edge radiating to the
  neck, a slow-rising pulse and a thrill over the carotids.

  H — Aortic stenosis.

  The carotid thrill is what separates it from pulmonary stenosis. Balloon valvotomy if symptomatic on
  exercise or the resting gradient exceeds 64 mmHg.




  13.5

  A cyanosed neonate on day 2 has no murmur, a loud single second sound and an "egg on side" chest X-
  ray.

  G — Transposition of the great arteries.

  Prostaglandin, balloon atrial septostomy in about 20%, then the arterial switch in the neonatal period.

  13.6

  A 7-year-old boy has smoky brown urine, puffy eyes, a blood pressure of 140/95 and reduced urine
  output. Three weeks earlier he had a sore throat. C3 is low.

  A — Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis.

  Type III immune-complex disease; the C3 returns to normal in 3–4 weeks and over 95% recover fully.




  13.7

  A 9-year-old has visible haematuria on the second day of a cold, for the third time this year.
  Complement levels are normal.


  B — IgA nephropathy.

  Haematuria coincides with the infection rather than following it by weeks, and complement is normal.
