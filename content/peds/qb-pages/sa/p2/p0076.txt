11.7 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A jaundiced newborn with poor feeding develops E. coli sepsis, and reducing substances are found in
the urine. What is the likely diagnosis, and what could mask it?

 A. Galactosaemia; a recent blood transfusion masks the red cell enzyme assay
 B. Biliary atresia; antibiotics mask the diagnosis
 C. Hypothyroidism; phototherapy masks it
D. Hepatitis B; maternal antibody masks it
 E. G6PD deficiency; a high reticulocyte count masks it

Answer: A.

Galactosaemia is confirmed by measuring galactose-1-phosphate-uridyl transferase in red cells.
Untreated it causes liver failure, cataracts and developmental delay; a galactose-free diet prevents liver
disease, but ovarian failure and learning difficulties may still occur.




11.8 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which hepatitis virus never causes chronic liver disease?

 A. Hepatitis A
 B. Hepatitis B
 C. Hepatitis C
D. Hepatitis D
 E. Hepatitis E in the immunosuppressed

Answer: A.

Hepatitis A is an RNA virus spread faecal–orally; most children recover within 2–4 weeks. Close contacts
should be vaccinated within 2 weeks of onset. Note that 30–50% of children with acute viral hepatitis do
not become jaundiced.
