8.14 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

What exactly is Gowers' sign, and when is it abnormal?

 A. Climbing up the legs with the hands; abnormal at any age
 B. The need to turn PRONE in order to rise from supine; normal until 3 years of age
 C. Waddling gait; abnormal after 18 months
 D. Toe walking; abnormal after 5 years
 E. Inability to stand on one leg; abnormal after 4 years

Answer: B.

Turning prone is the early feature; climbing up the legs is the late one. Any child still doing this after 3
years needs investigation — starting with a creatine kinase, which is markedly raised in Duchenne and
Becker muscular dystrophy.




8.15 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A 4-month-old is floppy with absent antigravity movements, absent tendon reflexes, tongue
fasciculation and a frog-like posture, but is alert and socially responsive. Where is the lesion?

 A. Cerebral cortex
 B. Anterior horn cell
 C. Basal ganglia
 D. Cerebellum
 E. Peripheral sensory nerves

Answer: B.

Floppy AND weak with absent reflexes = peripheral; fasciculation and an alert infant point to spinal
muscular atrophy type 1 (Werdnig–Hoffmann). Floppy but STRONG with brisk reflexes = central —
hypoxic–ischaemic injury, trisomy 21, Prader–Willi, hypothyroidism.
