12.3 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A child on chemotherapy has a temperature of 38.5 °C and a neutrophil count of 0.3 × 10⁹/L. What is the
correct action?

 A. Oral antibiotics and review in 24 hours
 B. Blood cultures and immediate broad-spectrum IV antibiotics
 C. Antipyretics alone
D. Wait for the culture result before treating
 E. Granulocyte transfusion first

Answer: B.

Febrile neutropenia is a medical emergency. Remember also that measles and varicella zoster are
potentially life-threatening in these children, so exposure must be reported immediately.




12.4 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which complication is precipitated by STARTING chemotherapy and risks acute renal failure?

 A. Tumour lysis syndrome
 B. SIADH
 C. Superior vena cava obstruction
D. Hypercalcaemia
 E. Cardiac tamponade

Answer: A.

Prevented by additional hydration and allopurinol — or urate oxidase when the white cell count is high
— given BEFORE chemotherapy starts.




12.5 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia accounts for what proportion of childhood leukaemia, and at what age
does it peak?

 A. 50%, peaking at 10–14 years
 B. 80%, peaking at 2–5 years
 C. 30%, peaking in infancy
D. 95%, peaking at 8–12 years
 E. 20%, peaking at 6 months

Answer: B.

Presentation is usually insidious over several weeks: pallor and lethargy, infection, bruising and
petechiae, bone pain, hepatosplenomegaly and lymphadenopathy.
