5.10 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A child with pneumonia remains febrile after 48 hours of appropriate antibiotics, with a stony dull
percussion note at the base. What is the most likely complication?

 A. Asthma
 B. Empyema
 C. Pneumothorax
D. Bronchiolitis
 E. Pulmonary embolism

Answer: B.

Persistent fever at 48 hours = effusion or empyema until imaged. Ultrasound guides drainage; a chest
drain with fibrinolytics may be required.




5.11 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 3-month-old has paroxysms of coughing followed by vomiting and episodes of apnoea. The white
cell count shows a lymphocytosis of 18 × 10⁹/L. What is the diagnosis?

 A. Bronchiolitis
 B. Pertussis
 C. Asthma
D. Cystic fibrosis
 E. Croup

Answer: B.

Pertussis: catarrhal phase, then paroxysmal coughing with whoop (often absent in infants), tussive
vomiting and apnoea, with a lymphocytosis >15 × 10⁹/L. A macrolide only alters the course if given in
the CATARRHAL phase, but is still given to reduce infectivity.
