10.8 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which set of results confirms diabetic ketoacidosis?

 A. Glucose above 11 mmol/L, blood ketones above 3.0 mmol/L, pH below 7.3 or bicarbonate below
   15 mmol/L
 B. Glucose above 7 mmol/L with glycosuria only
 C. Ketonuria with a normal pH
D. pH below 7.3 with a normal glucose
 E. Raised HbA1c alone

Answer: A.

Severity: mild pH <7.2 or bicarbonate 10–15; moderate pH 7.1–7.2 or bicarbonate 5–10; severe pH <7.1 or
bicarbonate <5.




10.9 · MANAGEMENT

Which statement about DKA management is correct?

 A. Insulin should be started immediately with the first fluid bolus
 B. Insulin is started after one hour of fluids at 0.05 units/kg/hour, and the deficit is replaced over 48
   hours
 C. Bicarbonate should be given if the pH is below 7.0
D. The IV insulin is stopped at the same moment as the first subcutaneous dose
 E. Potassium should be withheld throughout

Answer: B.

No bicarbonate. The IV insulin continues for an hour AFTER the first subcutaneous dose because
subcutaneous insulin takes time to absorb. Watch the ECG for hypokalaemia — total body potassium is
depleted even when the plasma level looks normal.
