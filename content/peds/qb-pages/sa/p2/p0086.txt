  13.12

  A 2-year-old is refused a biscuit, screams, holds his breath in expiration, turns blue and goes limp, then
  recovers within a minute.

  A — Breath-holding spell.

  Angry trigger, cries first, goes BLUE. Drug therapy is unhelpful; treating iron deficiency may reduce
  attacks.




  13.13

  A 14-year-old collapses while sprinting at school and has a brief convulsion, then recovers fully.

  D — Long QT syndrome.

  Exercise-related collapse demands a 12-lead ECG — the reason an ECG is recommended in every child
  with seizures.




  13.14

  A 7-year-old is described by her teacher as "switching off" many times a day for a few seconds with
  eyelid flickering, then carrying on as if nothing happened.


  G — Childhood absence epilepsy.

  Provoked by hyperventilation; EEG shows 3 Hz generalised spike-and-wave; 80% remit in adolescence.




  13.15

  A 5-month-old has clusters of sudden flexion of the head and trunk with extension of the arms, mostly
  on waking, and has stopped smiling and reaching.

  H — Infantile spasms (West syndrome).

  Loss of skills is the key marker; EEG shows hypsarrhythmia; treat with vigabatrin or corticosteroids.
