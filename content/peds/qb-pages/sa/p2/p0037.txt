6.5 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A superior QRS axis (mainly negative in aVF) in a child with an atrial septal defect suggests which
lesion?

 A. Secundum ASD
 B. Partial atrioventricular septal defect
 C. Sinus venosus defect only
D. Patent foramen ovale
 E. Ventricular septal defect

Answer: B.

The AVSD sits in the middle of the heart where the atrioventricular node is, displacing conduction
superiorly. There is also an apical pansystolic murmur from the regurgitant left AV valve, and treatment
is surgery at about 3 years rather than a device.




6.6 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A loud pansystolic murmur at the lower left sternal edge in a thriving 4-month-old with a quiet P2
indicates:

 A. A large VSD needing surgery
 B. A small VSD that will probably close spontaneously
 C. Pulmonary hypertension
D. An innocent murmur
 E. Aortic stenosis

Answer: B.

The louder the murmur, the smaller the hole. Small VSDs make up 80–90% of cases, close spontaneously,
and need only good dental hygiene — no restriction and no surgery.
