10.14 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Congenital hypothyroidism is detected on the newborn blood spot by measuring which analyte, and
by when must treatment start?

 A. Free T4; by 6 weeks
 B. TSH; before 2 weeks of age
 C. Thyroglobulin; by 3 months
D. Reverse T3; by 1 week
 E. TSH; by 6 months

Answer: B.

Incidence 1 in 3000; the commonest cause is thyroid agenesis, then maldescent, then dyshormonogenesis
(10%). Because screening uses TSH, it misses secondary (pituitary) hypothyroidism. Untreated it is a
preventable cause of severe learning difficulty.




10.15 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 12-year-old girl has fallen from the 50th to the 9th centile for height while gaining weight, with
constipation, dry skin and slow-relaxing reflexes. What is the most likely diagnosis?

 A. Simple obesity
 B. Acquired (autoimmune) hypothyroidism
 C. Cushing syndrome only
D. Growth hormone excess
 E. Anorexia nervosa

Answer: B.

Short and gaining weight = hypothyroid until proven otherwise; simple obesity makes children TALL.
Expect high TSH with low T4 and a delayed bone age; there is an increased risk in Down and Turner
syndrome. Also consider slipped upper femoral epiphysis as a complication.
