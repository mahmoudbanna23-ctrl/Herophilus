3.1 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

What is the commonest cytogenetic cause of Down syndrome?

 A. Robertsonian translocation
 B. Non-disjunction (trisomy 21)
 C. Mosaicism
D. Deletion of chromosome 21
 E. Uniparental disomy

Answer: B.

Non-disjunction accounts for about 94%, translocation about 5% and mosaicism about 1%. Only the
translocation form carries a substantially increased recurrence risk and requires parental karyotyping.




3.2 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Parents of a newborn with translocation Down syndrome ask about recurrence. What is the correct
advice?

 A. The recurrence risk is the same as the general population
 B. Both parents should be karyotyped, as one may carry a balanced translocation with a
   substantially higher recurrence risk
 C. Recurrence is impossible
D. Only the mother's age matters
 E. Prenatal diagnosis is not available

Answer: B.

In translocation Down syndrome, one parent may carry a balanced translocation. Karyotyping both
parents is essential for accurate counselling, and the recurrence risk can be considerable — much higher
than the age-related risk of non-disjunction.
