13.16

A 6-week-old has persistent jaundice, chalky pale stools, dark urine, hepatomegaly and faltering
growth.

A — Biliary atresia.

Conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia with raised GGT. The Kasai must be done before 60 days — 60% then
clear their jaundice.




13.17

A 3-week-old is jaundiced and constipated with a large tongue, a hoarse cry and an umbilical hernia;
the bilirubin is unconjugated.

C — Congenital hypothyroidism.

Detected by a raised TSH on the newborn blood spot; thyroxine must start before 2 weeks of age.




13.18

A 4-year-old boy becomes acutely pale and jaundiced with dark urine two days after a meal of fava
beans.


D — G6PD deficiency.

X-linked; triggers include fava beans, infection and oxidant drugs. Check the enzyme level after the acute
episode.




13.19

A 4-year-old has been pale and lethargic for 6 weeks with intermittent fever, and now has petechiae,
lymphadenopathy, mild hepatosplenomegaly and complains of leg pain.

G — Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia.

Marrow failure plus organ infiltration; bone marrow examination is definitive. Bone pain in a pale,
bruising child is never "growing pains".
