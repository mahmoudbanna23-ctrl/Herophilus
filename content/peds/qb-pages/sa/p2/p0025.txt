4.11 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which coagulation screen pattern fits vitamin K deficiency bleeding of the newborn?

 A. Prolonged PT with normal APTT and normal platelets, correcting with vitamin K
 B. Normal PT with prolonged APTT
 C. Low platelets alone
 D. Prolonged bleeding time only
 E. Normal screen

Answer: A.

Vitamin K-dependent factors are II, VII, IX and X; factor VII has the shortest half-life, so the PT rises first.
Severe deficiency prolongs both. Intramuscular vitamin K at birth prevents it, and exclusively breastfed
babies are at greatest risk.




4.12 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A child with meningococcal sepsis has bleeding from venepuncture sites, low platelets, prolonged PT
and APTT, low fibrinogen and raised D-dimers. What is the diagnosis?

 A. Immune thrombocytopenic purpura
 B. Disseminated intravascular coagulation
 C. Haemolytic uraemic syndrome
 D. Haemophilia
 E. Von Willebrand disease

Answer: B.

DIC — consumption of platelets and clotting factors with fibrinolysis. Treat the underlying cause and
support with fresh frozen plasma, cryoprecipitate and platelets. In HUS the clotting screen is
NORMAL.
