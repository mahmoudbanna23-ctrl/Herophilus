  13.8

  A 5-year-old has a palpable purpuric rash over the buttocks and extensor surfaces, painful swollen
  ankles and colicky abdominal pain. Platelets are normal and the urine shows blood and protein.

  C — Henoch–Schönlein purpura.

  The commonest childhood systemic vasculitis. Watch for intussusception, and follow the urine and blood
  pressure long term.




  13.9

  A 3-year-old is pale and passing very little urine a week after bloody diarrhoea following a farm visit.
  Platelets 40 × 10⁹/L, fragmented red cells, NORMAL coagulation screen.

  E — Haemolytic uraemic syndrome.

  Verocytotoxin-producing E. coli O157:H7. Normal clotting is what excludes DIC. Supportive care with
  early dialysis; long-term follow-up for hypertension and CKD.




  13.10

  A well 12-year-old has 2+ protein on a school dipstick. Three early-morning samples show a normal
  protein:creatinine ratio.

  G — Orthostatic proteinuria.

  Protein appears only when upright. The prognosis is excellent and no further investigation is required.
