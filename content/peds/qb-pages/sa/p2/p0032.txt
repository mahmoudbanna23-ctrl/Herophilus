5.12 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which feature best supports a diagnosis of asthma in a 6-year-old with recurrent wheeze?

 A. Wheeze only with colds and no symptoms in between
 B. Symptoms at night and in the early morning, with atopy and a family history
 C. Wet cough with clubbing
D. Faltering growth
 E. Stridor

Answer: B.

Interval symptoms, nocturnal and early-morning cough or wheeze, and atopy support asthma.
Clubbing, a wet cough and faltering growth are NEVER asthma — they mean suppurative lung disease.




5.13 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which spirometry result supports asthma?

 A. FEV₁:FVC below 80% with reversibility of FEV₁ greater than 12% after a bronchodilator
 B. A restrictive pattern with normal ratio
 C. Normal spirometry excludes asthma
D. Reduced FVC with normal FEV₁
 E. Reversibility of less than 5%

Answer: A.

And the trap: normal spirometry between attacks does NOT exclude asthma.




5.14 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which statement about asthma treatment is correct?

 A. A long-acting beta agonist may be used alone if symptoms are mild
 B. A long-acting beta agonist must NEVER be given without an inhaled corticosteroid
 C. Short-acting beta agonists are used as maintenance monotherapy
D. Leukotriene receptor antagonists are contraindicated in children
 E. Inhaler technique rarely matters

Answer: B.

LABA never without ICS; SABA never alone as maintenance. In under-fives the usual add-on is a
leukotriene receptor antagonist. Always check adherence, inhaler technique and trigger avoidance
before escalating.
