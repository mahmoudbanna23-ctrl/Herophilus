  13.21 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

  Which single examination finding, if absent, most reliably makes duct-dependent coarctation unlikely
  in a collapsed neonate?

   A. A parasternal thrill
   B. Palpable femoral pulses
   C. Hepatomegaly
   D. A loud second heart sound
   E. Central cyanosis

  Answer: B.

  Absent femoral pulses are the cardinal sign — which is why palpating them is mandatory in every
  neonatal and murmur examination.




  13.22 · MANAGEMENT

  A 6-year-old in DKA has had one hour of fluid resuscitation. Which is correct?

   A. Give sodium bicarbonate if the pH is below 7.0
   B. Start an insulin infusion at 0.05 units/kg/hour and replace the deficit over 48 hours
   C. Give the entire deficit over 12 hours
   D. Stop the insulin infusion as soon as the first subcutaneous dose is given
   E. Withhold potassium because the plasma level is normal

  Answer: B.

  No bicarbonate; the IV insulin continues for one hour after the first subcutaneous dose; and total body
  potassium is depleted even when the plasma level looks normal.
