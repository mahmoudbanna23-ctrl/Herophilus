11.1 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

By what age should neonatal jaundice have resolved, beyond which investigation is mandatory?

 A. 7 days at term
 B. 14 days at term, or 3 weeks if preterm
 C. 6 weeks in all babies
D. 3 months
 E. Only if the baby is unwell

Answer: B.

90% of physiological jaundice has resolved by 2 weeks. The first step is to split the bilirubin and ask
about stool colour.




11.2 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which cause of prolonged neonatal jaundice produces CONJUGATED hyperbilirubinaemia?

 A. Breast-milk jaundice
 B. Biliary atresia
 C. G6PD deficiency
D. Hypothyroidism
 E. Crigler–Najjar syndrome

Answer: B.

Conjugated causes: biliary atresia, choledochal cyst, neonatal hepatitis syndrome, α1-antitrypsin
deficiency, galactosaemia, tyrosinaemia, cystic fibrosis, Alagille, PFIC and TPN-associated disease. The
rest listed are unconjugated.
