7.10 · MANAGEMENT

A 10-week-old has a confirmed UTI. What is the correct management?

 A. Oral trimethoprim for 3 days at home
 B. Immediate hospital referral with IV antibiotics for at least 5–7 days, then oral prophylaxis
 C. Nitrofurantoin for 5 days
D. No antibiotics unless febrile
 E. Await the DMSA result before treating

Answer: B.

All infants under 3 months with a suspected UTI, and any seriously ill child, are referred to hospital.
Over 3 months: upper UTI 7–10 days, cystitis 5 days orally.




7.11 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which feature makes a UTI "atypical" and mandates further investigation?

 A. Fever of 38.5 °C
 B. Failure to respond to suitable antibiotics within 48 hours, or a non-E. coli organism
 C. Dysuria
D. A single previous UTI
 E. Age 4 years

Answer: B.

Atypical features: seriously ill or septicaemic, poor urine flow, abdominal or bladder mass, raised
creatinine, no response in 48 hours, non-E. coli organism. Proteus also raises the possibility of stones
because it splits urea and alkalinises urine.
