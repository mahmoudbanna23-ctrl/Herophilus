4.7 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 3-year-old with sickle cell disease has a rapidly enlarging spleen, pallor and shock with a falling
haemoglobin. What is the diagnosis?

 A. Aplastic crisis
 B. Acute splenic sequestration
 C. Vaso-occlusive crisis
D. Acute chest syndrome
 E. Haemolytic crisis

Answer: B.

Splenic sequestration — blood pools in the spleen, causing profound anaemia and hypovolaemic shock.
Treatment is urgent transfusion. An aplastic crisis (parvovirus B19) gives anaemia with a LOW
reticulocyte count and no splenomegaly.




4.8 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which measures reduce mortality in young children with sickle cell disease?

 A. Penicillin prophylaxis and pneumococcal vaccination
 B. Iron supplementation
 C. Regular aspirin
D. Long-term corticosteroids
 E. Fluid restriction

Answer: A.

Functional hyposplenism makes encapsulated organisms lethal, so daily penicillin and full vaccination
are central. Hydroxycarbamide reduces crises by raising HbF; folic acid is given; iron is not indicated
unless deficiency is proven.
