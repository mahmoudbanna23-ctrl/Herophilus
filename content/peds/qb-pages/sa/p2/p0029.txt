5.5 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which feature most strongly favours epiglottitis over croup?

 A. Barking cough
 B. Onset over hours with drooling, a muffled voice and NO cough
 C. Symptoms worse at night
 D. Preceding coryza for 2 days
 E. Loud harsh stridor

Answer: B.

Epiglottitis: hours not days, toxic, drooling, muffled voice, absent cough, SOFT stridor. Do not examine
the throat or lie the child down — call for anaesthetic, paediatric and ENT help to secure the airway first.




5.6 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 4-week-old has intermittent stridor that is worse when lying flat and when crying, but he feeds well
and is thriving. What is the most likely diagnosis?

 A. Croup
 B. Laryngomalacia
 C. Vascular ring
 D. Subglottic stenosis
 E. Inhaled foreign body

Answer: B.

Laryngomalacia — the commonest cause of persistent stridor in infancy; typically presents at about 4
weeks, worse supine, and resolves by about 2 years. Faltering growth or severe obstruction would
prompt laryngoscopy.




5.7 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which organism causes about 80% of bronchiolitis?

 A. Parainfluenza virus
 B. Respiratory syncytial virus
 C. Rhinovirus
 D. Mycoplasma pneumoniae
 E. Adenovirus

Answer: B.

RSV. Bronchiolitis affects infants 1–9 months, with coryza then a dry wheezy cough, feeding difficulty,
fine end-inspiratory crackles and, in the youngest, APNOEA.
