9.3 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A mother says her 7-week-old "cries all the time". He is thriving, examination is normal, and she is
tearful and exhausted. What is the most important element of management?

 A. Change the formula
 B. Acknowledge the distress, reassure with objective evidence, and assess maternal mood and
    support
 C. Prescribe a sedative
D. Advise leaving the baby to cry
 E. Arrange an abdominal X-ray

Answer: B.

Persistent crying is a recognised warning of parental stress and potential child maltreatment. Reassure
with 6 or more wet nappies a day and normal weight gain; changing formula usually does not help;
there is some evidence for the probiotic Limosilactobacillus reuteri in breastfed infants.




9.4 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A mother is worried that her thriving 3-year-old "eats nothing" at mealtimes. What is the single most
useful question?

 A. Which brand of food do you buy?
 B. How much does he eat BETWEEN meals?
 C. Does he take vitamins?
D. How tall is his father?
 E. Does he eat at nursery?

Answer: B.

A well-nourished child is getting food from somewhere — and not all parents count sweets and crisps as
food. Advice: avoid confrontation, keep mealtimes relaxed, use favourite foods and privileges as
rewards, and reduce snacking if needed. A food diary settles the argument.
