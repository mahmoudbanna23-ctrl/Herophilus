4.1 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

What is the commonest cause of anaemia in childhood worldwide?

 A. Thalassaemia
 B. Iron deficiency
 C. Sickle cell disease
 D. Aplastic anaemia
 E. Folate deficiency

Answer: B.

Iron deficiency — from inadequate intake (prolonged cow's milk, delayed weaning), malabsorption or
blood loss. The film shows a microcytic, hypochromic picture.




4.2 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 2-year-old has haemoglobin 8 g/dL with MCV 62 fL, low ferritin and a diet of 1.5 litres of cow's milk
a day. What is the correct management?

 A. Blood transfusion
 B. Dietary advice plus oral iron, continued for about 3 months after the haemoglobin normalises
 C. Intramuscular iron immediately
 D. Bone marrow examination
 E. Iron for 2 weeks only

Answer: B.

Oral iron is continued well beyond correction of the haemoglobin to replenish stores. Excessive cow's
milk is the classic cause — it is low in iron and displaces iron-rich foods. Transfusion is not needed in a
well child.
