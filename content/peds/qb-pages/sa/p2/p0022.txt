4.5 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A 4-year-old Egyptian boy becomes acutely pale and jaundiced with dark urine two days after eating
fava beans. Which is the most likely diagnosis?

 A. Hereditary spherocytosis
 B. G6PD deficiency
 C. Autoimmune haemolytic anaemia
 D. Iron deficiency
 E. Aplastic crisis

Answer: B.

G6PD deficiency is X-linked and triggered by fava beans, infection and oxidant drugs (sulphonamides,
quinolones, nitrofurantoin, primaquine, naphthalene mothballs). The film may show bite cells and Heinz
bodies; the enzyme level should be checked after the acute episode, as it may be falsely normal during
haemolysis when young red cells predominate.




4.6 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which statement about hereditary spherocytosis is correct?

 A. It is autosomal recessive in most families
 B. It is usually autosomal dominant and causes anaemia, jaundice, splenomegaly and gallstones
 C. Splenectomy is contraindicated
 D. Aplastic crisis is caused by rotavirus
 E. The blood film is normal

Answer: B.

Usually autosomal dominant, from red cell membrane protein defects. Parvovirus B19 causes aplastic
crisis; folic acid is given; splenectomy is considered in severe disease, with vaccination and penicillin
prophylaxis beforehand.
