10.12 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 2-year-old presents with an unexplained hypoglycaemic seizure. What must be done at the time of
the hypoglycaemia?

 A. Collect diagnostic blood (insulin, C-peptide, cortisol, growth hormone, ketones, lactate,
   ammonia, acylcarnitines) and save the first urine for organic acids
 B. Give hydrocortisone before any samples
 C. Book a fasting study for the following week
D. Only repeat the bedside glucose
 E. Arrange an MRI first

Answer: A.

The results may only be abnormal during the episode — the diagnostic opportunity is otherwise lost. A
low bedside reading must also be confirmed in the laboratory.




10.13 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A slim 3-year-old becomes hypoglycaemic after a short fast during a viral illness, with high ketones
and low insulin. What is the diagnosis?

 A. Hyperinsulinism
 B. Ketotic hypoglycaemia
 C. Glycogen storage disease
D. Congenital adrenal hyperplasia
 E. Insulinoma

Answer: B.

Ketotic hypoglycaemia — limited gluconeogenic reserve in a slim young child; prevented by regular
carbohydrate and extra glucose drinks when ill, and it resolves spontaneously in later life. Non-ketotic
hypoglycaemia with high glucose requirements suggests hyperinsulinism, treated with diazoxide.
Hepatomegaly suggests a glycogen storage disorder.
