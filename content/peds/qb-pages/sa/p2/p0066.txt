10.3 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which finding suggests type 2 rather than type 1 diabetes in a 13-year-old?

 A. Weight loss and ketosis
 B. Obesity with acanthosis nigricans, skin tags and a strong family history
 C. Presentation in spring
 D. Positive GAD antibodies
 E. Rapid onset over 2 weeks

Answer: B.

Type 2 reflects insulin resistance then β-cell failure and is commoner in some ethnic groups. Look also
for a polycystic ovary phenotype in adolescent girls.




10.4 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which two misdiagnoses classically delay recognition of diabetic ketoacidosis?

 A. Pneumonia (from the rapid breathing) and appendicitis or constipation (from the abdominal
   pain)
 B. Asthma and croup
 C. Meningitis and otitis media
 D. Urinary infection and reflux
 E. Migraine and epilepsy

Answer: A.

Which is why a bedside glucose belongs in the assessment of every child with unexplained tachypnoea,
vomiting or abdominal pain.




10.5 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

What is the pre-meal blood glucose target for a child with type 1 diabetes?

 A. 2–4 mmol/L
 B. 4–7 mmol/L
 C. 7–11 mmol/L
 D. 10–15 mmol/L
 E. Below 2.6 mmol/L

Answer: B.

Insulin requirements are 0.5–1 unit/kg/day, rising to 2 units/kg/day in puberty, after an initial
honeymoon period when requirements may be minimal.
