6.7 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which set of findings indicates a LARGE ventricular septal defect?

 A. Loud pansystolic murmur, normal chest X-ray, normal ECG
 B. Soft or absent murmur, loud P2, active precordium, apical mid-diastolic murmur, cardiomegaly
    and pulmonary oedema
 C. Continuous murmur below the left clavicle
 D. Ejection systolic murmur with a carotid thrill
 E. Fixed split second sound

Answer: B.

Treatment is diuretics, captopril and extra calories, then surgery at 3–6 months — to treat heart failure
and to prevent irreversible pulmonary vascular disease.




6.8 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Why is a persistent ductus arteriosus murmur continuous?

 A. Because flow is turbulent only in systole
 B. Because pulmonary artery pressure is lower than aortic pressure throughout the cardiac cycle
 C. Because the duct closes in diastole
 D. Because of associated aortic regurgitation
 E. Because the murmur is innocent

Answer: B.

Hence a continuous "machinery" murmur beneath the left clavicle, with bounding pulses if large.
Closure with a coil or device at about 1 year abolishes the lifelong risk of endocarditis.
