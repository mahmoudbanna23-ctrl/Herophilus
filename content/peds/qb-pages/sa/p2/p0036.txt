6.3 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which three assessments must accompany every examination for a murmur?

 A. Femoral pulses, oxygen saturations and blood pressure
 B. Chest X-ray, ECG and echocardiogram
 C. Height, weight and head circumference
D. Full blood count, CRP and blood culture
 E. Urinalysis, ECG and glucose

Answer: A.

Missing an absent femoral pulse or a low saturation is how duct-dependent lesions are missed.




6.4 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A 5-year-old has an ejection systolic murmur at the upper left sternal edge and a second heart sound
that is widely split and does not vary with respiration. What is the diagnosis?

 A. Innocent murmur
 B. Atrial septal defect
 C. Ventricular septal defect
D. Pulmonary stenosis
 E. Persistent ductus arteriosus

Answer: B.

FIXED splitting of the second sound is the signature of an ASD — the right ventricle carries a constant
extra volume load, so the respiratory variation is abolished. The murmur itself is flow across the
pulmonary valve, not across the defect.
