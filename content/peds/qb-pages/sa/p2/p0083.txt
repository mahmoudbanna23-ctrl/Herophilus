EMQ 1 · The murmur


  Options: A Secundum atrial septal defect · B Partial atrioventricular septal defect · C Small ventricular
  septal defect · D Large ventricular septal defect · E Persistent ductus arteriosus · F Tetralogy of Fallot · G
  Transposition of the great arteries · H Aortic stenosis · I Pulmonary stenosis · J Innocent murmur



  13.1

  A 4-year-old has an ejection systolic murmur at the upper left sternal edge with a widely split second
  heart sound that does not vary with respiration. The ECG shows partial right bundle branch block and
  right axis deviation.

  A — Secundum ASD.

  Fixed splitting plus right axis deviation. Device closure at 3–5 years. A superior axis with an apical
  pansystolic murmur would have made it a partial AVSD.




  13.2

  A 3-month-old is breathless on feeding with faltering growth, an active precordium, a loud second
  heart sound, an apical mid-diastolic murmur and only a very soft systolic murmur.

  D — Large VSD.

  Diuretics, captopril and extra calories, then surgery at 3–6 months to prevent pulmonary vascular disease.




  13.3

  A 2-year-old has a continuous murmur below the left clavicle and bounding pulses, but no symptoms.


  E — Persistent ductus arteriosus.

  Continuous because pulmonary pressure is below aortic pressure throughout the cycle. Coil or device
  closure at about 1 year abolishes the lifelong endocarditis risk.
