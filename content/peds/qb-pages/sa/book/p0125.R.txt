20.4
A. Advise that surgery will be needed when
older and will correct the problem
Correct. This is a glanular hypospadias. Surgery
is performed to ensure that the boy can pass
urine in a single stream in a straight line, has a
straight penile shaft and straight erection and
has a normal looking penis.
B. Advise that he is at an increased risk of other
congenital abnormalities
Hypospadias affecting the glans is not associated
with other congenital abnormalities.
C. Chromosomal analysis to check his gender
As both testes are descended and the phallus is
of normal size, chromosomal analysis is not
necessary.
D. Reassure his parents that it is a variant of
normal
Hypospadias is a congenital structural
abnormality of the urethra and meatus and is
frequently associated with ventral curvature of
the shaft of the penis and a hooded foreskin. It is
not a variant of normal.
E. Ultrasound of the bladder and kidneys
Hypospadias is not associated with renal tract
abnormalities unless very severe, so ultrasound
of the kidneys and bladder is not indicated.
