        In hypernatraemic dehydration,
        the sodium concentration in the
        extracellular compartment (ECC)
        is increased.
        Therefore, water moves by osmosis
        from the intracellular compartment
        (ICC) to the ECC.
        So, for a given degree
        of dehydration, the ECC
        loses less water than
        in other types of dehydration.
        This is why this form of dehydration
        is readily underestimated clinically.
