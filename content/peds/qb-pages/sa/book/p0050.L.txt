                   8.5.2
                   I. Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome
                   This is staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome,
                   caused by an exfoliative staphylococcal toxin
                   which causes separation of the epidermal skin
                   through the granular cell layers. This affects
                   infants and young children. They develop fever
                   and malaise, may have a purulent, crusting,
                   localized infection around the eyes, nose, and
                   mouth with subsequent widespread erythema
                   and tenderness of the skin. Areas of epidermis
                   separate on gentle pressure (Nikolsky’s sign)
                   leaving denuded areas of skin, as shown here,
                   which subsequently dry and heal without
                   scarring.
