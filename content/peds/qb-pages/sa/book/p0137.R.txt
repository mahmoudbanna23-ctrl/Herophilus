23.3
Tom is a 5-year-old boy who presents to hospital
with a recent history of bruising easily. Two
weeks ago he had an upper respiratory tract
infection which resolved spontaneously. On
examination today he is afebrile but has many,
widespread bruises with some scattered
petechiae.
Investigations reveal:
   • Hb (haemoglobin): 116 g/L
   • WBC (white blood cell count): 10.2 × 109/L
   • platelet count: 32 × 109/L
   • prothrombin time: 15 seconds (control:
     12–15 s)
   • activated partial thromboplastin time: 30
     seconds (control: 25–35 s)
   • fibrinogen: 2.5 g/L (normal: 2–4 g/L)
What is the most likely diagnosis?
Select one answer only.
A.   Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia
B.   Haemophilia A
C.   Immune thrombocytopenic purpura
D.   Non-accidental injury
E.   Vitamin D deficiency
