    A. Alopecia areata
    Usually flat, colourless, and painless. Not swollen
    and red.
    B. Cutis aplasia
    A scalp defect that presents at birth.
    C. Kerion
    Correct. Kerions are caused by infection with
    tinea capitis (scalp ringworm). Topical treatment
    is usually tried first but oral antifungals may be
    required for very severe cases.
    D. Langerhans cell histiocytosis
    Causes a seborrhoeic rash in infants. It is rare.
    E. Psoriasis
    The rash shown is localized and not scaly as in
    psoriasis.

    25.9
    A. Birth trauma
    There would be bruising and this site would be
    highly unusual with birth trauma.
    B. Burn
    A burn in that distribution and no history is very

    C. Eczema
    Eczema does not cause blistering and the infant

    D. Erythema toxicum
    Erythema toxicum is a benign pustular rash that
    migrates and requires no treatment.
    E. Infection
    Correct. Staphylococcal infection causing

    from bullous impetigo to staphylococcal scalded
    skin syndrome, as shown here.
