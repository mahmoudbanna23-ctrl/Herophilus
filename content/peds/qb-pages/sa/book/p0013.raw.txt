2.4
A. Diplegia
This affects all four limbs, the lower limbs more
than the upper limbs.

D. Hepatomegaly
Correct. In infants, hepatomegaly is an
important sign of heart failure, usually secondary
to congenital heart disease. Heart failure can
result from inadequate cardiac output (forwards
failure) or failure to pump enough blood away
from the system feeding it (backwards heart
failure). In adults, left ventricular failure
predominates, but in children either ventricle
can fail.

B. Right hemiplegia
Right hemiplegia describes the abnormal clinical
signs in the limbs, not the affected side of the
brain. Katie’s left limbs are affected, so she does
not have a right hemiplegia.

E. Ejection systolic murmur, grade II/VI, at the
left sternal edge
Ejection systolic murmur, grade II/VI, at the left
sternal edge in isolation is most likely to be an
innocent murmur.

D. Spastic quadriplegia
Spastic quadriplegia is when all four limbs are
affected, often severely.

2.5
A. Right convergent squint
Correct. It is a convergent squint because the eye
is facing inwards towards the midline. It affects
the right eye when both eyes are uncovered. The
uncovered left eye usually focuses on objects. But
when the left eye is covered, the right eye takes
up a normal position and focuses.

C. Hookworm infestation
Hookworm infestation is associated with
anaemia but not splenomegaly.

B. Right divergent squint
In a divergent squint, the eye deviates laterally
away from the midline.

D. Wilms tumour
Wilms tumour would cause enlarged kidney
rather than spleen.

C. Alternating convergent squint
The left eye would remain convergent after
uncovering.

E. Sickle cell disease
Sickle cell disease may cause anaemia and
splenomegaly, but prevalence is very low in
Indian ethnicity, whereas malaria is common in
Kenya.

D. Left convergent squint
The left eye is fixing until it is covered up, and
therefore is not squinting.
