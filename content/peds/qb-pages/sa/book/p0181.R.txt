K. Reflex asystolic syncope (reflex anoxic
seizure)
These are triggered by pain, often after a trivial
fall or knock. The child becomes pale and
subsequently may have a generalised tonic–
clonic seizure, provoked by hypoxia. This is
followed by a rapid recovery and there is no
lasting neurological insult. Children with reflex
asystolic syncope often have a first-degree
relative with a history of faints. A history of
breath holding should be sought as it is easy to     175
confuse this with breath-holding attacks.
