2.9 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which formula is indicated for an infant with cholestatic liver disease and fat malabsorption?

 A. Soya-based formula
 B. Anti-reflux (thickened) formula
 C. Medium-chain triglyceride formula
D. Standard whey-dominant formula
 E. Amino acid formula

Answer: C.

MCTs are absorbed without pancreatic enzymes or bile salts. Amino acid formula is for cow's-milk
protein allergy; soya is avoided in the first 6–12 months.




2.10 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A 13-month-old exclusively breastfed boy has an afebrile generalised seizure. He has frontal bossing,
widened wrists and delayed motor milestones. Which biochemical pattern do you expect?

 A. Low calcium, high phosphate, low PTH
 B. Low calcium, low phosphate, high alkaline phosphatase, high PTH
 C. High calcium, low phosphate, high PTH
D. Normal calcium, normal phosphate, normal alkaline phosphatase
 E. Low calcium, low phosphate, low alkaline phosphatase

Answer: B.

Nutritional rickets. Option A is hypoparathyroidism. Remember that vitamin D deficiency can present
with a hypocalcaemic seizure before any bone deformity, especially under 2 years and in adolescence.




2.11 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Where is vitamin D converted to its most active form?

 A. Skin
 B. Liver
 C. Kidney
D. Small intestine
 E. Bone

Answer: C.

Skin makes D3; the liver does 25-hydroxylation (the storage form you measure); the kidney does 1α-
hydroxylation to 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D. Hence rickets in chronic kidney disease.
