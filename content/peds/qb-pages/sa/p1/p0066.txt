EMQ 5 · Respiratory distress in the newborn


  Options: A Respiratory distress syndrome · B Transient tachypnoea of the newborn · C Meconium
  aspiration · D Persistent pulmonary hypertension · E Pneumothorax · F Congenital diaphragmatic hernia ·
  G Early-onset group B streptococcal pneumonia · H Cyanotic congenital heart disease · I Choanal atresia ·
  J Bronchopulmonary dysplasia



  10.21

  A 29-week infant develops grunting and subcostal recession within 2 hours of birth. Chest X-ray shows
  diffuse ground-glass shadowing with an air bronchogram.

  A — Respiratory distress syndrome.

  Surfactant deficiency. Antenatal steroids, CPAP and surfactant.




  10.22

  A term baby born by elective caesarean section is tachypnoeic from 2 hours of age. The chest X-ray
  shows fluid in the horizontal fissure. He settles by 24 hours.


  B — Transient tachypnoea of the newborn.

  The commonest cause of respiratory distress in term infants — but a diagnosis of exclusion; rule out
  infection first.




  10.23

  A term baby is cyanosed from 1 hour of age with only mild respiratory distress. The chest X-ray shows
  a normal-sized heart with oligaemic lung fields and no murmur is audible.

  D — Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn.

  Urgent echocardiography to exclude structural heart disease and confirm raised pulmonary pressures.
  Treat with ventilation and inhaled nitric oxide.




  10.24

  A ventilated preterm infant with RDS suddenly deteriorates with a rising oxygen requirement and
  reduced chest movement on the left.

  E — Pneumothorax.

  Occurs in up to 10% of infants ventilated for RDS. Transilluminate, then aspirate and insert a chest drain.
