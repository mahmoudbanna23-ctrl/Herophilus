9.14 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A child with sickle cell disease develops sudden severe anaemia with a LOW reticulocyte count. Which
infection is responsible?

 A. Parvovirus B19
 B. Pneumococcus
 C. Salmonella
 D. Epstein–Barr virus
 E. Cytomegalovirus

Answer: A.

Parvovirus B19 infects erythroblastoid red cell precursors, causing an aplastic crisis — hence the low
reticulocyte count. In pregnancy it can cause fetal hydrops.




9.15 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 14-year-old with fever, severe tonsillitis and cervical lymphadenopathy develops a florid
maculopapular rash after starting amoxicillin. What is the most likely explanation?

 A. Penicillin allergy — label him allergic for life
 B. Infectious mononucleosis
 C. Scarlet fever
 D. Stevens–Johnson syndrome
 E. Measles

Answer: B.

Ampicillin and amoxicillin cause a florid rash in EBV infection and should be avoided. It is not allergy.
Confirm with atypical lymphocytes and a Monospot; avoid contact sport if there is splenomegaly.




9.16 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which finding best distinguishes measles from roseola?

 A. The presence of fever
 B. The child is at his most unwell when the rash appears, with Koplik spots and conjunctivitis
 C. The rash is maculopapular
 D. The age of the child
 E. The rash lasts more than 24 hours

Answer: B.

In measles the rash spreads downward from behind the ears while the child is very unwell, with the 3 Cs
and Koplik spots. In roseola the rash appears as the fever settles in a well child.
