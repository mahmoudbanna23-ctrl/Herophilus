3.15 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 9-year-old has iron-deficiency anaemia that has not responded to 3 months of oral iron. She has
intermittent abdominal pain and is on the 9th height centile. What is the most appropriate
investigation?

 A. Faecal calprotectin
 B. IgA tissue transglutaminase antibody with total IgA
 C. Upper GI endoscopy first
 D. Abdominal ultrasound
 E. Bone marrow aspirate

Answer: B.

Unexplained iron-deficiency anaemia resistant to treatment, with short stature, is a classic modern
presentation of coeliac disease. She must stay on gluten until tested, and total IgA is measured because
IgA deficiency gives a false negative.




3.16 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 3-year-old passes 4–5 stools a day of varying consistency containing recognisable vegetables. He is
thriving and well, and coeliac serology is negative. What is the most likely diagnosis?

 A. Chronic non-specific ("toddler") diarrhoea
 B. Crohn disease
 C. Cystic fibrosis
 D. Giardiasis
 E. Lactose intolerance

Answer: A.

The commonest cause of persistent loose stools in preschool children; the child is well and thriving. Ask
about excessive fruit juice, especially apple juice. It improves with age.
