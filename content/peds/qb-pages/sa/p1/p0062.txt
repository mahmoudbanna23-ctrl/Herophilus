  10.4

  A 10-month-old has episodes of screaming with pallor and drawing up the legs, is lethargic in between,
  and has vomited. A mass is felt in the right upper quadrant.

  D — Intussusception.

  Ultrasound target sign; IV fluids then air enema (75% success).




  10.5

  A 4-month-old posset after most feeds, is thriving on the 50th centile, and is otherwise entirely well.
  The mother is exhausted by the laundry.

  A — Gastro-oesophageal reflux.

  Uncomplicated reflux: reassurance, feeding assessment, smaller more frequent feeds, thickeners. Nearly
  all resolve by 12 months.

  10.6

  A 5-year-old has a sore throat and a fine erythematous rash that feels like sandpaper, denser in the
  axillae, with pallor around the mouth and a red tongue with prominent papillae.

  E — Scarlet fever.

  Group A streptococcal exotoxin. Penicillin V for 10 days to prevent rheumatic fever and
  glomerulonephritis.




  10.7

  A 7-year-old has bright red cheeks followed by a lace-like rash on the arms and trunk. He is otherwise
  well. Several classmates are affected.

  D — Parvovirus B19.

  Erythema infectiosum (fifth disease). Warn about contact with pregnant women (fetal hydrops) and with
  children who have haemolytic anaemia (aplastic crisis).
