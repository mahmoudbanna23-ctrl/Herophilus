8.3 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which wheal size is regarded as a positive skin-prick test, and when is it read?

 A. 2 mm at 5 minutes
 B. 4 mm or more at 15 minutes
 C. 10 mm at 20 minutes
D. Any erythema at 30 minutes
 E. 5 mm at 48 hours

Answer: B.

Antihistamines must be stopped 48–72 hours beforehand. Remember that neither the wheal size nor the
IgE level predicts the severity of a reaction.




8.4 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which strategy has been shown to REDUCE the development of food allergy?

 A. Delaying peanut and egg until 3 years of age
 B. Early introduction of egg and peanut in infancy
 C. Maternal avoidance of nuts during breastfeeding
D. Routine probiotic supplementation
 E. Regular emollient use from birth

Answer: B.

Inducing immune tolerance. Probiotics, prebiotics, omega-3, vitamin D, antihistamines and moisturisers
have all been tried without success. Delaying allergens is the old, reversed advice.




8.5 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A 4-month-old formula-fed infant has loose stools, blood-streaked at times, and faltering growth. The
skin-prick test to cow's milk is negative. Symptoms resolve on an extensively hydrolysed formula and
recur on reintroduction. What is the diagnosis?

 A. Lactose intolerance
 B. IgE-mediated cow's milk allergy
 C. Non-IgE-mediated cow's milk protein allergy
D. Coeliac disease
 E. Infective colitis

Answer: C.

A negative skin-prick test excludes only the IgE-mediated form. Non-IgE disease is gastrointestinal,
delayed by hours, tests negative, and is diagnosed by elimination and reintroduction.
