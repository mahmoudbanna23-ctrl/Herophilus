9.21 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 5-year-old contact of a smear-positive adult has a Mantoux induration of 8 mm. He had BCG as a
neonate and is asymptomatic. What does this mean under UK (NICE) guidance?

 A. The test is positive because of BCG and can be ignored
 B. The test is positive; he should be assessed and given chemoprophylaxis for latent TB if active
    disease is excluded
 C. The cut-off is 15 mm in BCG-vaccinated children
D. Only the erythema should be measured
 E. He needs no follow-up as he is well

Answer: B.

≥5 mm induration is positive regardless of BCG in the UK, and only the induration is measured. Latent
TB is treated with rifampicin + isoniazid for 3 months or isoniazid alone for 6 months.




9.22 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 4-month-old born to an HIV-positive mother needs testing. Which test is appropriate?

 A. HIV antibody test
 B. HIV DNA PCR or viral load assay
 C. CD4 count alone
D. Western blot
 E. Wait until 18 months

Answer: B.

Maternal IgG crosses the placenta, so antibody tests show exposure, not infection, until 18 months.
Infection is excluded by at least two negative viral genome tests after stopping post-exposure prophylaxis,
confirmed by loss of maternal antibody after 18 months.
