3.17 · MANAGEMENT

A 4-year-old has faecal impaction with overflow soiling. What is the first-line treatment?

 A. Increase dietary fibre and fluids alone
 B. Oral macrogol in an escalating dose over 1–2 weeks
 C. Phosphate enema
D. Manual evacuation under anaesthesia
 E. Stop all laxatives because of the soiling

Answer: B.

Overflow soiling means more laxative, not less. Add a stimulant if macrogol alone fails; substitute
lactulose if macrogol is not tolerated. Dietary intervention alone has no benefit.




3.18 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 4-week-old has abdominal distension and bile-stained vomiting. He did not pass meconium until 36
hours of age. Rectal examination releases a gush of liquid stool. What confirms the diagnosis?

 A. Barium enema
 B. Anorectal manometry
 C. Suction rectal biopsy
D. Abdominal ultrasound
 E. Sweat test

Answer: C.

Hirschsprung disease — biopsy shows absent ganglion cells with large acetylcholinesterase-positive
nerve trunks. Barium and manometry only estimate the length of the aganglionic segment.
