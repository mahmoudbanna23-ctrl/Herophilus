3.7 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which feature most suggests an organic cause in a school-age child with recurrent abdominal pain?

 A. Periumbilical pain
 B. Pain relieved by defecation
 C. Pain that wakes the child at night
D. Symptoms worse before school
 E. A family history of irritable bowel syndrome

Answer: C.

Night waking and pain away from the umbilicus are red flags; so are vomiting, weight loss, blood in the
stool, dysphagia, perianal disease and delayed puberty. Fewer than 10% of recurrent abdominal pain is
organic.




3.8 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 9-year-old has episodes of severe midline abdominal pain lasting several hours, with pallor, nausea
and photophobia, and is entirely well for weeks between. His mother has migraine. What is the
diagnosis?

 A. Irritable bowel syndrome
 B. Abdominal migraine
 C. Functional dyspepsia
D. Peptic ulcer disease
 E. Recurrent appendicitis

Answer: B.

Paroxysms lasting ≥1 hour, long symptom-free intervals, migraine family history, same triggers and
relieving factors, and associated pallor, anorexia, vomiting and photophobia.
