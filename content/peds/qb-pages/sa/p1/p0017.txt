3.3 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A 9-month-old has episodes of screaming during which he goes pale and draws up his legs, with quiet
lethargy between. He has vomited twice. A sausage-shaped mass is felt in the upper abdomen. What is
the investigation of choice?

 A. Plain abdominal X-ray
 B. Abdominal ultrasound
 C. Technetium scan
D. Upper GI contrast study
 E. CT abdomen

Answer: B.

Intussusception — ultrasound shows the target/doughnut sign and is used to check response to
treatment. Note that a redcurrant jelly stool is a late sign and its absence means nothing here.




3.4 · MANAGEMENT

In the child above, air enema reduction is planned. Which is the correct sequence?

 A. Air enema first, then IV fluids if he becomes shocked
 B. IV fluid resuscitation first, with a paediatric surgeon available
 C. Immediate laparotomy in all cases
D. Oral rehydration then discharge
 E. Air enema even if there are signs of peritonitis

Answer: B.

Fluid pools in the gut, so hypovolaemic shock is common and resuscitation comes first. Success rate ≈75%;
peritonitis is a contraindication to enema reduction.
