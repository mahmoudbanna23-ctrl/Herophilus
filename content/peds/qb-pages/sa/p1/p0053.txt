9.8 · MANAGEMENT

A GP sees a febrile 3-year-old with a spreading non-blanching rash. What should be done before
transfer?

 A. Nothing — transfer immediately without treatment
 B. Give IM benzylpenicillin
 C. Give oral amoxicillin
D. Take a blood culture first
 E. Perform a lumbar puncture

Answer: B.

Any febrile child with a purpuric rash gets IM benzylpenicillin (or IV third-generation cephalosporin)
immediately and urgent transfer. Meningococcal septicaemia can kill within hours.




9.9 · MANAGEMENT

A 4-year-old has fever, seizures and altered consciousness. There are no cold sores or skin lesions.
What must be started?

 A. High-dose IV aciclovir
 B. Oral aciclovir if lesions appear
 C. Corticosteroids
D. IV immunoglobulin
 E. Antibiotics alone, pending CSF PCR

Answer: A.

All children with encephalitis are treated initially with high-dose IV aciclovir until HSV is excluded —
most affected children have no outward herpetic signs. Untreated HSV encephalitis has a mortality
above 70%. Treat for 3 weeks if confirmed, because relapses follow shorter courses.
