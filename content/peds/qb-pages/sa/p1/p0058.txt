9.19 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 2-year-old has had fever for 6 days, is markedly irritable, and has cracked red lips, non-purulent
conjunctivitis, a rash and unilateral cervical lymphadenopathy of 2 cm. What is the priority?

 A. Await desquamation to confirm the diagnosis
 B. IV immunoglobulin and aspirin, with echocardiography
 C. IV antibiotics alone
D. Oral penicillin for 10 days
 E. Discharge with antipyretics

Answer: B.

Kawasaki disease: fever ≥5 days plus 4 of the 5 features. IVIG within 10 days prevents coronary artery
aneurysms, which develop in about one-third of untreated children within 6 weeks. Peeling of the fingers
belongs to the second week — waiting for it wastes the window.




9.20 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

In Kawasaki disease, which laboratory change classically occurs in the second week?

 A. Neutropenia
 B. A rising platelet count
 C. Falling CRP with a normal ESR
D. Thrombocytopenia
 E. Eosinophilia

Answer: B.

Inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR, white cell count) are high from the start; the platelet count rises in
week 2. Echocardiography is done at diagnosis and again at 4–6 weeks.
