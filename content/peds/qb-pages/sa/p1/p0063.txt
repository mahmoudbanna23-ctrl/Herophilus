  10.8

  A 4-year-old is unwell with fever, cough, coryza and red eyes. White spots are seen on the buccal
  mucosa. Two days later a maculopapular rash starts behind the ears and spreads downward.

  A — Measles.

  Koplik spots and the 3 Cs. Complications: encephalitis (1 in 5000, mortality 15%) and SSPE (1 in 100,000, 7
  years later).




  10.9

  A 3-year-old has a febrile illness with a rapidly spreading rash of irregular non-blanching lesions
  larger than 5 mm, some with necrotic centres.

  F — Meningococcal septicaemia.

  IM benzylpenicillin immediately, then urgent transfer. Can kill a healthy child within hours.




  10.10

  A 6-year-old has crops of itchy vesicles on an erythematous base, appearing at different stages over
  several days, starting on the head and trunk.


  G — Chickenpox.

  Lesions at different stages simultaneously is the hallmark. Watch for secondary bacterial infection (fever
  settles then recurs) and VZV cerebellitis a week after the rash.

  10.11

  A term boy of a group O mother becomes jaundiced at 14 hours. Bilirubin 180 µmol/L, haemoglobin
  168 g/L, direct antiglobulin test positive. There is no hepatosplenomegaly.

  C — ABO incompatibility.

  Peaks at 12–72 hours, less severe than rhesus disease, no hepatosplenomegaly, haemoglobin normal or
  slightly low. Now more common than rhesus disease.
