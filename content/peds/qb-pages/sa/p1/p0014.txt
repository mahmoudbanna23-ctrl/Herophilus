2.12 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 3-year-old refugee has a mid-upper arm circumference of 108 mm, generalised pitting oedema, sparse
depigmented hair and refuses to eat. Which is most likely to be abnormal?

 A. Serum albumin
 B. Serum sodium (high)
 C. Haematocrit (high)
D. Serum calcium (high)
 E. Alkaline phosphatase (low)

Answer: A.

Kwashiorkor: oedema, low albumin, apathy and anorexia, with sparse depigmented hair and a flaky-
paint rash. Marasmus has normal albumin, no oedema, and an irritable hungry child. MUAC <115 mm
confirms severe acute malnutrition.




2.13 · MANAGEMENT

Which is INCORRECT in the initial management of severe acute malnutrition?

 A. Treat or prevent hypoglycaemia urgently
 B. Give broad-spectrum antibiotics even without fever
 C. Give standard oral rehydration solution freely
D. Start small, frequent F-75 feeds including overnight
 E. Delay iron until the second week

Answer: C.

Standard ORS has too much sodium and too little potassium for these children, who already have excess
total body sodium. IV fluids are used only for shock, and too much fluid causes heart failure.
