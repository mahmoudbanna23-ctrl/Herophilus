6.12 · MANAGEMENT

What is the first step in newborn resuscitation?

 A. Suction the airway
 B. Give 5 inflation breaths
 C. Dry the baby and keep it warm
 D. Start chest compressions
 E. Give adrenaline

Answer: C.

Dry, remove wet towels, keep warm (36.5–37.5 °C) and start the clock. Routine suction is avoided — it
provokes vagal bradycardia. The whole goal of newborn resuscitation is lung aeration.




6.13 · MANAGEMENT

After 5 inflation breaths and 30 seconds of effective ventilation with good chest movement, a newborn's
heart rate is 45/min. What is the next step?

 A. Repeat the inflation breaths
 B. Chest compressions at 3 : 1 with ventilation and increase oxygen to 100%
 C. Give IV sodium bicarbonate
 D. Stop resuscitation
 E. Give a 20 ml/kg fluid bolus

Answer: B.

Compressions start when the heart rate is <60/min despite effective ventilation: 90 compressions to 30
breaths (120 events/min), stopping compressions once the rate exceeds 60. Note the ratio is 3 : 1, not 15 : 2.




6.14 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which condition is screened for by the newborn blood spot test taken on day 5?

 A. Developmental dysplasia of the hip
 B. Congenital cataract
 C. Medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase (MCAD) deficiency
 D. Congenital heart disease
 E. Hearing loss

Answer: C.

The blood spot screens for congenital hypothyroidism, haemoglobinopathies, cystic fibrosis and six
inherited metabolic diseases (PKU, MCAD, maple syrup urine disease, isovaleric acidaemia, glutaric
aciduria type 1, homocystinuria). DDH and cataract are found on clinical examination.
