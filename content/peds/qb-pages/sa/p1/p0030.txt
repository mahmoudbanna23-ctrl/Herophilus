5.6 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A child with meningococcal sepsis develops widespread bruising with prolonged clotting times and a
falling platelet count. What has happened?

 A. Immune thrombocytopenia
 B. Disseminated intravascular coagulation
 C. Haemolytic uraemic syndrome
D. Vitamin K deficiency
 E. Aplastic crisis

Answer: B.

Widespread inflammation → microvascular thrombosis consuming platelets and clotting factors. Correct
with fresh frozen plasma, cryoprecipitate and platelets, guided by a haematologist.




5.7 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

An AVPU score of "P" in a sick child means:

 A. The child is alert
 B. The child is stable and can be observed on the ward
 C. The airway is at risk and will need to be maintained
D. The child has meningitis
 E. The blood glucose is normal

Answer: C.

"P" (responds only to pain) is roughly equivalent to GCS ≤8. Reduced consciousness itself destabilises the
airway — and some causes are immediately reversible, above all hypoglycaemia and opiate toxicity.




5.8 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A comatose child has a unilaterally dilated pupil. What does this suggest?

 A. Opiate toxicity
 B. Hypothermia
 C. An expanding ipsilateral lesion with uncal herniation
D. A pontine lesion
 E. Post-ictal state

Answer: C.

A neurosurgical emergency. Pinpoint fixed pupils = opiates or a pontine lesion; bilaterally fixed and
dilated = severe hypoxia, hypothermia, post-ictal or anticholinergics.
