3.11 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 2-year-old with gastroenteritis has been drinking large volumes of water. He has a seizure. Serum
sodium is 124 mmol/L. What is the mechanism?

 A. Water moves out of the brain, causing cerebral shrinkage
 B. Water moves into cells, increasing brain volume
 C. Cerebral vasospasm
D. Hypoglycaemia
 E. Hypocalcaemia from bicarbonate loss

Answer: B.

Hyponatraemic dehydration: extracellular hypotonicity drives water into cells → increased brain volume
→ seizures, plus early exaggerated peripheral signs and early shock. Hypernatraemia does the opposite
(cerebral shrinkage).




3.12 · MANAGEMENT

A child with hypernatraemic dehydration has a sodium of 162 mmol/L. What rate of correction is
appropriate?

 A. As fast as possible with 5% dextrose
 B. Reduce sodium by less than 0.5 mmol/L per hour over at least 48 hours
 C. Reduce sodium by 2 mmol/L per hour
D. Give 0.45% saline as a rapid bolus
 E. Restrict all fluids for 24 hours

Answer: B.

Rapid correction causes cerebral oedema and seizures. Use 0.9% saline (± 5% glucose) tailored to
response, with regular sodium measurement.
