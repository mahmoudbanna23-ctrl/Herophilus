7.10 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A term infant develops cyanosis soon after birth with only mild respiratory distress. The chest X-ray
shows a normal-sized heart and pulmonary oligaemia, and no murmur is heard. What is the most
appropriate next investigation?

 A. Repeat chest X-ray in 6 hours
 B. Urgent echocardiography
 C. Cranial ultrasound
D. Blood culture only
 E. Sweat test

Answer: B.

The echo distinguishes PPHN (raised pulmonary pressures, tricuspid regurgitation, right-to-left shunting)
from cyanotic congenital heart disease. PPHN is treated with ventilation, inhaled nitric oxide, and if
needed HFOV or ECMO.




7.11 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A term baby is visibly jaundiced at 10 hours of age. What is the most likely mechanism?

 A. Physiological jaundice
 B. Breast milk jaundice
 C. Haemolysis
D. Biliary atresia
 E. Congenital hypothyroidism

Answer: C.

Jaundice within 24 hours is always abnormal — rhesus or ABO incompatibility, G6PD deficiency,
spherocytosis, or congenital infection. It needs urgent investigation and close monitoring.
