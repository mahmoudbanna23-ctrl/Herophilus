7.15 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which organisms most commonly cause early-onset (<72 hours) neonatal sepsis?

 A. Coagulase-negative staphylococcus and Candida
 B. Group B streptococcus and E. coli
 C. Staphylococcus aureus and Pseudomonas
D. Neisseria meningitidis and pneumococcus
 E. Mycoplasma and Chlamydia

Answer: B.

Treated with benzylpenicillin or ampicillin plus gentamicin. Option A is the profile of late-onset
(nosocomial) sepsis.




7.16 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A newborn has a septic screen for risk factors. The first CRP at 4 hours is normal. What is the correct
interpretation?

 A. Infection is excluded; stop antibiotics now
 B. CRP takes 12–24 hours to rise, so one normal value does not exclude infection
 C. CRP is not useful in neonates at all
D. A normal CRP means the blood culture will be negative
 E. Antibiotics must continue for 7 days regardless

Answer: B.

Two consecutive normal values are strong evidence against infection, allowing antibiotics to be stopped
at 36–48 hours if cultures are negative and the baby is well.
