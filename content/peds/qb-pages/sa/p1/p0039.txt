7.3 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

What is the target oxygen saturation range for a preterm infant?

 A. 85–90%
 B. 91–95%
 C. 96–100%
D. Above 98%
 E. Any value above 88%

Answer: B.

Below 91% increases necrotising enterocolitis and death; above 95% increases retinopathy of
prematurity. Excess oxygen damages lungs, brain and eyes through free radicals.




7.4 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A ventilated preterm infant with RDS suddenly needs more oxygen. Breath sounds and chest
movement are reduced on the right. Which bedside test can confirm the diagnosis fastest?

 A. Blood gas
 B. Transillumination with a fibre-optic light
 C. Echocardiography
D. Cranial ultrasound
 E. Blood culture

Answer: B.

Pneumothorax complicates up to 10% of infants ventilated for RDS. Confirm with transillumination, lung
ultrasound or chest X-ray; treat by aspiration and chest drain.




7.5 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 28-week infant has recurrent apnoea with bradycardia. Sepsis, anaemia and electrolyte disturbance
have been excluded. Which treatment is indicated?

 A. Caffeine
 B. Phenobarbitone
 C. Aminophylline nebulisers
D. Surfactant
 E. Ibuprofen

Answer: A.

Apnoea of prematurity reflects immaturity of central respiratory control and resolves by about 32 weeks.
Caffeine helps and improves long-term outcomes.
