7.17 · MANAGEMENT

A term infant born after placental abruption is hypotonic with seizures at 3 hours of age. Which
intervention improves outcome?

 A. Prophylactic phenobarbitone
 B. Therapeutic hypothermia to 33–34 °C for 72 hours, started within 6 hours
 C. Warming to 38 °C
 D. High-dose corticosteroids
 E. Hyperventilation to a PaCO₂ of 3.5 kPa

Answer: B.

Moderate or severe HIE in an infant ≥36 weeks. Cooling works by interrupting delayed (reperfusion)
neuronal death — hence the 6-hour window. NNT 8; it reduces death and cerebral palsy but not seizures.
Hyperthermia must be avoided.




7.18 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A newborn has a scalp swelling that does not cross the suture lines and feels soft in the centre. What is
it?

 A. Caput succedaneum
 B. Cephalhaematoma
 C. Subaponeurotic (subgaleal) haemorrhage
 D. Chignon
 E. Encephalocele

Answer: B.

Bleeding below the periosteum, confined to one bone (usually parietal), resolving over weeks. Caput
crosses sutures and clears in days; a diffuse boggy swelling of the whole scalp is a subgaleal
haemorrhage and can cause hypovolaemic shock and coagulopathy.
