5.9 · MANAGEMENT

Which is NOT part of neuroprotective management of raised intracranial pressure?

 A. Head midline and tilted 20–30°
 B. Maintaining normothermia
 C. Osmotic diuretics such as mannitol
D. Deliberate hypercapnia
 E. Intubation and ventilation if GCS <9

Answer: D.

Aim for normocapnia (PaCO₂ 4.5–5.3 kPa). Hypercapnia causes cerebral vasodilatation and raises ICP
further.




5.10 · MANAGEMENT

A child has been fitting for 20 minutes and has already had two doses of benzodiazepine. Glucose is
normal. What is the next step?

 A. A third dose of benzodiazepine
 B. IV phenytoin over 20 minutes
 C. Rapid sequence induction with thiopental
D. IV dextrose
 E. Rectal paraldehyde alone and wait

Answer: B.

A maximum of two benzodiazepine doses. Phenytoin is step 3 (phenobarbitone if already on phenytoin).
Thiopental with an anaesthetist present is step 4, at about 45 minutes.




5.11 · MANAGEMENT

A 6-year-old collapses at a party with stridor, wheeze and urticaria after eating a biscuit. What is the
first treatment?

 A. IV hydrocortisone
 B. Nebulised adrenaline
 C. IM adrenaline into the anterolateral thigh
D. IV chlorphenamine
 E. IV fluid bolus

Answer: C.

Lie the child flat with legs raised and give IM adrenaline, repeated every 5 minutes. Antihistamines and
steroids are adjuncts, not treatment. Never sit a hypotensive anaphylactic patient up.
