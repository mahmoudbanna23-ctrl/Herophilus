  once, more than once, or not at all. Cover the answers, read the stem, decide before looking at the list —

EMQ 1 · The vomiting infant


  Options: A Gastro-oesophageal reflux · B Pyloric stenosis · C Malrotation with volvulus · D
  Intussusception · E Necrotising enterocolitis · F Hirschsprung disease · G Cow's-milk protein allergy · H
  Raised intracranial pressure · I Pertussis · J Urinary tract infection



  10.1

  A 5-week-old boy vomits forcefully after every feed. The vomit is milky, he is hungry immediately
  afterwards, and he has lost 300 g. Sodium 131, potassium 3.0, chloride 88 mmol/L.

  B — Pyloric stenosis.

  Non-bilious projectile vomiting at 2–8 weeks with hypochloraemic hypokalaemic alkalosis. Correct
  electrolytes before pyloromyotomy.




  10.2

  A 2-day-old term infant vomits green fluid. The abdomen is soft and he is otherwise well.


  C — Malrotation with volvulus.

  Bilious vomiting in a neonate is obstruction until proven otherwise — urgent upper GI contrast study.




  10.3

  A 27-week infant on day 11, three days into increasing formula feeds, has a distended shiny abdomen,
  bile-stained aspirates and fresh blood in the nappy.

  E — Necrotising enterocolitis.

  X-ray: intramural and portal venous gas. Stop feeds, antibiotics, parenteral nutrition; surgery for
  perforation.
