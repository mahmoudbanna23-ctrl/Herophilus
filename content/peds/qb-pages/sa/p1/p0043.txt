7.12 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which statement about ABO haemolytic disease is correct?

 A. It is less common than rhesus haemolytic disease
 B. Hepatosplenomegaly is characteristic
 C. It usually affects a group A infant of a group O mother, peaking at 12–72 hours
D. The haemoglobin is always severely reduced
 E. The direct antiglobulin test is negative

Answer: C.

ABO incompatibility is now more common than rhesus disease. Jaundice is usually less severe, there is no
hepatosplenomegaly, and the haemoglobin is normal or only slightly reduced.




7.13 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A breastfed baby is still jaundiced at 3 weeks. His stools are pale and his urine stains the nappy dark.
What is the priority?

 A. Reassure — breast milk jaundice can last 12 weeks
 B. Measure the split bilirubin urgently
 C. Start phototherapy
D. Stop breastfeeding for 48 hours
 E. Check thyroid function only

Answer: B.

Pale stools and dark urine mean conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia — biliary atresia until proven
otherwise. The prognosis of the Kasai portoenterostomy depends on early diagnosis.




7.14 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Kernicterus results from deposition of which substance, and where?

 A. Conjugated bilirubin in the liver
 B. Unconjugated bilirubin in the basal ganglia and brainstem nuclei
 C. Conjugated bilirubin in the cerebral cortex
D. Urobilinogen in the kidney
 E. Haemosiderin in the choroid plexus

Answer: B.

Unconjugated bilirubin is lipid-soluble and crosses the blood–brain barrier when albumin binding is
saturated. Acute: lethargy, opisthotonos, seizures. Survivors: choreoathetoid cerebral palsy and
sensorineural deafness.
