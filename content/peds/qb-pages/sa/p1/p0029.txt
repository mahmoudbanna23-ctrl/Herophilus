5.3 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 3-year-old with severe asthma has become quiet and drowsy, with a falling respiratory rate and a
silent chest. What does this indicate?

 A. He is improving
 B. He is exhausted and needs urgent intubation and ventilation
 C. He needs a further nebuliser and reassessment in an hour
D. He has a pneumothorax
 E. He should be discharged after observation

Answer: B.

Cyanosis, exhaustion, reduced conscious level, saturation <92% on oxygen and a rising pCO₂ define
severe respiratory failure. A quietening chest is loss of air movement, not improvement.




5.4 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which is the EARLIEST reliable sign of shock in a child?

 A. Hypotension
 B. Bradycardia
 C. Tachycardia with prolonged capillary refill
D. Absent urine output
 E. Kussmaul breathing

Answer: C.

Hypotension is LATE and means compensation is failing. Bradycardia, acidotic breathing, anuria and
confusion are all features of decompensated, potentially irreversible shock.




5.5 · MANAGEMENT

A 10 kg child in septic shock has had 40 ml/kg of fluid with limited improvement. What should be
anticipated?

 A. Discharge once the heart rate settles
 B. The need for mechanical ventilation and intensive care
 C. Stopping antibiotics as the cultures are pending
D. Switching to oral rehydration
 E. Immediate exchange transfusion

Answer: B.

Children needing more than 40 ml/kg of resuscitation fluid are likely to require ventilation. Inotropes may
also be needed because inflammatory mediators depress myocardial contractility.
