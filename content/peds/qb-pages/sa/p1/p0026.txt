4.3 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 6-year-old is pulled from cold water. He is apnoeic with fixed dilated pupils and a core temperature
of 28 °C. What is correct?

 A. Resuscitation should be stopped — the pupils are fixed
 B. Resuscitation should continue until he is rewarmed to normal body temperature
 C. Fixed pupils confirm brain death in hypothermia
D. He should be rewarmed rapidly to 39 °C
 E. Resuscitation is futile below 30 °C

Answer: B.

"Nobody is dead until they are warm and dead." Hypothermia is neuroprotective and recovery is still
possible. Up to 30% of drowning deaths can be prevented by skilled on-scene resuscitation.




4.4 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Activated charcoal is INEFFECTIVE for which of the following?

 A. Paracetamol
 B. Tricyclic antidepressants
 C. Iron
D. Aspirin
 E. Carbamazepine

Answer: C.

Charcoal does not adsorb iron, hydrocarbons or pesticides, and is only useful within 1 hour of ingestion.
Gastric lavage and induced vomiting are no longer recommended.




4.5 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Blood levels guide acute management in poisoning with all of the following EXCEPT:

 A. Paracetamol
 B. Iron
 C. Salicylate
D. Alcohol
 E. Amitriptyline

Answer: E.

Only paracetamol, iron, salicylate and alcohol levels are helpful acutely. Tricyclic poisoning is managed
on the ECG (broad QRS → sodium bicarbonate), not on a level. A urine toxicology screen does not guide
acute treatment.
