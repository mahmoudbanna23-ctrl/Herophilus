3.5 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 2-day-old term infant vomits bile-stained fluid. The abdomen is soft and he is otherwise well. What
is the most appropriate action?

 A. Observe and reassess in 12 hours
 B. Trial of thickened feeds
 C. Urgent upper gastrointestinal contrast study
D. Abdominal ultrasound for pyloric stenosis
 E. Rectal biopsy

Answer: C.

Bilious vomiting in a neonate is intestinal obstruction until proven otherwise — malrotation with
volvulus can infarct the entire midgut within hours. A soft abdomen is falsely reassuring.




3.6 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 4-year-old has painless but massive rectal bleeding that is neither bright red nor melaena, with an
acute fall in haemoglobin. Which investigation is most useful?

 A. Colonoscopy
 B. Technetium scan
 C. Barium enema
D. Faecal calprotectin
 E. Abdominal ultrasound

Answer: B.

Meckel diverticulum — technetium shows uptake by ectopic gastric mucosa in about 70%. A negative
scan does not exclude it, and laparoscopy may be needed.
