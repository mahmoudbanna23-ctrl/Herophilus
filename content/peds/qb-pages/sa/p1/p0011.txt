2.3 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A mother says her milk supply is falling. Which explanation is physiologically correct?

 A. Prolactin levels fall progressively after birth regardless of feeding
 B. Milk left in the breast accumulates feedback inhibitor of lactation, suppressing secretion
 C. Oxytocin release is inhibited by frequent suckling
 D. Supply is fixed by the third day and cannot be increased
 E. Colostrum production continues for 6 weeks

Answer: B.

FIL is the local control mechanism: an unemptied breast switches secretion off. The management is more
frequent feeding or expression, not medication.




2.4 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which is the primary anti-infective agent in breast milk?

 A. Lactoferrin
 B. Lysozyme
 C. Secretory IgA
 D. Human milk oligosaccharides
 E. Maternal leucocytes

Answer: C.

Secretory IgA coats the intestinal mucosa and prevents bacteria entering the cells. The others all contribute
but sIgA is the primary agent.




2.5 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A term breastfed baby has lost 12% of his birthweight on day 6. What is the correct action?

 A. Reassure — up to 15% loss is normal
 B. Stop breastfeeding and start formula
 C. Assess the baby for an underlying medical problem and support feeding
 D. Wait until day 14 and reassess
 E. Start phototherapy

Answer: C.

Normal loss is 7–10%, regained by 14 days. More than 10%, or failure to regain by 14 days, requires
assessment — feeding problems, dehydration, infection, or metabolic disease.
