1.8 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

An 18-month-old girl has bilateral breast enlargement that fluctuates in size. Her growth rate is normal,
there is no pubic or axillary hair, and no growth spurt. What is the most appropriate management?

 A. GnRH analogue therapy
 B. Reassurance and observation
 C. Urgent MRI of the brain
D. Bone age and pelvic ultrasound today
 E. Karyotype

Answer: B.

Premature thelarche: 6 months–2 years, may be asymmetrical and fluctuating, rarely beyond stage 3, non-
progressive and self-limiting. The absence of a growth spurt and of pubic/axillary hair distinguishes it
from true precocious puberty; investigations are not usually required.




1.9 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A 7-year-old boy has pubic hair, an enlarged penis and a growth spurt. Both testes measure 10 ml.
Which investigation is most likely to reveal the cause?

 A. 17-hydroxyprogesterone
 B. Testicular ultrasound
 C. MRI of the brain
D. Karyotype
 E. Thyroid function tests

Answer: C.

Bilateral testicular enlargement >4 ml means the pituitary is driving it — gonadotrophin-dependent
(central) precocious puberty. In boys this is pathological until proven otherwise, so image the brain
(craniopharyngioma and other tumours). Prepubertal testes would point to CAH or an adrenal tumour;
one enlarged testis to a Leydig cell tumour.
