9.5 · EXCEPT

All of the following are contraindications to lumbar puncture EXCEPT:

 A. Cardiorespiratory instability
 B. Focal neurological signs
 C. Thrombocytopenia
 D. A purpuric rash
 E. Local infection at the puncture site

Answer: D.

A purpuric rash is a reason to give antibiotics immediately, not a contraindication in itself. The
contraindications relate to coning (raised ICP, focal signs), bleeding (coagulopathy, low platelets),
instability, and local infection. Never delay antibiotics for the LP.




9.6 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which complication of bacterial meningitis is most strongly associated with pneumococcal infection?

 A. Subdural effusion
 B. Cranial nerve palsy
 C. Cerebral abscess
 D. Limb loss
 E. Purpura fulminans

Answer: A.

Confirmed on CT or MRI. Remember also that every child after bacterial meningitis needs audiological
assessment — deafness follows damage to the cochlear hair cells.




9.7 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which form of bacterial meningitis has the LOWEST risk of long-term neurological sequelae?

 A. Pneumococcal
 B. Meningococcal
 C. Haemophilus influenzae type b
 D. Group B streptococcal
 E. Listeria

Answer: B.

Most survivors of meningococcal meningitis recover fully — though the septicaemia can kill within hours
and may cause limb loss.
