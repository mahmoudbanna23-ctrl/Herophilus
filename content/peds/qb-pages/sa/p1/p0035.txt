6.6 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which congenital malformation is characteristically associated with maternal diabetes?

 A. Tracheo-oesophageal fistula
 B. Sacral agenesis
 C. Duodenal atresia
D. Diaphragmatic hernia
 E. Cleft palate

Answer: B.

Sacral agenesis (caudal regression) is rare but characteristic; cardiac malformations are the commonest.
Overall malformation risk is 6% — four times background — and is reduced by good periconceptional
control.




6.7 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 4.4 kg infant of a diabetic mother is jittery at 90 minutes of age. What is the mechanism?

 A. Hypocalcaemia from poor stores
 B. Persisting fetal hyperinsulinism after the placental glucose supply stops
 C. Glycogen storage disease
D. Adrenal insufficiency
 E. Hypothermia alone

Answer: B.

Maternal hyperglycaemia → fetal hyperglycaemia → islet cell hyperplasia and high insulin, which persists
after birth. Prevented by early feeding and close glucose monitoring for 24 hours.




6.8 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Maternal rubella infection at 6 weeks' gestation carries a risk of cataracts, deafness and congenital heart
disease of approximately:

 A. 5%
 B. 20%
 C. 50%
D. Over 80%
 E. Minimal

Answer: D.

Before 8 weeks >80%; at 13–16 weeks about 30% have impaired hearing; beyond 18 weeks the risk is
minimal. Congenital rubella is entirely preventable by immunisation.
