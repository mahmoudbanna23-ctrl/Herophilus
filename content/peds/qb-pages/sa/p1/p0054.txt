9.10 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 6-year-old has a sore throat and a rash with a sandpaper texture that spares the skin around the
mouth. His tongue is red with prominent papillae. How long should antibiotics be given?

 A. 3 days
 B. 5 days
 C. 10 days
 D. Single dose
 E. No antibiotic is needed

Answer: C.

Scarlet fever. Penicillin V shortens the illness by only about 16 hours, but the 10-day course eradicates
group A streptococcus and prevents rheumatic fever and glomerulonephritis.




9.11 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 4-year-old has a swollen red eyelid and fever. Eye movements are full and painless and visual acuity
is normal. What is the diagnosis and management?

 A. Orbital cellulitis — urgent CT and surgery
 B. Periorbital cellulitis — IV antibiotics to prevent orbital spread
 C. Allergic angioedema — antihistamines
 D. Conjunctivitis — topical chloramphenicol
 E. Chalazion — reassurance

Answer: B.

Preseptal disease. Proptosis, painful or limited eye movements, or reduced visual acuity would mean
orbital cellulitis, which needs CT/MRI and ophthalmology. Complications include meningitis, abscess and
cavernous sinus thrombosis.
