10.16

A 9-year-old girl is on the 0.4th height centile with a weight on the 25th. She is well, has no dysmorphic
features, but has had recurrent ear infections.

C — Turner syndrome.

Every short girl gets a karyotype; the phenotype can be very subtle, and recurrent otitis media is a clue.




10.17

A 7-year-old boy has dropped from the 50th to the 9th height centile over 3 years. His weight is now on
the 91st centile and his bone age is markedly delayed. He is otherwise well.

E — Hypothyroidism (an endocrine cause).

Short and overweight, crossing centiles down = endocrine. Acquired hypothyroidism is the commonest
endocrine cause and may have few symptoms. Check TSH.




10.18

A 20-month-old has abdominal distension, wasted buttocks, foul stools and irritability. She was well
until wheaning onto wheat-containing foods.


D — Coeliac disease.

The "classical" presentation at 8–24 months. Test IgA tTG plus total IgA while she is still eating gluten.




10.19

A 10-month-old has fallen two weight centiles. Length and head circumference are unaffected,
development is normal, and there are no symptoms or abnormal signs.

G — Inadequate intake.

About 95% of faltering growth is non-organic. Take a detailed feeding history and a 3-day food diary
before investigating.




10.20

A 4-year-old in foster care has been crossing centiles downwards. After 3 months in a new home he
shows dramatic catch-up growth with no medical intervention.

H — Psychosocial deprivation.

Deprivation impairs hypothalamic–pituitary function and reduces GH secretion; catch-up on removal
from the environment is diagnostic.
