M6 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which is the strongest indicator that a child with gastroenteritis is shocked rather than clinically
dehydrated?

 A. Sunken eyes
 B. Dry mucous membranes
 C. Reduced skin turgor
D. Capillary refill of 4 seconds with mottled cold peripheries
 E. Decreased urine output

Answer: D.

Shock (>10% loss) adds pale or mottled skin, cold extremities, weak pulses and prolonged capillary
refill. Hypotension appears later still.




M7 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

A 3-year-old has fever, drooling and stridor and is sitting forward refusing to lie down. What must be
avoided?

 A. Calling for senior and anaesthetic help
 B. Keeping the child calm on the parent's lap
 C. Examining the throat with a tongue depressor
D. Giving oxygen if tolerated
 E. Preparing for intubation

Answer: C.

Suspected epiglottitis — examining or distressing the child can precipitate complete airway obstruction.
This is an upper airway obstruction presentation of the seriously ill child. ADDED




M8 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which is the investigation of choice for suspected Hirschsprung disease?

 A. Barium enema
 B. Anorectal manometry
 C. Suction rectal biopsy
D. Abdominal ultrasound
 E. Plain abdominal X-ray

Answer: C.

Absent ganglion cells with large acetylcholinesterase-positive nerve trunks. Suspect it with delayed
meconium, no soiling, and an empty rectum.
