1.5 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A 13-year-old boy is short and has no pubertal development. Testicular volume is 3 ml, bone age is
delayed by 2 years, and his father did not shave until 18. His growth has followed the 2nd centile
throughout childhood. What is the most likely diagnosis?

 A. Growth hormone deficiency
 B. Familial short stature
 C. Constitutional delay of growth and puberty
D. Klinefelter syndrome
 E. Hypothyroidism

Answer: C.

Delayed bone age, delayed puberty, family history of late puberty, and a normal predicted final height. In
familial short stature the bone age equals the chronological age and puberty is on time.




1.6 · MOST COMMON

What is the most common endocrine cause of short stature?

 A. Growth hormone deficiency
 B. Cushing syndrome
 C. Acquired hypothyroidism
D. Congenital adrenal hyperplasia
 E. IGF-1 deficiency (Laron syndrome)

Answer: C.

And it often has few symptoms — which is why TSH is a standard first-line test in every short child.




1.7 · EXCEPT

All of the following are recognised patterns of premature sexual development EXCEPT:

 A. Premature thelarche
 B. Premature adrenarche
 C. Isolated premature menarche
D. Gonadotrophin-dependent precocious puberty
 E. Constitutional delay of growth and puberty

Answer: E.

CDGP is delayed, not premature, sexual development.
