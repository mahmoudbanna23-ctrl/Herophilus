3.9 · CLINICAL SCENARIO

A 6-week-old boy has forceful non-bilious vomiting after every feed and has lost weight. He is hungry
after vomiting. A wave is seen moving across the abdomen. What is the immediate priority?

 A. Pyloromyotomy today
 B. Correction of fluid and electrolyte abnormalities
 C. Start a proton-pump inhibitor
D. Upper GI contrast study
 E. Change to an anti-reflux formula

Answer: B.

Pyloric stenosis causes hypochloraemic hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis; correction may take more
than 24 hours and must precede surgery. Diagnosis is by test feed and ultrasound.




3.10 · SINGLE BEST ANSWER

Which feature would make you doubt a diagnosis of uncomplicated gastro-oesophageal reflux in a 4-
month-old?

 A. Effortless regurgitation after feeds
 B. Six or more episodes each day
 C. Weight tracking along the 50th centile
D. Faltering growth with recurrent chest infections
 E. The infant is otherwise well

Answer: D.

That is GORD — reflux causing harm. Other harm markers: oesophagitis with haematemesis or iron-
deficiency anaemia, apnoea in preterms, and Sandifer syndrome (dystonic neck posturing).
