  10.12

  A 5-week-old thriving breastfed girl remains jaundiced. Her stools are pale and putty-coloured, her
  urine is dark, and the liver edge is 3 cm below the costal margin.

  F — Biliary atresia.

  Conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia. Split the bilirubin urgently — the prognosis of the Kasai
  portoenterostomy depends on early surgery.




  10.13

  A 3-week-old breastfed baby is mildly jaundiced, feeding well and gaining weight. The split bilirubin
  is entirely unconjugated and the stools and urine are normal.

  B — Breast milk jaundice.

  Up to 15% of healthy breastfed infants; benign; fades by 12 weeks. Continue breastfeeding — but only
  after conjugated jaundice has been excluded.




  10.14

  A 6-week-old is jaundiced, constipated, feeds slowly and has a large tongue and a hoarse cry. The
  blood spot result has not been checked.

  G — Congenital hypothyroidism.

  Prolonged unconjugated jaundice may precede coarse facies, dry skin, hypotonia and constipation.
  Detected on the day 5 blood spot screen.




  10.15

  A male infant of Mediterranean parents becomes deeply jaundiced on day 2. The blood film shows
  haemolysis; the direct antiglobulin test is negative.


  E — G6PD deficiency.

  X-linked, so mainly boys; common in Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, Far Eastern and African
  populations. Coombs-negative haemolysis. Avoid precipitant drugs and foods.
