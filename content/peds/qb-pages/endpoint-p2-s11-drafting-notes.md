## ⚠️ Three measured gaps — answer and tag, never declare

Per the standing rule, a gap in the material is **answered from general medical knowledge with
`not taken from the course material` carried as a short tag on the specific outside claim** — never
a dead-end sentence, never an unfiled question. Shape to copy: `entep-throat-103`.

1. **Retinoblastoma and leukocoria — zero corpus hits** (ruling 3). Affects **n7, n16 and n22**,
   all three leukocoria / red-reflex-asymmetry vignettes. All three still file to
   `malignant-solid`, the chapter that teaches every other named childhood solid tumour, which is
   the closest shelf rather than an invented one. Note across the three that they are the same
   diagnosis reached by three different presentations (a white reflex, a new squint with an absent
   reflex, an asymmetric reflex noticed on a family photograph) — they are siblings, not reprints.
2. **Lymphoma as a disease — not taught anywhere** (ruling 4). Affects **n18**. The word appears
   corpus-wide only as ataxia telangiectasia's cancer-risk row, Duncan syndrome's EBV-driven
   lymphoma, and CAR-T's approved targets in `mal-14` — none of which teaches its presentation or
   work-up. Files to `malignant` on `mal-14`'s shelf.
3. **Li-Fraumeni syndrome — zero corpus hits** (ruling 5). One option inside **n20**.

## ⚠️ n20 — a contradiction to RECORD, never correct

n20 asks which of Down · Beckwith-Wiedemann · ataxia telangiectasia · Li-Fraumeni · Turner is
**not** associated with increased malignancy risk. Printed key: **Turner syndrome (index 4)**.

`gen-8` says the opposite for a defined subset: **"Y chromosome sequence — raises the risk of
gonadoblastoma, so gonadectomy is indicated"** — the mosaic and structural Turner karyotypes that
carry a Y-bearing cell line, about half of all Turner cases on `gen-8`'s own cytogenetics table.

**`answer` stays at 4, the bank's printed key.** Quote both the box and `gen-8` in `explanation`
and let them stand side by side. **Do not dispute the key, do not adjudicate it, and do not raise
it with the user.** The four other options are each sourced from a different chapter: Down's
leukaemia risk `mal-3`, Beckwith-Wiedemann's Wilms association `mal-22`, ataxia telangiectasia's
lymphoma/ALL risk `neuro-17`, Turner itself `gen-8`. Anchor the entry on `mal-3`, whose own table
is exactly this shape for a different list of syndromes.

## ⚠️ n15 — a second contradiction to RECORD

n15's printed box explains itself as *"Hypertension and irregular abdominal mass suggest
neuroblastoma."* `mal-22`'s own comparison table puts hypertension under **Wilms** ("Usually WELL;
haematuria, hypertension") and gives neuroblastoma no blood-pressure association at all — only
"UNWELL — bone pain, weight loss, limp" and the periorbital signs. Both tumours live in
`malignant-solid`, so the chapter is unaffected. **Record the discrepancy in `explanation`, quoting
both; `answer` does not move.**

## ⚠️ n6 — the chapter leaves oncology even though the patient is an oncology patient

A well 3-year-old on chemotherapy exposed to a sibling's rash. `mal-13`'s oncological emergencies
cover tumour lysis, febrile neutropenia, SVC syndrome, leukostasis and spinal chloroma, and never
mention varicella, rash exposure or VZIG; `inf-33` is written for exactly this contact
(**"In the immunocompromised — Aciclovir and/or VZIG in severe cases"**). Same shape as section
9's ruling 1a: the chapter follows **what is asked**, not who the patient is. Files to
`infection-vesic`. Say so in the expansion — this is the section's one chapter that will look
wrong at a glance.

## Two shared option menus — they PAIR questions and never fold them

- **n9 · n10 · n11 · n12** (pp.1240-1246): identical five-item ladder, four different keys.
  Discriminating tokens: n9 recurrent tonsillitis + pallor + bruising + hepatosplenomegaly → ALL ·
  n10 left-sided mass **not crossing the midline** → Wilms · n11 large **central** mass with weight
  loss and pallor → neuroblastoma · n12 headache, morning vomiting, diplopia → brain tumour.
- **n14 · n15 · n16 · n17** (pp.1250-1256): identical five-item investigation ladder (Full blood
  count / MRI scan / PET scan / Abdominal ultrasound / Urine catecholamines), four different keys.
  Discriminating tokens: n14 haematuria + flank mass → abdominal ultrasound · n15 irregular mass +
  hypertension → urine catecholamines · n16 new squint + absent red reflex → MRI · n17 vomiting,
  headache, abnormal eye movements → MRI.

Each of the eight expansions must name its own discriminating token and say the ladder is shared
with the other three in its menu. **Naming the token is what proves the match was to a question and
not to a template.** n16 and n17 share both the ladder *and* the key (MRI): the token is the whole
of the difference between them, so be explicit.
