Run against: `cand14-A.txt` (the candidate list), `dump14.js 1 27` (staged text for n1–n27, verbatim),
`live14.js` (live text for every candidate id named below, across both endpoint files and House),
and the native page images at `ep2-index\native\p1597.jpeg`–`p1649.jpeg` (one page per staged
entry, n1 = p.1597 through n27 = p.1649, step 2). Every page image named in a verdict below was
opened and read; n1, n2, n3, n17, n19, n23, n25, n26, n27 were not opened because no candidate for
them was called a reprint (n2/n3/n19/n23/n25/n26/n27 carried no candidate at all; n1 and n17 are
rejections and the staged dump's own transcription, already flagged "fully legible" by the stager,
was sufficient to make the call).

Fourteen of the twenty n values that carry a candidate turned out to be genuine reprints. In every
one of them, this exam ("Model Final Exam 3", section 14) is not printing an identical copy — it is
printing the same clinical case with exactly one demographic or numeric detail changed: an age (7
days → 2 days at n4; 3 → 8 years at n8; 8 → 11 months at n22; "a newborn" → "2 months" at n24), a sex
(girl → boy at n12), a lab value's *absence* (a dropped ethnicity or an added negative such as "There
is no hypertension" at n8, n12, n16, n22), or a printed numeral that looks like it should have matched
and did not (murmur grade V/VI at n18 against I/VI in the file's other printing). None of these
looked like fresh errors — they read as this exam's own house style of lightly re-skinning a
question for a later paper, and every one of them still keys the same diagnosis through the same or a
trivially reordered option list. Two entries (n5, n16) go further and pair the reused stem with a
option list that is not simply reordered but genuinely altered (a different menu style at n5, a
substituted distractor at n16); both are called out explicitly below because they do not fit any of
the six named fold shapes cleanly.

This range also carries three heavily templated families that generated most of the false pairs,
exactly as the section-13 file predicted for its own range:

1. **The "level of the neuromuscular lesion" vs "named disease" GBS pair** (n5's own family). The
   endpoint file teaches Guillain-Barré syndrome twice over: once against a named-disease menu
   (`pedep2-neu-26`, and this entry's own reused stem at n5) and once against a menu asking which
   *level* of the neuromuscular axis is affected (`pedep2-neu-51`, `pedep2-neu-6`). n5's ten-candidate
   neighbour n11 is unrelated to this family; it belongs to the next one.
2. **The five-way cerebral palsy movement-disorder ladder** — Moses / Gerald / Hassan / Alan / Ronaldo,
   `pedep2-dp-12` through `-16` and the mirrored `pedhd-devp-12` through `-16`, reused a third time as
   isolated single questions at `pedep2-mf1-64` and `pedep2-mf1-80`. n11 is the only staged entry in
   this range that is actually Moses; every other candidate in its ten-item list is a template hit on
   the shared five-option menu against a *different* child's vignette, and none of those folds.
3. **The five-item liver-disease ladder** — Jason / Raj / the southern-Asian infant / Lee,
   `pedhd-liv-6` through `-9` with the matching `pedep2-liv-*` entries — supplied n16's true target
   (`pedep2-liv-9`, the Lee/hepatitis-B case) but also three rejected template neighbours.

A fourth, smaller template pair sits at n13 (`pedep2-car-22`/`-24` and their House twins): a
different five-lesion congenital-heart-disease menu (VSD / coarctation / ASD / Tetralogy / PDA)
reused for a rubella vignette and for a plain "Robert" vignette, neither of which is the TGA case n13
actually reprints. A fifth sits at n14 (`pedep2-gen-3`, `-38`, `pedhd-gen-12`): an inheritance-pattern
menu reused for two unrelated pedigree questions, neither of which is the hyper-IgE father-and-son
case n14 actually reprints.

One correction to this brief: §3's list of six fold shapes does not have a slot for "reused stem,
substituted or restyled option menu." n5 and n16 both need one. Both are recorded as REPRINT below,
with the divergence spelled out in full rather than forced into one of the six labels.
