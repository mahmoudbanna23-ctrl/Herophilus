# Section 10 — chapter rulings

Binding for every drafting half of peds endpoint part 2 section 10, "Liver disorders",
pp.1146-1210, 20 questions. Written before any section-10 agent exists, and measured off
`app\data\theory.peds.js` (`liv-1`–`liv-15`, `nnj-1`–`nnj-12`, `haem-1`–`haem-40`, `gen-15`,
`ren-13`) and the OCR text of the section's own pages, plus a hand read of every answered page
and the opening notes block's headline slide — not assumed from chapter names. Read this with
`endpoint-p2-s09-s11-page-map.md`.

Why this file exists: the section carries the sharpest parity flip of the three pages-mapped so
far (three flips in 20 questions), and one of its 20 printed questions, on the page-map's own
account, **is not a liver question at all** — see ruling 6. Nothing here can be trusted from the
chapter name alone.

## The rulings

**1. Conjugated against unconjugated is the fork the whole section hangs on, and it is what
splits `liver` from `neonatal` — not an age boundary.** `liv-1` states it as two rules: any
infant jaundiced after 2 weeks is fractionated into total and direct bilirubin, and if the
direct fraction crosses the cholestasis threshold (>1 mg/dL where total ≤5, or >20% of total
where total >5) the jaundice is **cholestatic, always pathologic, and `liver`'s**; if not, it is
**unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia and `neonatal`'s**, causes and phototherapy thresholds
included. `nnj-2` states the same fork from the other side. **Pale stool and dark urine are the
bedside proxy for the lab result** (`liv-6`): a vignette with pale stool, dark urine or a
measured conjugated fraction over the threshold is `liver`; a vignette with normal stool and
urine colour is `neonatal`. Measured directly against Q2 (p.1169, pale stool + bruising +
hepatomegaly → `liver`) and Q10 (p.1186, total 12 mg/dL / direct 9 mg/dL, 75% conjugated →
`liver`) on the cholestatic side, and Q3 (p.1171, explicitly normal stool and urine colour →
`neonatal`) on the other. **`nnj-2` itself carries the umbilical-hernia sign, tagged there as
deferred from `endocrine`'s congenital-hypothyroidism differential** — Q3's dry skin and
umbilical hernia are that exact distractor shape, printed against a jaundice-cause option list
with no hypothyroidism option; the fork still resolves it to `neonatal` on stool/urine colour
alone, whatever the correct answer among the printed options.

**2. Where haemolytic jaundice goes depends on what the question is actually asking, not on
whether ABO or Rh appears.** `nnj-5` owns ABO incompatibility and Rh isoimmunisation as **causes
of neonatal jaundice** — a question asking what is causing the jaundice, or how fast the
bilirubin is rising under phototherapy, is `neonatal`. `haem-9` teaches the same two entities
again, but as **causes of anaemia in the newborn** ("destruction: HDN — Rh, ABO") — a question
whose own focus has moved past the jaundice to the hospital's next move on a falling
haemoglobin is `haematology`. Measured against the section's own pair: Q14 (p.1194, bilirubin
rising on phototherapy, "most likely cause of the jaundice" → `neonatal`, `nnj-5`) against Q13
(p.1192, jaundiced at 24 hours weeks ago, presents now with Hb fallen from 15 to 10.2 g/dL, no
bilirubin in the stem at all, "what is the next step in management" → `haematology`, `haem-9`).
Q15 (p.1196, "most important cause of jaundice in the first 24 hours") is the same shape as Q14
and stays `neonatal`.

**3. Hepatitis B vaccination splits on whether the question is the routine schedule or the
exposed-newborn pathway.** The Egyptian compulsory schedule, hepatitis B inside the pentavalent
at 2/4/6 months, is `infection-vaccine`'s own content (`inf-51`) and is not repeated in `liver`
or `liver-hep`. But `liv-13` is written specifically for **"the exposed newborn — what the baby
of an HBsAg-positive mother gets"** — HBIG and vaccine at birth, the additional birth-dose
pathway — and says explicitly that the routine schedule is `infection-vaccine`'s and is *"not
repeated here."* A question about an HBsAg-positive mother's newborn is `liver-hep`, not
`infection-vaccine`. Measured on Q4 (pp.1173-1175): mother HBsAg-positive/HBeAg-negative,
advice on immunising the baby and family — this is `liv-13`'s own vignette shape, so `liver-hep`.

**4. Chronic liver disease's complications, coagulopathy included, stay `liver-hep` — they do
not move to `haem-bleeding`.** `liv-15` lists coagulopathy as one of chronic liver disease's own
named complications, alongside portal hypertension (oesophageal varices, ascites, spontaneous
bacterial peritonitis), hepatic encephalopathy, hepatocellular carcinoma and hepato-renal
syndrome — the same list the opening notes print at p.1162-1164 under "Complications of Chronic
Liver Failure." `haem-bleeding` teaches the primary haemostatic disorders (ITP, the haemophilias,
Henoch-Schönlein purpura) and none of it is liver-caused; nothing in this section's 20 questions
needs it. Measured on Q9 (p.1184): jaundiced, malnourished, splenomegaly and blood-stained
vomiting in a boy with a chronic-hepatitis-B history — variceal bleeding on a background of
portal hypertension, `liver-hep`'s own complication, not `haem-bleeding`'s.

**5. Galactosaemia is `liver`, not `genetics`, unless the question is about the inheritance
itself.** `liv-8` is a dedicated section — "the one metabolic cause the deck singles out,"
naming the five organs it damages and the absolute breastfeeding contraindication. `gen-15`
names galactosaemia exactly once, inside a six-item list of autosomal recessive disorders
(alongside cystic fibrosis, phenylketonuria, thalassaemia), and teaches nothing else of the
disease. A galactosaemia vignette — jaundice, vomiting, failure to thrive, cataracts, as in Q8
(p.1182) — is `liver`; only a question asking about the AR pedigree itself would be `genetics`,
and none of this section's 20 does.

**6. ⚠️ p.1200-1201 is not a liver question at all — it is nephrotic syndrome, and it needs
`renal`, which is not on the candidate list.** The vignette (periorbital puffiness, pitting
oedema spreading to the abdominal wall and scrotum, "most important complication") and its
printed answer (spontaneous bacterial peritonitis, "due to loss of immunoglobulins and
complement in the urine") match `ren-13` ("Proteinuria and nephrotic syndrome") almost word for
word: gross oedema — periorbital, ascites, scrotal, pitting — and infection as a named
complication of the urinary protein loss. Nothing in `liver`, `liver-hep`, `neonatal` or
`haematology` teaches this. **⚠️ SBP is not a liver-specific sign here** — the section's own
notes teach SBP as a complication of chronic liver failure too (p.1162, `liv-15`), which is
exactly what makes this page easy to mis-file by keyword alone; the whole vignette, not the
one abbreviation, is what settles it. File to `renal` (`ren-13`) and flag it: the book's own
page order, not a transcription error, put a renal question inside the liver section.

**7. ⚠️ Measured gap: Wilson's disease is not taught as a liver topic anywhere in the corpus.**
Q5 (p.1176: school decline, confusion, unsteady gait, an eye finding, "select the most likely
diagnosis" → Wilson's disease) is not covered by `liv-15` or anywhere else in `liver-hep`. The
one and only mention of Wilson disease in the whole peds corpus is in the neurology deck's
basal-ganglia causes-of-chorea table, as a cause of a movement disorder — not as a liver disease.
File it to `liver-hep` anyway: the vignette is functionally a chronic-liver-disease case (a
genetic disorder with hepatic and neuropsychiatric/ocular involvement), which is `liv-15`'s own
domain even though `liv-15`'s text never names it. Tag the explanation `not taken from the
course material` for the disease mechanism and the eye sign, per the project's gap-filling rule
— do not leave the question unfiled for want of a source.

## What this section does NOT contain

Measured, not assumed:

- **Rickets is mentioned twice** (p.1148, p.1151) **and never as its own topic** — both mentions
  are a single bullet inside a list of advanced/chronic-cholestasis features ("hepatosplenomegaly,
  ascites, encephalopathy, rickets, easy bruising, peripheral neuropathy") or PFIC's presentation.
  No hypocalcaemia and no tetany appear anywhere — zero OCR hits for either across all 65 pages.
  `nutrition-vit` is not a candidate chapter here.
- **No leukaemia, no meningitis or febrile-child content, no shock/resuscitation content** —
  zero OCR hits for any of them across the section, teaching notes included. `malignant`,
  `infection` and `emergencies` are not candidate chapters here.
- **`gastroenterology` and `gi-abdopain` are not needed either.** "Vomiting" and "abdominal
  pain" surface only as symptom words inside hepatitis and galactosaemia vignettes and inside
  one hepatitis-facts option list (Q20) — no question in the section is actually about reflux or
  about abdominal pain as its own topic.
- **`haem-bleeding` is not needed** (ruling 4) — every bleeding-and-clotting mention in the
  section's 20 questions is chronic-liver-disease coagulopathy, which stays on `liver-hep`'s own
  shelf.

If a half finds content on one of these five topics on a page this file did not read
individually, that disagreement is the finding and should be reported rather than quietly filed.
