## The rulings

**1. ⚠️ The section's central collision is `liver` against `neonatal`, and it is the same shape that
misfiled three diseases in section 5.** Both chapters teach jaundice in a small infant and both open
on the conjugated/unconjugated fork. The line:

- **`liver` owns the CONJUGATED baby** — cholestasis, pale stools, dark urine, biliary atresia,
  galactosaemia, the diagnostic run, the Kasai.
- **`neonatal` owns the UNCONJUGATED baby** — physiologic against pathologic, ABO and Rh, G6PD and
  spherocytosis, the breastfeeding jaundices, bilirubin toxicity, and **every question about
  phototherapy or exchange transfusion**, which `nnj-11` and `nnj-12` are written for and which the
  liver chapters mention once in passing.

Measured: phototherapy appears on pp.1194-1195, G6PD on pp.1192-1193, Rh on p.1165. Those pages are
`neonatal` even though they sit inside a section the book calls Liver disorders. **File by what the
question teaches, never by which section of the book prints it.**

**2. Biliary atresia is `liver`.** `liv-5` is written for it and `liv-7` and `liv-9` carry its
work-up and operation; `neonatal`/`nnj-2` names it once, inside the fork. It is the section's densest
topic — measured on eighteen pages.

**3. Galactosaemia is `liver`,** `liv-8`, the one metabolic cause the deck singles out. Measured on
pp.1171-1172 and pp.1178-1185.

**4. Hepatitis of every letter is `liver-hep`.** `liv-10` through `liv-14`. A question about
vaccinating against hepatitis B, or about the immunoglobulin given to the newborn of an
HBsAg-positive mother, is still `liver-hep` — `liv-13` is written for exactly that newborn.
`infection-vaccine`/`inf-50` and `inf-51` mention HBV vaccine only as items in a schedule, so they
take a hepatitis question only when the question is about the schedule itself.

**5. Cirrhosis, portal hypertension and transplantation are `liver-hep`,** `liv-15`. Measured on
pp.1158-1165 and pp.1163-1164.

**6. ⚠️ Wilson disease has no liver chapter.** Measured: the only place any theory chapter mentions
it is `neuro-nm`/`neuro-16`, where it is taught as a movement disorder. It appears on pp.1176-1177 of
this section. If the question asks about the liver presentation, file it `liver-hep` (chronic liver
disease, `liv-15`) and tag the specifically hepatic content `not taken from the course material`. If
it asks about the neurological presentation or the Kayser-Fleischer ring, file it `neuro-nm`. Either
way the gap is **answered and tagged, never declared**.

**7. Everything else goes to the chapter that teaches the disease asked about.** If a question
genuinely straddles `liver` and `neonatal`, assign the primary and say so in `explanation`.

## What this section does NOT contain

Measured, not assumed: **zero OCR hits for "kernicterus" and zero for "spherocyt" across
pp.1146-1210.** Both are `neonatal` topics that a reader might expect a jaundice-heavy section to
raise, and neither is here. If a half finds either on its own pages, that disagreement with this file
is the finding and should be reported.
