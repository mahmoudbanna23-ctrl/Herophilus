Definition
• They refer to any child whose development is delayed or disordered
  leading to difference with their peers which increases as the child gets
  older.
•Delay : implies slow acquisition of all skills (global delay) or of one
 particular field or area of skill (specific delay)
•Disorder : implies maldevelopment of a skill.




                                   dr. Hanan Azouz
During infancy and early childhood, problems often present at
an age when a specific area of development is most rapid and
prominent
▪ Motor problems: occur during the first 18 months of
 age
▪ Speech and language problems occur between 18
 months and 3 years
▪ Social and communication disorders occur between
 2–4 years of age).

Etiology
Abnormal development may be caused by:
• Neurodevelopmental disorders
• Chronic ill health
• If the child’s physical or psychological needs are not met.
This may be due to prenatal , perinatal or postnatal causes. However
etiology may be unknown.

           Autistic Spectrum Disorders
• This developmental disorder is manifested as a behavior syndrome of
  neurological dysfunction. This is usually between 2–4 years of age
  when language and social skills normally rapidly expand.

  difficulties and imposition of routine and repetitive behavior,
  however, according to the Diagnostic and Statistical manual in the US
  (DSM5) it is a continuum of mild to severe impairment in two areas:

     Inattentive presentation:
1.   Makes careless mistakes
2.   Difficulty sustaining attention
3.   Does not seem to listen
4.   Does not follow through instructions, fails to finish
     work
5.   Difficulty organizing tasks and activities
6.   Reluctant to engage in tasks which require sustained
     mental activity
7.   Loses equipment necessary for activities
8.   Distracted by extraneous stimuli
9.   Forgetful in daily activities
                           dr. Hanan Azouz
     Hyperactive Impulsive
     presentation
1.    Fidgets with hands or feet or squirms in seat
2.    Leaves seat in classroom or in other situations where remaining
      seated is expected
3.    Runs about excessively in situations in which it is inappropriate
4.    Difficulty in playing quietly
5.    Often ‘on the go’ as if driven by a motor
6.    Talks excessively
7.     Blurts out answers before questions have been completed.
8.    Has difficulty awaiting turn.
9.    Interrupts or intrudes on others e.g. pushes into conversations or
      games.

To establish diagnosis before age of 12
years:
⚫ the first step
  ⚫ abnormal and persistent manifestations, from at least six symptoms continuing
    over a minimum six months
  ⚫ these symptoms were present before 12 years of age
  ⚫ these symptoms must be present in two settings, usually at school (or work, if
    an adult) and at home
  ⚫ clear evidence that the symptoms interfere with, or reduce the quality of,
    social, academic, or occupational functioning.
⚫ the second step: comorbid disorders
