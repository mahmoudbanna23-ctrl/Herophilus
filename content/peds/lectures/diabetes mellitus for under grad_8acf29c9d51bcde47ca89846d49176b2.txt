             Diabetes Mellitus in children & adolescents



Introduction / Overview
      Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by
       hyperglycemia due to impaired insulin secretion, action, or both.
      It includes multiple subtypes, each with different causes, treatments, and
       outcomes, making proper classification essential.
      In children, type 1 diabetes mellitus is the most common form and usually
       results from autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells.

      Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) is a life-threatening acute complication of
       diabetes.

Classification of Diabetes Mellitus
1. Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus (T1DM)
      Most common type in children and adolescents.

      Caused by autoimmune destruction of pancreatic β-cells.

      Results in absolute insulin deficiency.

      Patients require lifelong insulin therapy.

2. Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM)
      Caused by insulin resistance with relative insulin deficiency.

      More common in obese adolescents.

      Associated with family history, obesity, and sedentary lifestyle.

3. Monogenic Diabetes
Includes:

       a. MODY (Maturity-Onset Diabetes of the Young): Caused by single gene
       mutations affecting insulin secretion.

       b. Neonatal Diabetes: Diabetes diagnosed before 6 months of age.it May be
       transient or permanent.

4. Secondary Diabetes
• Due to endocrine disorders, pancreatic diseases, medications, or genetic
    syndromes.

Epidemiology
       T1DM = >90% of pediatric diabetes
       T2DM : once rare in children, is now increasingly common, especially in high-
        risk and certain ethnic populations, due to obesity, lifestyle, and genetic
        factors
       Monogenic diabetes is rare but underdiagnosed



Etiology and Pathophysiology of Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus
T1DM results from autoimmune destruction of pancreatic β-cells in genetically
   susceptible individuals (especially HLA genes).

Environmental factors such as viral infections( as enterovirus) may trigger the
   autoimmune process.

Progressive β-cell destruction leads to absolute insulin deficiency.

Insulin deficiency causes: decreased glucose utilization by tissues and increased
   gluconeogenesis.

Increased counter-regulatory hormones (glucagon, cortisol, catecholamines, growth
   hormone) worsen hyperglycemia.

Increased lipolysis leads to ketone body formation and metabolic acidosis.

   Protein breakdown and weight loss.

Osmotic diuresis results in dehydration and electrolyte loss.
Clinical Presentation of Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus:
Classic symptoms:

     Polyuria
     Polydipsia
     Polyphagia
     Weight loss
     Fatigue
     Nocturnal enuresis
     Abdominal Pain
     DKA could be the first presentation :Vomiting, Abdominal pain, Kussmaul
      breathing, Dehydration, Altered consciousness

Diagnostic Criteria

     Diagnosis is confirmed by ANY ONE:
     Random glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL + symptoms
     Fasting glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL
     OGTT ≥ 200 mg/dL
     HbA1c ≥ 6.5%

Management of Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

     Insulin therapy is essential.
     Basal-bolus regimen is preferred.
     Carbohydrate counting and nutritional education are important.
      Regular exercise is encouraged.
      Blood glucose monitoring and HbA1c monitoring are required.

Insulin Regimens

      Intensive insulin therapy (basal–bolus regimen), either by multiple daily
       injections or insulin pumps, aiming to mimic normal pancreatic insulin
       secretion with basal and meal-time insulin is the gold standard regimen.
      Premix insulin regimen using NPH and regular soluble insulin should not be
       considered a best practice treatment for T1DM.
      Newer insulin analogs and technologies (e.g., continuous glucose monitoring
       (CGM), insulin pumps) improve glycemic control and reduce complications.
      Insulin needs in children vary with age, growth, puberty, activity, and illness,
       requiring frequent dose adjustments and individualized care.

Types of Insulin

Bolus (Meal-time) Insulin

      Rapid-acting insulin: Lispro, Aspart, Glulisine.
      Given before meals (10–15 min)
      50-70% of the total daily insulin dose.

      Controls: Post-meal glucose rise

Basal Insulin:

      Long-acting (Given once or twice daily): Glargine, Detemir . or Ultra-long
       acting (Given once daily): Degludec.
      30–50% of the total daily insulin dose.
Management of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

     Lifestyle modification (Healthy diet (reduce calories, balanced meals), Weight
      reduction, Regular physical activity, Limit sedentary behavior (screen time)
     Pharmacological therapy : Metformin (First-line drug in most patients), it
      Improves insulin sensitivity, Insulin Therapy may be indicated if severe
      hyperglycemia , ketosis or DKA
     Education of patient and family is essential.
     Regular blood glucose monitoring
     Management of Comorbidities : Hypertension , Dyslipidemia and Obesity
Prediabetes:
Definition: Intermediate state before diabetes

Criteria

      Fasting: 100–125 mg/dL
      OGTT: 140–199 mg/dL
      HbA1c: 5.7–6.4%

Importance

      High risk of progression
      Preventable with lifestyle

Follow-up & screening in type 1 diabetes mellitus:

Routine Monitoring: Regular follow-up visits are essential
          Monitor: Height, weight, BMI, Growth velocity, Pubertal development and
           Assessment glycemic control (HbA1c/ 3 months, glucose logs)
          Screening of associated comorbidities:

               a. Thyroid Disease Screening: At diagnosis and every 2 years:TSH and
                  Anti-thyroid antibodies
               b. Celiac Disease Screening: At diagnosis (or within first year) and
                  Every 2–5 years thereafter by TTG-IgA (first-line test)
               c. Other Autoimmune Diseases: Addison’s disease, Autoimmune
                  gastritis

          Injection Site Care each visit: examine for Lipohypertrophy and
           Lipoatrophy and Educate on: Proper injection technique, Rotation of sites
           and Avoid reuse of needles.



Clinical Pearls
          T1DM is the most common form of diabetes in children and adolescents.

          Polyuria, polydipsia, and weight loss are the classical presenting
           symptoms of diabetes mellitus.

          Always check blood glucose in any ill child presenting with dehydration,
           vomiting, abdominal pain, or altered consciousness.

          Insulin therapy is mandatory in type 1 diabetes mellitus.

          Basal-bolus insulin regimens provide better glycemic control and greater
           flexibility than premixed insulin regimens.

          DKA may be the first presentation of type 1 diabetes.

Clinical Case

A 12-year-old boy presents with polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, vomiting, abdominal pain,
and deep rapid breathing. On examination, he is dehydrated with Kussmaul respiration and
acetone odor of the breath. Blood glucose is 480 mg/dL, venous pH is 7.1, bicarbonate is 8
mmol/L, and ketones are strongly positive.
Questions and Answers

1. What is the most likely diagnosis?

   The child has Newly diagnosed type 1 diabetes mellitus presenting with DKA

2. How severe is the DKA?

   This is moderate DKA because: pH = 7.1 and bicarbonate = 8 mmol/L

3. What are the important clinical signs suggesting DKA?

   Dehydration, Kussmaul breathing, Vomiting, Abdominal pain, Acetone breath odor ,
    Weight loss and disturbed level of conciouseness

4. What is the first step in management?

   Initial management includes: Airway, breathing, circulation assessment

   Start isotonic fluid resuscitation with 0.9% saline

5. When should insulin therapy be started?

   Intravenous insulin infusion should be started after initial fluid therapy, usually after the
    first hour.

6. What is the most serious complication to monitor for?

   Cerebral edema is the most serious and life-threatening complication of pediatric DKA.
