There are NICE guidelines for the management of the child with
fever


 The child should not be underdressed.

 The use of antipyretic agents either paracetamol or ibuprofen
    can be used.
    They can be given alternatively if unresponsive to a single

 Evidence that antipyretics prevent febrile seizures is lacking.

Message to take home
 Most acute fever is caused by viral infections.

 Causes and evaluation of acute fever differ depending on the
  age of a child.
 A number of children < 24 mo with fever without localizing signs
  can have pathogenic bacteria (occult bacteremia) a potentially
  life-threatening infection.
 Follow-up of the clinical course remains the most sensitive
  indicator in serious conditions.
 Do not abuse antibiotics: FEVER does not mean ANTIBIOTIC.

           1. MENINGITIS AND ENCEPHALITIS

• Meningitis occurs when there is inflammation of the meninges covering the
  brain.
• Viral infections are the most common cause of meningitis.
• Bacterial meningitis may have severe consequences.
• Tuberculous meningitis is rare in countries with low TB prevalence.
• Fungal and parasitic meningitis are rare .
• Causes of noninfectious meningitis (malignancy and autoimmune diseases)

 No delay in the administration of antibiotics and supportive
therapy.
• A third-generation cephalosporin, e.g. ceftriaxone, is the
  preferred choice to cover the most common bacterial causes.
• Evidence suggesting that dexamethasone administered with
  antibiotics reduces the risk of long-term complications such as
  deafness.
               VIRAL MENINGITIS
• Two-thirds of CNS infections are viral.
• Causes include: enteroviruses, Epstein–Barr virus, adenoviruses and
•                  mumps. (Mumps meningitis is now rare due to MMR).
• Viral meningitis is usually much less severe than bacterial meningitis.
• Full recovery can be anticipated.
• Diagnosis of viral meningitis can be confirmed by PCR of CSF; and
  serology
