      Congenital pneumonia

•   Clinical signs include respiratory distress, hypotension, temperature instability, apnea,
    hypoglycemia, lethargy, shock
•   Often indistinguishable from HMD +/-septicemia signs
•   Pathogenesis – aspiration of infected amniotic fluid

                   pneumothorax

Pneumothorax may occur spontaneously
Most commonly, when on mechanical ventilation….unhealthy lung (RDS)
Sudden deterioration of respiratory status
Increased oxygen requirements
Decreased breath sounds and chest movement in affected side
Chest bulge in affected side



Diagnosis :Transillumination test CXR
Chest tube with underwater seal
    Apnea of prematurity



•    Developmental problem with regulation of respiration (Generally resolves by 36 weeks CGA)
•     Incidence inversely related to GA
      Maybe secondary to hypoxia, infection, anaemia, electrolyte disturbance, hypoglycaemia,
seizures, heart failure or aspiration due to gastro-oesophageal reflux
    Cessation of breathing for 20sec leads to bradycardia and desaturation


•    Treatment: gentle physical stimulation, methylxanthines, ventilation

Temperature control
          Temperature control

1- they have a large surface area relative to their mass, so
there is greater heat loss (related to surface area) than
heat generation (related to mass)
2- their skin is thin and heat permeable, so transepidermal
water loss is significant in the first week •
3- they have little subcutaneous fat for insulation •
4-they are often nursed naked and cannot conserve heat
by curling up or generate heat by shivering.
