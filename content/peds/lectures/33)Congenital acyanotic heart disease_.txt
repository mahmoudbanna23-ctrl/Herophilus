--- slide 12 ---
Circulatory changes at birth
At birth:
Clamping the cord shuts down low-pressure system and 
increased systemic vascular resistance causes lungs to inflate with oxygen.
With the first breaths, resistance to pulmonary blood flow falls and the volume of blood flowing through the lungs increases six-fold. This results in a rise in the left 
atrial
 pressure. 
Meanwhile, the volume of blood returning to the right atrium falls as the placenta is excluded from the circulation.
 The change in the pressure difference causes the flap valve of the foramen 
ovale
 to be closed. The 
ductus
 
arteriosus
, which connects the pulmonary artery to the aorta in fetal life, will normally close within the first few hours or days.

--- slide 34 ---
Importance during fetal life
The ducts arteriosus in the fetus is an important conduit that allows 
deoxygenated blood to bypass the collapsed lungs to
 descending aorta and umbilical arteries. 

--- slide 35 ---
Patency factors during fetal life:
 
The placenta produces 
prostaglandins, 
which maintain prenatal patency of the ductus and inhibit the ability of the ductus to contract. 
The ductus arteriosus itself also produces 
prostaglandins and 
nitric oxide-like vasodilators. 
Hypoxia 

--- slide 36 ---
after birth:
During the postnatal period, final closure of the 
ductus
 arteriosus results from:
local vasoconstrictors (like 
endothelin
) in response to higher arterial oxygen, 
removal of placental prostaglandin and a decrease in the number of prostaglandin E2 receptors in the ductal wall.
During the first 60 hours of life, spontaneous closure of the 
ductus
 occurs in 55% of full-term newborn infants
Persistence of duct more than 72h = PDA

--- slide 54 ---
In patients with an ASD smaller than 3 mm in size diagnosed before 3 months of age, spontaneous closure occurs in 100% of patients at 11⁄2 years of age. Spontaneous closure occurs more than 80% of the time in patients with defects between 3 and 8 mm before 11⁄2 years of age. An ASD with a diameter larger than 8 mm rarely closes spontaneously. Spontaneous closure is not likely to occur after 4 years of age.

AVCD 
Common in Down syndrome 
A complete AVSD is a single five-leaflet (common) valve between the atria and ventricles,
 which stretches across the entire atrioventricular junction and tends to leak.
 As there is a large defect there is 
high pulmonary artery pressure.
