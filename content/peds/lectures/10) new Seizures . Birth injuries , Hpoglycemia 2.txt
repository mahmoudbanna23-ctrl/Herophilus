Epidemiology
• The National Neonatal Perinatal Database (NNPD; 2002-03), has reported
  an incidence of 1.5-5.5 per 1000 live-births.

• The incidence was found to increase with decreasing gestation and birth
  weight

• Preterm neonates had almost twice the incidence when compared to term
  neonates (20.8 vs. 8.4 per 1000 livebirths)

• Very low birth weight neonates had more than 4-fold higher incidence
  (36.1 per 1000 live-births).

 1. Epileptic seizures: phenomena associated with corresponding EEG seizure activity e.g. clonic
seizures
 2. Non-epileptic seizures: clinical seizures without corresponding EEG correlate e.g. subtle and
generalized tonic seizures
3. EEG seizures: Abnormal EEG activity with no clinical correlation.

Hypoglycemia
Hypoglycemia in neonates is associated with long term neurological
sequalae
• To know risk factors of hypoglycemia
• To know the clinical signs of hypoglycemia
• Enhance early feeding and IV fluid managements
Definition (no consensus)
• <30mg/dl in the first 24 hours
• <40mg/dl from 1-3 days
• <45mg/dl after 3days
Etiology
LGA
Infants of diabetic mothers
SGA
Decreased glycogen stores
AGA
But low birth weight <2 kg or preterm <37week
 Diseases
Hypoxia, hypothermia, shock, sepsis, RDS, and polycyathemia
  Pathogenesis


-Hyperinsulinemia: PHHI , persistent hyper-insulinemic hypoglycemia
of infancy
-Limited glycogen stores: PT and IUGR.
-Increased glucose use: polycythemia, sepsis, and hypothermia
-Decreased glycogenolysis or gluconeogenesis eg IEM and adrenal
insufficiency

 -The injury can either stretch, rupture or avulse the roots of the plexus from
the spinal cord
 -It is the most common birth-related brachial plexus injury (50- 60%).
-It is a lesion of C5 & C6 nerve roots (in some cases C7).

•Risk factors
•Shoulder dystocia, Fetal macrosomia, Gestational diabetes, Duration of
second stage of labor(over 60 minutes),Breech presentation

Diagnosis
•MRI of the shoulder- may demonstrate shoulder dislocation; presence of
pseudomeningoceles indicates avulsion injury of the affected spinal roots.
•EMG/Nerve conduction studies- presence of fibrillation potentials indicate
denervation
