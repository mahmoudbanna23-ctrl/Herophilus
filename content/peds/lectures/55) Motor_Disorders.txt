• Movement governed by cerebral control
  centers

• Motor commands modulated by sensory
  inputs (joint position, touch, vision,
  vestibular, auditory)

• Signals pass via brainstem → spinal cord →
  anterior horn → peripheral nerves →
  muscles

  Investigations for Myopathy


• Plasma CK: Elevated in Duchenne, Becker, and other
muscular dystrophies
• EMG
• DNA testing: Identify genetic mutations
• Ultrasound & MRI: Assess and monitor muscle pathology
• Muscle biopsy: Histochemistry confirms diagnosis
  History & clinical examination


• waddling gait
• +ve Gower’s sign
• Increased fatiguability
• Ophthalmoplagia, ptosis
  Investigations for Myopathy



• Plasma CK: ++++ dystrophies, inflammatory myopathies
• DNA testing: Genetic mutations
• Electromyography (EMG): Distinguish neuropathy vs
myopathy
• Muscle biopsy: definitive diagnosis
Investigations for Neuropathy


• Nerve conduction studies: Detect velocity and amplitude
abnormalities for sensory & motor nerves
DNA testing: Genetic mutations (e.g., hereditary
neuropathies)
• Electromyography (EMG): Distinguish neuropathy vs
myopathy
• Nerve biopsy: Occasionally needed (e.g., sural nerve)

      Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA)



• AR disorder (of SMN1 gene)

• Degeneration of anterior horn cells
  → progressive weakness &
  wasting of skeletal muscles

• Several types
            SMA Type 1
      Werdnig Hoffmann disease



• Severe, progressive disease
• Diminished fetal movements
• onset birth to 3 months
                SMA Type 1
          Werdnig Hoffmann disease


Signs:
• symmetrical flaccid paralysis,
• Muscle wasting,
• absent deep tendon reflexes,
• tongue fasciculations,
• Weakness of bulbar muscles: weak cry, poor
   suckling
            SMA Type 1
      Werdnig Hoffmann disease



Natural history:
• death by 12 months if untreated

New treatments →gene therapy
           Other SMA Types


• Type 0: neonatal, most severe, very limited survival

• Type 2: onset 3–15 months, sit but don’t walk

• Type 3: onset after 1 year, can walk

• Phenotypes vary by severity and onset

  Bell Palsy and Facial Nerve Palsies

• Bell palsy: isolated LMN
  paresis of VII cranial nerve
• → facial weakness

• Etiology: often post-infectious
  (HSV)

• Symptoms: facial weakness,
  sometimes vesicles (herpes)
Bell Palsy and Facial Nerve Palsies

Treatment:
 • Corticosteroids (early),
 • Acyclovir (HSV)
 • Recovery: complete (months)


• Complication:
 • conjunctivitis
 Facial Nerve Palsy:
Differential Diagnoses

• Brain stem lesions: VI nerve palsy,
  cerebellar signs, UMN signs

• Cerebellopontine angle lesion: VIII
  nerve symptoms

• Systemic associations: coarctation
  of aorta, renal failure: (HTN)

   Duchenne Muscular
    Dystrophy (DMD)

• X-linked recessive, mutations
• dystrophin gene deletion

Symptoms:
• 5 yrs → waddling gait,
  Gowers’ sign, running
  pseudohypertrophy
• progressive
• 10-14 yrs: not able to walk
• Learning difficulties
• Early death: RF
 Duchenne Muscular
  Dystrophy (DMD)

Diagnosis:
• elevated CK,
• genetic testing

Management:
• physiotherapy,
• corticosteroids,
• ventilation support
• Genetic therapy
