Definition of constipation 
a 
delay or difficulty in defecation
, present for 2 or more weeks, and sufficient to cause significant distress to the patient.
Usually involves  
painful or incomplete evacuation 
of hard stools. 

--- slide 3 ---
Epidemiology
constipation is a very common worldwide problem.
It account for 
~ 3%
 of all pediatric office visits and up to 10 to 45% of all pediatric gastroenterology clinic visits.

--- slide 4 ---
Functional constipation 
Most common cause of childhood constipation

--- slide 5 ---
Pathophysiology of FC
The most common mechanism is 
withholding of stools 
that may begin after experiencing a hard, painful or frightening bowel movement.

Disimpaction
Fecal impaction 
is defined as: 
a hard mass in the lower abdomen
a dilated rectum filled with a large amount of stool found during rectal examination, 
or excessive stool in the colon identified by abdominal radiography
positive history of overflow incontinence

--- slide 25 ---
Dietary modification 
A 
balanced diet 
that includes whole grains, fruits and vegetables is recommended as part of the treatment.
Carbohydrates
 (especially sorbitol) found in prune, pear and apple juices can cause increased frequency and water content in stools.
There is little evidence supporting 
fibre
 
supplementation (above the recommended daily intake) in children with constipation.
there is inconclusive evidence that 
probiotics
 are effective.

--- slide 26 ---
Milk intake &amp; constipation 
excessive milk intake can exacerbate constipation
.
there is insufficient evidence that eliminating it from the diet improves refractory constipation.

--- slide 27 ---
Milk intake &amp; constipation 
For 
infants and young children
 unresponsive to adequate medical and 
behavioural
 management, consideration could be given to a time-limited trial of a cow's milk-free diet. 
Intolerance to cow's milk, particularly in children with atopy, has been associated with chronic constipation.

--- slide 29 ---
Behavioural modification
A toileting 
regimen
 that dedicates time for defecation.
A constipated child should have a routine scheduled toilet sitting for 3 min to 10 min once or twice a day. 
There should be no punishment for not stooling during the toileting time.
praise and reward for stooling and the 
behaviour
 of toilet sitting should be offered.

--- slide 37 ---
Prognosis &amp; follow-up
Regular follow-ups are essential.
treatment should continue for 
at least six months
.
Children should have regular bowel movements without difficulty before considering a trial of weaning maintenance therapy. 
The relapse rate for constipation can be quite high, and problems with stooling may persist into adulthood
Approximately 50% of all children with functional constipation recover and are taken off medication within 6 to 12 months
