GH/IGF-1 axis
Isolated GHD

§ reported incidence 1:4000 to 1:10000.

§ usually asymptomatic.

§ Most cases are identified as
 “idiopathic”.

§ Most mutations resulting in isolated
 GHD involve the genes for GH (GH1)
 or the GHRH receptor (GHRHR).
Multiple Pituitary Hormonal Deficiencies

§ A variety of transcription factors are
  involved in pituitary ontogeny,
  differentiation of specific cells within
  the pituitary, and coordinated
  expression of specific hormones.
§ Defects in these factors lead to
  variable degrees of MPHD

§ Early: TSH and GH deficiencies.
§ Later: FSH, LH, PRL, and ACTH
 deficiencies.
When to investigate for GHD?

§ Severe short stature (height
 >3 SD below the mean)
§ Height affected > Weight
§ Height >1.5 SD below MPH
§ Poor growth velocity (< 4cm
 per year)
In GH deficiency

§ Delayed bone age

Treatment of Short stature
Growth hormone treatment


§ GH deficiency is treated with
 recombinant human GH.

§ Daily subcutaneous injection.
How to assess response to GH therapy?
§ The determination of the
 growth response to GH
 treatment is the single
 most important parameter
 in monitoring a child with
 GHD.

§ Increase in height and
 change in height velocity.
