SVT
 
Regular Narrow Complex Tachycardia
Mechanism: reentrant atrioventricular Tachycardia 
HR is usually above 180 beats/min
 In newborns, usually above 220 beats/min  
 Presents with palpitation, syncope , heart failure, cardiogenic shock 
Treatment for hemodynamically stable patients, IV adenosine is the first line 
For hemodynamically unstable patients, synchronized cardioversion is the first line

BACTERIAL ENDOCARDITIS
Bacterial (or infective) endocarditis is a state of septicemia resulting in
infection of endocardium which may occur after :
  • Dental extraction
  • Central venous catheter.
  • Cardiac surgery.
Bacterial endocarditis may affect:
  • Congenitally abnormal valves.
  • Rheumatic valve disease.
  • Cyanotic Congenital heart disease
  • Shunts e.g. VSD, PDA.

--- slide 49 (image41.emf) ---
Causative organisms:
  • Common: Streptococcus viridians, enterococcus faecalis , and Staphylococus
    aureus
  • HACEK group ( Haemophilus, Aggregatibacter, Cardiobacterium, Eikenella, and
    Kingella)
  • Other microorganisms could also cause ( Pseudomonas , Klebsiella , ……)

--- slide 50 (image42.emf) ---
Clinical manifestations:
General non-specific manifestations:
  • Prolonged fever,                      [bolded on the slide]
  • Chills, headache, malaise, anorexia.
  • Easy fatigue.
  • Arthralgia/myalgia.
  • Splenomegaly.
  • Petechiae in the skin and mucous membranes.
  • Clubbing of fingers, splinter hemorrhages under finger nails if diagnosis is
    late.
Cardiac manifestations:
  • Tachycardia.
  • New murmurs or changing character of existing murmurs.
  • Arrhythmias.
  • Acute or subacute congestive heart failure.

--- slide 51 (image43.emf) ---
Embolic vascular dissemination :
  • Major systemic and pulmonary emboli/infarcts and abscesses.
  • Haematogenous osteoarticular septic complications (i.e. spondylodiscitis)
  • Mycotic aneurysms.
  • Intracranial ischaemic/haemorrhagic lesions.
  • Conjunctival haemorrhages.
  • Janeway's lesions.
Immunological phenomena:
  • Glomerulonephritis.
  • Osler nodes and Roth spots.

--- slide 54 (image46.emf) ---
Investigations:
  • leukocytosis.,Anemia
  • ↑ESR (may be low in CHF), ↑ CRP.
  • Positive blood culture (multiple ) (should be repeated several times if
    negative).
  • Echocardiographic evidence of valve vegetations, new valve regurgitation,
    and valve dehiscence
  • CT heart may detect paravalvular abscess
  • Urine: microscopic hematuria.
Diagnosis : Modified Duke's Criteria is used to diagnose infective endocarditis
( 2 major , one major + 3 minor , 5 minor citeria )

--- slide 57 (image51.emf) ---
Treatment:
  • Prophylaxis :  antibiotic prophylaxis against bacterial endocarditis
  • For culture positive patients: appropriate intravenous bactericidal
    antibiotics parenterally in large doses for  4 -8 weeks.
  • If results of blood culture are not available: empirical intravenous
    antibiotics till blood culture results are available
