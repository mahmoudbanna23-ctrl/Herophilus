Definition




      • Neonatal sepsis is a clinical syndrome
        characterized by signs and symptoms of
        infection with or without accompanying
        bacteremia in the first month of life.
                     Definite sepsis

• Clinical syndrome of signs associated with sepsis along with growth of
   bacteria in one or more sterile body sites (positive blood, CSF or BAL
   cultures)
                      Probable sepsis


• Clinical features suggestive of sepsis with sterile cultures and usually with
   supportive tests such as inflammatory biomarkers, Csf or urine, or chest
   xray.
                     Possible sepsis

   • Only clinical features suggestive of sepsis or presence of risk factors for
      early onset sepsis, but not supported by laboratory tests or cultures.
Magnitude of
 problem




          According to the World Health Organization (WHO), in 2016,
          46% of deaths in children underfive were neonates and 7% of
          these were caused by neonatal sepsis.

          30-50% of deaths in the first week of life in developing
          countries are related to sepsis.

  Sepsis
biomarkers

               The challenge of biomarker identification is reflected by
               the fact that over 3000 sepsis biomarker studies have
               been published


                With almost 200 candidate biomarkers evaluated.




             However, there is not a single biomarker that has
             sufficient diagnostic accuracy for the diagnosis of
             neonatal sepsis. The combination of biomarkers or
             their serial measurements may be strategies to
             enhance diagnostic accuracy.
