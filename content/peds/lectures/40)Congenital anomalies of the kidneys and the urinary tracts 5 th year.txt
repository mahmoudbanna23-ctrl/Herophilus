     Congenital anomalies of the kidney and
             urinary tract (CAKUT)

• A spectrum of structural malformations.
• They result from abnormal development of the kidneys and/or
  urinary tract (ureters, bladder, and urethra).
                         Epidemiology

• CAKUT affects over 1% of live births.
• They are among the most common congenital anomalies
  (approximately 30% of all diagnosed congenital anomalies)
• The leading cause of chronic kidney disease (CKD) in children,
  accounting for approximately 40–50% of cases worldwide.
• CAKUT can be sporadic or familial, syndromic or non-syndromic.
• Can be identified during antenatal screening.

        Multicystic dysplastic kidney (MCDK)
• Failure of union of the ureteric bud with the nephrogenic
  mesenchyme.
• Non-functioning large fluid-filled cysts (cluster of grapes) with no
  renal tissue in between.
• A significant proportion of MCDKs involute by 2 years of age.
• Nephrectomy is indicated only if it remains very large or hypertension
  develops, but this is rare.
• Very low risk of malignant transformation.
Polycystic kidney disease
• Some or normal renal function is maintained but both kidneys are
  always affected.
• The main symptoms in childhood are hypertension, and it causes
  chronic kidney disease requiring renal replacement in late adulthood.
• Several extra renal features including cysts in the liver and pancreas,
  cerebral aneurysms, and mitral valve prolapse.

• Pelvic, iliac, or thoracic

                      Hoarse shoe kidney
• Fusion of lower poles of both kidneys.
• The kidney is trapped by the inferior mesenteric artery during ascent.
• The abnormal position may be associated with obstruction, infection
  or stone formation.
• High association with Turner Syndrome.
                   Vesico-ureteral reflux
• Predisposes to recurrent UTIs and renal scarring.
• The ureters are displaced laterally and enter directly into the bladder
  rather than at an angle, with a shortened or absent intramural course.
• International Reflux Study (IRS) classification (VCUG)
                          Duplex kidneys
• Vary from simply a bifid renal pelvis to complete division with two
  ureters.

             Posterior urethral valve (PUV)
• Occurs in males only.
• Causes bladder outlet obstruction (posterior urethral obstruction).
• May present with poor stream, urinary tract infections (UTI), or renal
  failure.
• 30% of patients experience end-stage renal disease or chronic renal
  insufficiency

                  Prune belly syndrome
• Triade syndrome of lax abdominal wall muscles (AWM deficiency),
  intra-abdominal testis and urinary tract abnormalities.
• Associated extra-urological anomalies as cardiac anomalies
• 95% are males
                      Bladder extrophy
• The bladder protrudes from the abdominal wall, and its mucosa is
  exposed.
• The umbilicus is displaced downwards.
• Pubic rami are widely separated.
• The anus is displaced anteriorly and there may be rectal prolapse.
                    Clinical presentation
• Antenatal hydronephrosis on routine ultrasound.
• Accidental postnatal discovery in asymptomatic cases.
• Recurrent urinary tract infections.
• Poor urinary flow.
• Failure to thrive.
• Hypertension and proteinuria.
• Chronic kidney disease

                         Ultrasonography

• Standard imaging procedure of the kidneys and urinary tract.
• Anatomical assessment but not function (detects uurinary tract
  dilatation, stones, and nephrocalcinosis)

• Advantages: Non-invasive, mobile

• Disadvantages: Operator dependent, may not detect all renal scars
    Micturating (voiding) cystourethrogram (M(V)CUG)

• Contrast introduced into the bladder through urethral catheter Can
  visualize bladder and urethral anatomy.

• Detects VUR and urethral obstruction

• Disadvantages: invasive and unpleasant investigation especially
  beyond infancy, high radiation dose, and can introduce infection
                             DMSA scan

• Static scan of the renal cortex Detects functional defects, such as
  scars or areas of non-functioning renal tissue
• Need to wait at least 2 months after a urinary tract infection to avoid
  diagnosing false ‘scars’
                        MAG3 renogram

• Dynamic scan, isotope-labelled substance MAG3 excreted from the
  blood into the urine and measures the drainage.
• Furosemide often given.
                   Bladder flow urodynamics

• To assess how well bladder is emptying together with flow rates.
  Bladder abnormalities can contribute to recurrent UTIs
                Principles of management
1. Antenatal:

• If severe oligohydramnios is present, fetal intervention (shunting) may
  be considered in highly specialized centers.
2. Postnatal medical management:

• Monitoring growth and development.
• Prophylactic Antibiotics: (high-grade VUR or obstructive uropathy
  with recurrent infections) e.g., Trimethoprim or Nitrofurantoin.
• Blood Pressure Control.
3. Surgical Management:

• PUV: Immediate catheterization followed by endoscopic valve
  ablation.

• UPJO: Pyeloplasty if high grade or there is deteriorating renal function
  or symptoms.

• VUR: Most low-grade cases resolve spontaneously. Surgery
  (reimplantation) is reserved for recurrent breakthrough infections
  and/or high grades.
4. Long-term follow up:

• when necessary for CKD patients
