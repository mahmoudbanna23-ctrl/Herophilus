                 Anaphylaxis .. Why?

• Global incidence of anaphylaxis: 50 -112 episodes
  /100 000 person-years (1 episode for every 1000 person)
• Estimated lifetime prevalence is 0.3–5.1%
• In children, the incidence of anaphylaxis ranged from
  1-761/100 000 person-years. (0.76%)
• Recurrent: reactions occurs in 27–54% of anaphylaxis
  patients during a follow-up time of 1.5 years–25 years
Anaphylaxis: definition

• Sudden,
• severe serious life threatening,
• generalized or systemic hypersensitivity reaction,
• due to sudden release of potent biologically active
  mediators from inflammatory cells,
• presented by systemic symptoms and signs.

Factors increasing the risk for
anaphylaxis or making it difficult to
treat:
• Age factors:
  – Infants: difficult to diagnose
  – Adolescents: risky behaviors
• Concomitant disease:
  – Asthma and atopy
  – Chronic lung disease
  – Cardiovascular disease
  – Depression and cognitive dysfunction
• Drugs:
  – B-blockers, ACE inhibitors
  – Medications of psychiatric diseases, alcohol,
    substance of abuse

Anaphylaxis scenario
• a 9 year-old boy came with his teacher running to an ER. he is
  dyspneic with face rash and lip swelling and has a voice of
  stridor and catching his abdomen from pain. he is wearing a
  bracelet of nuts allergy in his hand. the doctor in the ER
  measured his Blood pressure, respiratory rate and heart rate
  and made him lie recumbent and with feet up, and injected his
  lateral thigh with epinephrine autopen. at the same time a
  nurse put a mask of oxygen and nebulizer on his face. they
  waited for his improvement and monitored him regularly till he
  became calm and advised to wait for 4 hours before returning
  home.
