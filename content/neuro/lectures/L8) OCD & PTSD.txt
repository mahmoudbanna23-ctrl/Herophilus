Definition of OCD

Obsessions
Recurrent, intrusive thoughts, urges, or images
causing marked anxiety. Recognised as
products of one's own mind; individual
attempts to suppress or neutralise them.



Compulsions
Repetitive behaviours or mental acts
performed in response to obsessions or rigid
rules, aimed at reducing distress — not
realistically connected to what they prevent.



     Clinical Threshold: Symptoms must be
     time-consuming (>1 hr/day) or cause
     clinically significant distress or functional
     impairment.

KEY TAKEAWAYS



OCD — Summary
Diagnosis
DSM-5: obsessions + compulsions >1 hr/day causing distress or impairment;
assess insight carefully


Neurobiology
CSTC circuit hyperactivation; serotonergic, dopaminergic & glutamatergic
dysregulation


Treatment
First-line: CBT-ERP ± SSRI (high dose, 8–12 weeks). Stepped-care per NICE
guidelines.


Prognosis
Chronic course; suicide risk 10×; early treatment = better outcomes

Definition of PTSD
PTSD is a psychiatric disorder occurring after experiencing or witnessing a traumatic event — disaster, accident, combat, rape, or threat of
death, sexual violence, or serious injury.


  Re-Experiencing                                                        Avoidance
  Flashbacks, nightmares, intrusive memories — trauma relived            Avoiding trauma-related thoughts, feelings, places, activities, or
  as if present                                                          people



  Negative Cognitions                                                    Hyperarousal
  Distorted beliefs; persistent negative emotions; detachment            Hypervigilance, exaggerated startle, sleep disturbance,
                                                                         irritability

  ~70% of adults experience trauma; only 20% develop PTSD. Median onset within 3 months.



  12-month prevalence: 3.5% USA; 0.5–1.0% Europe. Comorbid MDD in 50%; SUD in 35–52%.

DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria for PTSD (309.81 / F43.10)
        Criterion A — Trauma Exposure
 1      Direct, witnessed, learned, or repeated exposure to traumatic event




        Criterion B — Intrusion (≥1)
 2      Flashbacks, nightmares, intrusive memories, reactivity to trauma cues




        Criterion C — Avoidance (≥1)
 3      Avoidance of trauma-related thoughts/feelings or external reminders




        Criterion D — Neg. Cognitions (≥2)
 4      Negative beliefs, blame, persistent negative emotions, detachment, anhedonia




        Criterion E — Arousal (≥2)
 5      Hypervigilance, startle, sleep disturbance, irritability, concentration difficulties




        Criteria F & G
 6      Duration >1 month; clinically significant distress or functional impairment



     Specifiers: With dissociative symptoms (depersonalisation/derealisation) | With delayed expression (onset ≥6 months post-trauma)
Symptom Clusters — Clinical Features

•   Flashbacks — dissociative re-experiencing          •   Social withdrawal and isolation

Management of PTSD: Overview
       Psychological First Aid
       Safety, calming, connectedness, self-efficacy. Avoid formal debriefing (harmful).



       Trauma-Focused Psychotherapy
       TF-CBT (12–20 sessions), EMDR (8–12 sessions), CPT, Prolonged Exposure



       Pharmacotherapy
       SSRIs: Sertraline, Paroxetine (FDA). SNRIs: Venlafaxine. Prazosin (nightmares).



       Adjunctive
       Mindfulness, group therapy, inpatient for complex/suicidal cases




  NICE 2018: TF-CBT or EMDR recommended first-line. SSRIs when psychological therapy
  declined or unavailable.
Trauma-Focused CBT & EMDR
Trauma-Focused CBT                                           EMDR

•   12–20 sessions: psychoeducation, stabilisation, trauma   •   8 phases across 8–12 sessions; bilateral stimulation
    processing, cognitive restructuring                          while recalling trauma
•   Variants: Prolonged Exposure (in-vivo + imaginal), CPT   •   Mechanism: working memory overload reduces vividness
    (targeting "stuck points")                                   of trauma memory
•   60–80% response rate (APA Guidelines 2017)               •   WHO & NICE recommended; especially effective for
