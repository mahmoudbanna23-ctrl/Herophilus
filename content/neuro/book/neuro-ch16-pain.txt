# CHAPTER 16 — CLINICAL APPROACH TO "CERVICAL AND LOW BACK PAIN"

Cervical and Lower back pain is one of the most common reasons for neurological and neurosurgical
consultation. Exacerbated as a result of occupational activity. Lower limb pain (sciatica) is a
common accompaniment to lower back pain but can occur independently. The same is applied for
brachialgia and cervical pain.

## Approach to Back Pain
[Heading in the rounded blue display face, underlined, with a small illustration of a woman holding
her lower back.]

***Lower Back Pain*** is a broad clinical term, not a specific disease. There are a variety of
causes - the **most common cause** is **mechanical** back pain due to muscle, tendon, or ligamentous
strain. In up to **85%** of individuals, no specific cause can be isolated.

### Acute Low Back Pain (LBP)
[Centred heading on a pale grey full-width band.]

- Acute low back pain is a common presenting illness in the primary care setting.
- It is defined as pain below the costal margins and above the inferior gluteal folds with no
  serious underlying pathology.
- As such, acute back pain is often referred to as mechanical low back pain. Acute back pain
  frequently recurs and resolves on its own, regardless of treatment.
- Work-up for a complaint of acute low back pain should focus on ruling out more serious pathology
  by screening for **red flags**.
- The presence of red flags suggests it is not simple mechanical back pain

[BOXED TEXT — rounded box with a dashed dusty-red border; heading centred, underlined, in the same
dusty red.]
> ### Red Flags - Screen with Acute LBP
> - Less than 20 or older than 50, with back pain for the first time.
> - Trauma.
> - The pain is constant and getting worse.
> - Pain is worse at night or when supine.
> - Previous cancer history.
> - Steroid use, IV drug use, risk of UTI and/or immuno-suppressed.
> - Fever and/or weight loss.
> - Neurological signs such as weakness, numbness, saddle-anesthesia or bowel/bladder incontinence

### Chronic back pain
[Centred heading on a pale grey full-width band.]

- Chronic back pain is low back pain of unspecified pathology that **persists longer than 3 months.**
- The evolution of chronic LBP is complex, with physiological, psychological, and psychosocial
  influences.
- Depression is often associated with chronic back pain – **malingering is uncommon**.
- Work-up for chronic back pain (and acute LBP) should include a screen for **yellow flags**
  [the words `yellow flags` are printed in yellow highlighter-coloured type] (special scales and
  scores to assess social, psychological and occupational disabilities) that indicate risk for
  chronic disability.

## Management of Chronic Pain
- ✓ In the absence of red flags, chronic back pain should be treated in a multi-modal but
  conservative fashion.
- ✓ Early return to work is a priority. The patient's ideas about pain and other yellow flags need
  to be addressed with appropriate sensitivity.

*[Centred, in dusty-red italic:]* **An important treatment goal for both acute and chronic back pain
is for the patient to be active as soon as possible.**

### 1. Lumbar disc prolapse
[Numbered topic heading on a pale grey full-width band.]

#### Definition:
Lumbar disc prolapse occurs when the soft **nucleus pulposus** herniates through tear in the
**annulus fibrosus**.

[FIGURE — no number, no caption. Two axial vertebral cross-sections side by side. Left labelled
`NORMAL DISC` with call-outs `Spinal cord`, `Nucleus pulposus`, `Disc annulus`. Right labelled
`HERNIATED DISC` with call-outs `Compressed nerve root`, `Vertebral body`, showing the nucleus
displaced posterolaterally onto the root.]

#### Etiology
[Printed with a Greek epsilon for the initial E: `Ɛtiology`. The book uses this glyph for E-initial
headings throughout the chapter — `Ɛtiology`, `Ɛxamination`, `Ɛnt.` etc.]
- Lumbar spine degeneration.
- Multiple repetitive micro-traumata such as lifting heavy weights.
- Single major trauma such as falling or lifting heavy weight preceding the onset of symptoms.

#### Clinical presentations:
1) **Back syndrome:** This includes back manifestations only.
2) **Leg syndrome:** This includes neurological & mechanical manifestations in one or both legs
   along one or more nerve roots (Monoradiculopathy or Polyradiculopathy).
3) **Classic back & leg syndrome.**
4) **Neurogenic claudication:** This includes unilateral or bilateral monoradicular or
   polyradicular manifestations along the lower limbs on movement and relieved by rest.
5) **Cauda equina syndrome.**

#### Clinical picture:

##### ➤ Symptoms
- **Low back pain**, which is usually dull aching pain in the lower back that is aggravated by
  movement and partially relieved by rest.
- **Sciatica**: Pain in the leg in the distribution of the affected root which is aggravated by
  coughing and sneezing and **relieved by flexion of knee and thigh**.
- **Numbness** or **tingling** along the distribution of the affected nerve root i.e., dermatomal
  distribution.
- **Motor weakness** according to the affected nerve root.

### 2. Sciatica
[Numbered topic heading on a pale grey full-width band.]

#### Definition:
- The most frequent **radicular pain** syndrome of spinal origin.
- Sciatica describes the pain referred down the course of the **sciatic nerve** from the back to the
  buttock, down the back of the leg to the foot.
- Occurs due to irritation of a spinal nerve root associated with disc herniation at **L4-L5 OR
  L5-S1**.
- Pain usually begins in the lower back radiating to the sacroiliac regions, buttocks, thighs, calf
  & foot. Sciatica is a **symptom, NOT A DIAGNOSIS.**

[FIGURE, right margin — no number, no caption. Faint anatomical drawing of the sciatic nerve running
from the sacrum down the posterior thigh and leg.]

#### Causes:
- The **most common cause** of sciatica arises from compromise of the **L5** and **S1** roots, this
  commonly occurs due to:
  - **Inflammatory**: Sciatic neuritis, arachnoiditis.
  - **Nerve Root Compression:**
    - Compression in the vertebral canal by Lumbar disc prolapse, tumor, TB.
    - Compression in the intervertebral foramen due to **root canal stenosis** because of
      osteoarthritis, spondylolisthesis, facet arthropathy, tumors.
    - Compression in the buttock or pelvis by abscess, tumors, hematoma.
  - **Piriformis Syndrome (Wallet sciatica/fat wallet syndrome):**
    - Neuromuscular syndrome that occurs when the sciatic nerve is compressed/irritated by the
      **piriformis muscle** causing pain, tingling & numbness in the buttocks & along the path of
      sciatic nerve.
    - Caused/aggravated by sitting with a large wallet in the affected side's rear pocket.
  - **Diabetic ischemia** of the nerve and roots.
  - **Neoplastic** involvement.

#### Differential Diagnosis
- **Spondyloarthropathy:**
  - Usually seen in the young.
  - Pain does not refer distal to the knee.
  - Bilateral or alternating occurring episodically.
  - Not modified by activity.
  - **Nocturnal pain** is common.
  - Diagnosis: PA Views of pelvis or specialized hip views of the sacroiliac joints.
  - **ESR is elevated**.
  - Rapid response to medication.
- **Intramedullary Tumors (Gliomas):**
  - **Nocturnal pain** is common.
  - Patient will stand or walk to bring relief.
  - Physical activity has no influence on the pain.
  - Spine is sometimes very stiff.
  - Radiographic studies are normal.
  - Diagnosis: **MRI/CT/myelography**.
  - Surgery relieves the patient.
- **Metastatic** lesions or a **multiple myeloma** can result in intense refractory sciatic pain.
- **Infectious discitis.**
- **Infectious sacroiliitis.**
- **Psuedosciatic syndromes:** [sic — `Psuedosciatic`, transposed] some disorders can simulate
  sciatic pain as **periarthritis of the hip.**

#### IMAGING & Diagnosis:
- ✓ **Radiography:** Most occasions radiographs is normal, loss of lumbar lordosis, scoliosis &
  reduced intervertebral disc space may be seen.
- ✓ **CT:** Morphologic abnormalities in relation to a herniated disc. Relative impact on adjacent
  soft tissues. Any neuroforaminal or extra foraminal encroachment.
- ✓ **Myelography:** Excellent for assessing the entire subarachnoid space. Assessment of spinal
  stenosis. Disadvantages: headache, nausea.
- ✓ **Discography:** Often neglected modality. Excellent means of assessing disc pathology.
- ✓ **MRI: STUDY OF CHOICE** for recurrence following discectomy, to differentiate recurrent
  herniation from perineural fibrosis.
- ✓ **Sciatic neuropathy** is a **clinical diagnosis.**

#### Treatment
- **Conservative Management:**
  - **Intermittent bed rest** with movement for short periods in between.
  - Patient should lie on a firm mattress, in the position that feels most comfortable.
  - **Rigid lumbar orthosis** can shorten the duration or obviate the need for bed rest.
  - **Heat/cold application.**
- **Analgesics & Anti-Inflammatory Drugs:**
  - In hyperalgic forms, intrathecal injection of steroids by **LUCHERINI'S technique** can produce
    a remarkable reduction in pain.
  - Epidural analgesia in severe cases.
- **Surgery:**
  - Indications
    - Failure of conservative management.
    - When neurological deficit is present.
  - Chemonucleolysis.
  - Percutaneous discectomy.

#### Rehabilitation
Therapeutic Exercises.

#### Investigations:

##### ➤ Laboratory Investigations
- ✓ It is recommended that no laboratory investigations be ordered for patients with uncomplicated
  mechanical low back pain.
- ✓ If there is suspicion about a systemic cause of low back pain such as inflammatory arthritis,
  connective tissue disease, or infection, then CBC, ESR, and other markers may be appropriate.

##### ➤ Imaging
- ✓ No imaging is recommended for acute mechanical low back pain in the absence of red flags.
- ✓ Keep in mind that imaging can be used to help confirm a clinical diagnosis, but cannot confirm
  that a particular structure is the cause of a patient's pain.
- ✓ In chronic low back pain, it may be appropriate to take AP and lateral lumbosacral x-rays.
- ✓ If the pain radiates below the knees, an MRI may be indicated.

#### Treatment

##### ➤ Conservative treatment:
- It is done for patients who present with low back pain and sciatica without any neurological
  deficit.
- It is usually applied for **4-6 weeks**.
- It includes: Bed rest for **2-4 days** on hard board mattress, activity modification and avoid
  lifting heavy weights, analgesics and NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, reduction of body weight, external
  lumbar support, physical therapy.

##### ➤ Surgical treatment:
###### Indications:
a) Relative indication in cases of failure of conservative measures.
b) Absolute indication for surgery:
   - Cauda equina syndrome.
   - Progressive or persistent neurological motor deficit.
   - Severe, unremitting, leg pain which is not relieved by conservative measures.
   - The presence of visceral complaint.
   - Pain not responding to medical therapy.

## Neck Pain And / Or Arm Pain (Brachialgia)
[Section heading, rounded blue display face, underlined, centred. This is the chapter's SECOND HALF

They are very common clinical problems; **2 out of 3 people** experience neck pain at some point in

## Neurological Causes Of Neck Pain And Brachialgia
[Underlined blue heading, left.]

### 1. Cervical spondylosis
[Numbered topic heading on a pale grey full-width band.]

- It is **the most common cause**.
- This is a degenerative disorder of the cervical spine which can compress the cord or nerve roots.
- Patients often wake up in the morning with a painful stiff neck and diffuse non pulsatile headache
  that resolves in a few hours.
- The lesion is most commonly at **C5/6** and **C6/7**, and focal signs are likely to be at these
  levels.
- Wasting and weakness of the small muscles of the hands, particularly weakness of abduction of the
  little finger, is also frequently seen.
- Restricted neck movement is always present with significant cervical spondylosis.
- Bladder dysfunction, or the finding of long-tract signs or symptoms, indicates spinal cord
  compression.

### 2. Cervical disc prolapse
[Numbered topic heading on a pale grey full-width band.]

#### Definition:
Cervical disc prolapse occurs when the soft nucleus pulposus herniates through tear in the annulus
fibrosus.

#### Ɛpidemiology:
The most commonly affected levels are **C5-C6** and **C6-C7** levels.
Usually occurs in the middle age group from **30-50 Years**.

#### Clinical picture:
- If prolapse is posterolateral, it will cause nerve root compression i.e. **radiculopathy**.

##### ➤ Symptoms:
- Neck pain and stiffness.
- Pain radiating down to the arm and hand is called **brachialgia** which is exacerbated by neck

- Paresthesia and numbness along the affected dermatome.
- Motor weakness in long standing compression.
- Sphincteric dysfunction in cases of myelopathy.

##### ➤ Signs:
###### a. Neck signs:
- Loss of cervical lordosis.
- Local tenderness.
- Limitation of spinal movement.
- Spasm of paravertebral muscle.

###### b. Signs of radiculopathy:
- Motor weakness in the group of muscles supplied by the compressed nerve root.
- Sensory changes or dermatomal affection along the compressed nerve root.
- Decreased reflexes according to the root affected.
- The distribution of pain in the upper limb varies according to the nerve root affected.

###### c. Signs of myelopathy:
- Spastic quadriparesis.
- Hyperreflexia below the level of compression.
- Clumsiness and ataxic Gait disturbance.
- Sphincteric disturbance.

*[Centred, bold, over three lines:]* **The most common nerve root affected is C6 nerve root in
C5-C6 disc herniation and C7 nerve root in C6-C7 disc herniation and to a lesser extent is C5 root
in C4-C5 disc herniation.**

### 3. Ulnar Ɛntrapment
[Numbered topic heading on a pale grey full-width band; the Greek epsilon glyph is used for the E.]

- Entrapment of the **ulnar** nerve in the **cubital tunnel** at the **elbow**.
- Numbness and tingling and pain are referred to the **hypothenar eminence** and the **little and
  ring fingers**.
- Symptoms are often worse at **night** when the patient sleeps with a **flexed elbow**, and they
  may interrupt sleep.
- There is **decreased sensation** over the palmar aspect of the ring and little finger, and there
  may be decreased sensation on the medial dorsal aspect of the hand and ulnar two fingers.
- In severe cases, there is **wasting and weakness** of the small muscles of the hand which can lead
  to **clawing** of the fourth and fifth digits.
- **Diagnosis:** Nerve conduction studies and EMG.
- **Treatment:** Avoiding prolonged elbow flexion. Surgical decompression.

#### Treatment of Neck Pain According to Onset:

Treatment of neck pain mainly depend on etiology & onset of condition. Main treatment is to treat
underlying causative factors. Sometime emergency first aid is necessary for traumatic neck pain.

- **Acute neck pain**
  - Acute pain occurs suddenly from an injury or stress.
  - Most of the time neck pain will resolve itself within **7-10 days** with rest, ice, and over the
    counter pain relievers.
  - For symptoms that persist longer than a few weeks. **Conservative therapy** can include
    anti-inflammatory pain medication, injections, physical therapy, acupuncture, and chiropractic
    care.
- **Chronic neck pain**
  - Chronic pain is defined as neck pain lasting longer than **3 months**.
  - People who have not found relief through conservative treatments and suffer from chronic pain
    may benefit from pain management by Physical therapy or surgical intervention.

#### Management Modalities:
- **Conservative treatment**
  - **Rest** is important for all acute neck pain with others remedy. Duration of rest depend on
    patient prognosis & causative factors. Rest with proper positioning of neck that is seen by
    therapist or physician. Avoidance of heavy lifting.
  - Analgesic, Anti-inflammatory, Injections.
  - **Physical therapy:**
    - Ice in acute stage or cryotherapy followed by moist heat, contrast therapy & hot compression.
    - Exercise therapy: Neck muscle strengthening exercise. Neck mobilizing exercise. Neck
      Manipulation technique. Cervical traction approach.
    - Orthotics device: Cervical collar, Halter collar, Hallo [sic — for `Halo`] cervical traction,
      Contour types of pillows.
- **Activities for daily living for correction of life (ADL):** Regular exercise. Proper lying,
  sitting & working. Maintain proper body alignment. Maintain appropriate **Ergonomics**.
- **Surgery Indications:**
  - **Brachialgia** not responding to medical treatment.
  - Progressive **neurological deficit** due to root compression.
  - Manifestations of **cord compression** i.e., myelopathy.
  - ➤ **Procedures:**
    - **Discectomy** (with or without fusion): The surgeon removes herniated disc material that is
      pressing on a nerve root or the spinal cord.
    - **Cervical spinal fusion:** Selected bones in the neck are joined (fused) together.
    - **Spinal Decompression:** Pressure is reduced on the spinal cord or spinal nerve roots by
      removing part of a bone or disc.
    - Artificial disc replacement.
    - Percutaneous radiofrequency neurotomy.
