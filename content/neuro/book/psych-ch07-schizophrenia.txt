# 7 — Schizophrenia

### Terminology:
[Section heading in the rounded blue face on a pale-blue full-width band — the chapter's standard
top-level heading form.]

Despite the origin of the term (Greek **skhizein** meaning "to split", and **phrēn** meaning
"mind"), schizophrenia **does not imply** a "**split personality**" or "**multiple personality
disorder**" — a condition that is often confused in public perception. Rather, the term means a
"**splitting of mental functions,**" reflecting the presentation of the illness.

In 2002, the term for schizophrenia in Japan was changed from schizophrenia to (**integration
disorder**) to reduce stigma.

### Prevalence & epidemiology:
- Occurs in **adulthood** at **similar rates worldwide**.
- About **1%** of people are affected during their lifetime.
- Symptoms typically begin in **late adolescence** and **young adulthood**.
- Slightly more common in **males** with an **earlier age of onset**.
- **Comorbidity:**
  - Patients are likely to have additional conditions, including major depression and anxiety
    disorders.
  - The lifetime occurrence of **substance use disorder** is almost **50%**.
- **Social Impact: Long-term unemployment**, **poverty**, and **homelessness**.
- **Life Expectancy:** Reduced by **10 to 25 years than the average life expectancy** due to
  increased **physical health problems** and a **higher suicide rate**.

### Genetics:
- **First degree relative:** Risk is **6.5%**.
- **Monozygotic twins:** Risk is **more than 40%**.
- **One parent affected:** Risk is about **13%**.
- **Both parents affected:** Risk is nearly **50%**.

### Environment:
- **Risk Factors:**
  - Living environment, poor housing, unemployment.
  - Drug use.
  - Prenatal stressors.
  - Social isolation, immigration, racial discrimination.
  - Family dysfunction.
  - Childhood trauma, death of a parent, being bullied or abused.
- **Parenting Style:** Seems to have **no major effect** on cause, although **supportive parents**
  lead to better outcomes than **critical or hostile parents**.

### Biological Theories: The Dopamine Hypothesis
Based on **Dopamine dysregulation**, which is likely secondary to:
- Abnormal GABA-glutamate interaction.
- Serotonin effect on the GABA-Glutamate system.

### Dopamine dysregulation in schizophrenia:
- **Positive Symptoms:** Associated with **Excessive dopamine (DA)** in the **associative part of
  the nigrostriatal pathway**.
- **Cognitive Function (e.g., working memory):** Associated with **Inadequate DA** in the **frontal
  cortex**.
- **Negative Symptoms:** Associated with a **deficit in dopamine** in the **mesolimbic pathway
  (ventral striatum)**.
- **At-Risk Subjects:** A **hyperdopaminergic state** in the **striatum** is recorded in subjects
  pre-morbidly vulnerable (the **transition group**, where **30%** will become schizophrenic).
- **Primary Cause:** Dopamine dysregulation is **not** a primary abnormality but **secondary** to a
  more proximal abnormality (probably **GABA-glutamate interaction**).

### Phases:

#### ✓ Premorbid phase:
- Refers to the state of functionality **prior to the onset** of a disease or illness.
- Includes **at risk mental state (ARMS)** subjects.
- Patients act **odd or eccentric** and **avoid relationships** (e.g., **paranoid & schizotypal**

- Those who go on to develop schizophrenia may experience **transient or self-limiting psychotic

### Diagnostic Criteria for Schizophrenia (DSM-5)

**A. Characteristic Symptoms: Two (or more)** of the following, each present for a significant
portion of time during a **1-month period** (or less if successfully treated):
1. **Delusions**
2. **Hallucinations**
3. **Disorganized speech** (e.g., frequent derailment or incoherence)
4. **Grossly disorganized or catatonic behavior**
5. **Negative symptoms** (i.e., **affective flattening**, **alogia**, or **avolition**)

[NOTE BOX — pale-blue full-width box, lightbulb glyph top left, centred word `Note`. The box uses
blue type for the emphasised phrases.]
> Only **one Criterion A symptom** is required if **delusions are bizarre** or **hallucinations**
> consist of a voice keeping up a **running commentary** on the person's behavior or thoughts, or
> **two or more voices conversing** with each other.

**A.** *Onset of the disturbance*, one or more major **areas of functioning** such as work,
interpersonal relations or self-care are **markedly below the level achieved prior to the onset**
(or when the onset is in childhood or adolescence, failure to achieve expected level of
interpersonal, academic, or occupational achievement).

**B.** *Duration:* Continuous signs of the disturbance persist for ***at least 6 months.*** This
6-month period must include ***at least 1 month*** of symptoms *(or less if successfully treated)*
that meet Criterion A (**active-phase symptoms**) and may include periods of prodromal or residual
symptoms. During these **prodromal or residual** periods, the signs of the disturbance may be
manifested by **only negative symptoms** or two or more symptoms listed in Criterion A present in
an **attenuated form** (e.g., odd beliefs, unusual perceptual experiences).

**C.** *Schizoaffective and Mood Disorder exclusion:* Schizoaffective Disorder and Mood Disorder
with Psychotic Features have been ruled out because either
1. No major depressive or manic episodes have occurred concurrently with the active-phase symptoms;
   or
2. If mood episodes have occurred during active phase symptoms, their total duration was **brief
   relative** to the duration of the active / residual periods.

**D.** *Substance/general medical condition exclusion:* Disturbance is not due to the direct
physiological effects of a **substance** (drug of abuse, medication) or a **medical condition**.

### Positive and Negative (deficit) symptoms:

#### Positive symptoms
- Those that most individuals **do not normally experience** but are **present** in people with
  schizophrenia (manifestations of **psychosis**).
- **Include:** (manifestations of **Psychosis**)
  - Delusions
  - Disordered thoughts and speech
  - Hallucinations (tactile, auditory, visual, olfactory, and gustatory). Also typically related to
    the content of the delusional theme.
- Generally, **respond well to medication**.

#### Negative symptoms
- **Deficits** of normal emotional responses or thought processes.
- **Include:**
  - Flat expressions (little emotion)
  - Poverty of speech (Alogia)
  - Inability to experience pleasure (Anhedonia)
  - Lack of desire to form relationships
  - Lack of motivation (Avolition)
- Contribute **more to poor quality of life**, functional ability, and burden on others.
- **Response:** Response to medication is often **limited**.
- **Association:** Often linked to a history of **poor adjustment before the onset**.

### Course & Prognosis:
- **Schizophrenia** is a major cause of **disability**, with **active psychosis** ranked as the
  **third-most-disabling condition** (after quadriplegia and dementia).
- Patients have a **lower life expectancy** than the general population due to **poor general
  health** and **high comorbidities**.
- The course of illness is **graded** in first-admission patients with schizophrenia, indicated by
  episodes of illness, symptomatology, and social impairment over **5 years**.

### Differential diagnosis:

#### Other Psychiatric illness:
- **Psychotic symptoms** may appear in several other mental disorders, **Bipolar disorder** and
  **Borderline personality disorder**.
- **Delusions ("non-bizarre")** are present in **Delusional disorder**.
- **Social withdrawal** is found in **Social anxiety disorder**, **Avoidant personality disorder**,
  and **Schizotypal personality disorder**.
- *Note:* **Schizotypal** symptoms are similar but **less severe**.
- **OCD:** Occurs with schizophrenia more often than chance; distinguishing **obsessions** from
  **delusions** can be difficult.

#### Other medical & neurological illness:
- Metabolic disturbance, Systemic infection, Syphilis, HIV infection, Epilepsy, and Brain lesions,
  Stroke, Multiple Sclerosis (MS), Hyperthyroidism, Hypothyroidism, and Dementias (e.g.,
  Alzheimer's).
- **Delirium:** Must be ruled out; distinguished by **visual hallucinations**, **acute onset**, and
  **fluctuating level of consciousness**, and indicates an underlying medical illness.

#### Substance abuse:
**Drug intoxication** and **drug-induced psychosis** (e.g., **amphetamines**).

[⚠️ The medical/neurological DD is a bare eleven-item list. **Not one of the eleven is expanded**;

#### Hospitalization:
Hospitalization may occur for severe episodes either **voluntarily** or (if mental health

### Medication (Antipsychotics):
- **First-line treatment** which reduces **positive symptoms** in **7 to 14 days**.
- **Continued use** decreases the risk of **relapse**.
- **Efficacy:** Requires **D2 receptor occupancy** in the associative striatum of optimally **65%**
  for an adequate portion of time.
- The choice of which antipsychotic to use is based on **benefits**, **risks**, and **costs**.

[FIGURE, centred — no number, no caption. A brain with a pill bottle labelled `MEDICATION
(ANTIPSYCHOTICS)` and scattered capsules. Decorative; carries no text content.]

#### Classification:
- **Typical antipsychotics:** **Haloperidol, Chlorpromazine** (Dopamine antagonist)
- **Atypical antipsychotics:** **Risperidone, Olanzapine, Clozapine** (Dopamine and Serotonin
  antagonist)
- **3rd generation:** **Aripiprazole** (Partial Dopamine agonist antagonist) or dopamine
  stabilizers.

#### Mechanism of action:
- **Typical antipsychotics** are not particularly selective and also block dopamine receptors.
  **Blocking D2 receptors** is thought to produce some unwanted side effects that typical
  antipsychotics can produce.
- Atypical antipsychotics have a similar blocking effect on D2 receptors, however, most also act on
  serotonin receptors, especially **5-HT2A** and **5-HT2C** receptors.

#### Adverse effects:

**1. Common (≥ 1% and up to 50% incidence for most antipsychotic drugs):**
- Extrapyramidal side effects (by blocking **D2 receptors at nigrostriatal pathway**, particularly
  common in patients on **first generation** antipsychotics), which includes:
  - **Akathisia** — an often-distressing sense of inner restlessness.
  - Dystonia.
  - Parkinsonism.
  - Tremor

##### Treatment by Anticholinergic medications and Propranolol
[Heading in the rounded blue underlined face, over three lines. **This is the chapter's only
statement of EPS treatment**, and it names two drug classes with no agent, no dose, no route and no
frequency.]

##### Tardive dyskinesia:
- **Slow, repetitive, involuntary and purposeless movements** (face & lips).
- Appears after **chronic use**; frequently **irreversible**.
- More frequent with **Typical** antipsychotics (haloperidol). However, it can occur with other
  antipsychotics.
- It tends to **resist treatment**.

##### Hyperprolactinemia:
Due to **blocking D2 receptors at tuberoinfundibular** pathway (rare with clozapine, quetiapine and
aripiprazole), which can cause:
- Galactorrhea — unusual secretion of breast milk.
- Gynecomastia
- Sexual dysfunction (in both sexes)
- Osteoporosis.
- Hypotension

### Treatment of resistant cases & non-compliant patients:
- *Clozapine* is an effective treatment for those who respond poorly to other drugs
  ("**treatment-resistant**" or "**refractory**" schizophrenia), but it has the potentially serious,
