# Anxiety And Related Disorders

### Background

- **Fear** is "physiological" from a **known** thing; **Anxiety** is fearing from the **unknown**.
- The main difference between physiological fear & anxiety and related **disorders** is the
  presence of **significant distress & impairment** in activities & relations.
- **Prevalence:** Common conditions (2% to 13%). Female to male ratio is about 1.5:1
- **Features:** Psychotic features are **absent**. Insight is **intact**.
- Mostly caused by an interaction between genetic predisposition & external stressors or traumas,
  leading to appearance of different disorders → They are **illnesses** NOT weakness of personality
  nor weakness of religious beliefs. They are underdiagnosed & undertreated.
- **Classification (DSM-5 Categories):**
  - I. Anxiety disorders.
  - II. Obsessive compulsive & other related disorders.
  - III. Trauma- & stressor- related disorders.

[NOTE BOX — pale-blue full-width box, lightbulb glyph top left, centred word `Note`.]

> - Comorbidities between **different types** of anxiety disorders are common.
> - Also, Comorbidities between **depressive symptoms (or disorder)** and different types of
>   anxiety disorders are common.
> - Finally, Comorbidities with **substance use disorders** are common.

## CLINICAL FEATURES: (according to DSM-5 criteria)

### A. Generalized anxiety disorder

- This disorder is characterized by **excessive anxiety and worry** occurring for **6 months or
  more**. The worrying is **difficult to control**.
- **Anxiety & Worry** are accompanied by at least **3 of the following symptoms**:
  - Restlessness or feeling keyed-up/ on edge.
  - Being easily fatigued.
  - Difficulty concentrating or the mind going blank.
  - Irritability.
  - Muscle tension.
  - Sleep disturbance (such as difficulty falling or staying asleep or unsatisfying sleep).
- The disorder causes significant distress & impairment in activities & relations.

[NOTE BOX]
> Although not a diagnostic feature, **suicidal ideation** and **completed suicide** have been
> associated with generalized anxiety disorder.

Differential diagnostics have to be excluded.

### B. Specific phobia:

- **Exposure to certain objects** (e.g., **animals** like cats, **environmental** factors like
  heights, or **blood-injuries** like needles) provokes **immediate & marked fear** that is **out
  of proportion** to the actual danger.
- This condition lasts for **6 months or more**, causing the patient to always try to **avoid**
  these objects.
- The disorder results in significant distress & impairment in activities & relations.
- Differential diagnoses have to be excluded.

### Panic disorder:

- This disorder involves **recurrent episodes** of **panic attacks**, which are defined as a
  **sudden onset** of **intense fear or discomfort**, usually reaching a **peak within 10
  minutes**.
- Attacks are associated with a group of systemic symptoms, including at least **4 of the
  following**:
  - Palpitations, pounding heart, or accelerated heart rate
  - Sweating
  - Trembling or shaking
  - Shortness of breath or feeling of smothering
  - Choking sensation
  - Chest pain or discomfort
  - Nausea or abdominal distress
  - Feeling dizzy, unsteady, lightheaded, or faint
  - Derealization (feeling of unreality) or depersonalization (being detached from oneself)
  - Fear of losing control or going crazy
  - Fear of dying
  - Paresthesia (numbness or tingling sensations)
  - Chills or hot flashes
- Patients experience **fear of recurrent attack** and worry about the **implications** or
  consequences (e.g., losing control, going crazy, dying), resulting in **significant behavioral
  changes** (e.g., avoiding situations or locations).
- The disorder results in significant distress & impairment in activities & relations.
- Differential diagnoses have to be excluded.

### Agoraphobia:

- Exposure to **2 or more** of the following situations provokes **immediate & marked fear**:
  - Using public transportation
  - Being in open spaces
  - Being in closed places
  - Standing in line or in a crowd
  - Being outside the home alone
- The patient always tries to **avoid** these situations because of thoughts that **escape might be
  difficult**.
- This fear is **out of proportion** to the actual danger and persists for **6 months or more**.
- The disorder results in significant distress & impairment in activities & relations.
- Differential diagnoses have to be excluded.

[NOTE BOX]
> Again, GAD, phobias & panic disorder are illnesses not weakness of personalities nor religious
> beliefs

### C. Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD):

- **Obsessions** are defined as **recurrent, persistent & usually unwanted thoughts, urges or
  images.**
  - The patient knows that they are a **product of his/her mind.**
  - Their presence causes **marked distress.**
  - The patient tries to **ignore or stop them**, but it is **not easy** to reach this goal
    (**stoppage of obsessions**).
- **Compulsions** consist of **repetitive behaviors** (e.g., **handwashing**) or **mental acts**
  (e.g., **counting**) performed as a response & as a **trial to stop obsessions**.
- **Obsessions & compulsions** are **time consuming** (taking **more than one hour per day**).
- The **duration** of symptoms is **6 months or more**.
- The disorder causes significant distress & impairment in activities & relations.
- Differential diagnoses have to be excluded.

### D. Posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD):

- **Exposure to a severe stressor** is required (e.g., combat violence, sexual assault, physical
  assault, near death experience, or witnessed death of a loved one).
  - The stressor can be experienced **directly** (witnessed directly) or **indirectly** (hearing of
    exposure of a close relative to severe trauma).
- Diagnosis requires symptoms in **4 symptom domains** for **more than one month**:
  - **Intrusion:** Intrusive memories, flashbacks (acting as if traumatic events were recurring),
    nightmares.
  - **Avoidance:** Avoidance of trauma-related cues, thoughts, or feelings.
  - **Negative cognition and mood:** Memory impairment regarding the trauma, self-blame, negative
    evaluation of self.
  - **Alteration of arousal and reactivity:** Insomnia, hypervigilance, irritable or aggressive
    behavior.
- The disorder causes significant distress & impairment in activities & relations.

[FIGURE, top right, no number, no caption: a painting of a soldier asleep in bed with a wife beside
him, a thought-cloud above showing a battlefield explosion and troops.]

[TWO NOTE BOXES SIDE BY SIDE, each with the lightbulb glyph.]

> **Note**
> **Acute Stress Disorder (ASD) vs. PTSD:**
> - If the condition starts **immediately after trauma** and persists for **at least 3 days up to a
>   month**, it is called **Acute Stress Disorder (ASD).**
> - If the condition starts or persists **after one month**, it is named **Posttraumatic Stress
>   Disorder (PTSD).**

> **Mnemonic "TRAUMA"**
> - **T**raumatic event (exposure)
> - **R**e-experiencing (flashbacks, nightmares)
> - **A**voidance (of stimuli associated with trauma)
> - **U**nable to function (social/occupational impairment)
> - **M**onth (Duration > 1 month)
> - **A**rousal (hypervigilance, startle response)

### Differential diagnosis:

In all types of anxiety and related disorders, the following must be excluded:
- **Other types of anxiety disorders.**
- **Other types of mental disorders** (e.g., if a patient fears insects because they believe they
  are "advanced tools," this suggests **psychosis**, not phobia).
- **Substance/medication induced** anxiety & related disorders (e.g., cannabis).
- Anxiety & related disorders due to **another medical condition** (e.g., hyperthyroidism,
  myocardial infarction).

### Investigations:

- Investigations to exclude differential diagnosis (e.g., **TSH** or **urine screening for illicit
  drugs**).
- Investigations for **psychopharmacological treatment initiation or follow up** (e.g., **serum Na
  level** for patients starting treatment, due to fear of **hyponatremia** as a side effect).

[NOTE BOX]
> Choice of investigations (if needed) depends on the Clinical assessment

### Prognosis:

- Generally, most cases show **significant improvement** with treatment.
- **Better prognosis** is associated with:
  - Absence of comorbid **substance use disorder**.
  - Absence of comorbid **depression**.
  - Presence of **good social support**.

### Treatment:

#### Psychotherapy:
- This is the **main line of treatment** for **mild cases**.
- In **moderate & severe cases**, a **combination** of psychopharmacological & psychotherapeutic
  intervention yields better results than pharmacotherapy alone.
- **Common methods:**
  - Relaxation techniques
  - Systematic desensitization
  - Thought stopping technique
  - Exposure & response prevention

#### Psychopharmacology:
- Needed for **moderate & severe cases**.
- **Clinical improvement** typically starts in **2-8 weeks** (note: **4-12 weeks for OCD**).
- **Duration:** The **least duration** of treatment is **12 months** while the patient is **free of
  symptoms**.
  - **Stoppage** before 12 months increases the likelihood of **relapse**.
- **Important Notes:**
  - Intervention is **NOT harmful**.
  - They **WON'T cause addiction** (except for **benzodiazepines** if used for a prolonged period).
  - Usage **DOESN'T mean weakness** of personality (anxiety disorders are **Illnesses**, NOT
    weakness).

## printed p.201  (PDF p.213)  [psychiatry half]

#### 1. Antidepressants:

[NOTE BOX]
> The term "Antidepressants" is a **misnomer** because these drugs can be used for treating
> **different conditions other than depression**. **Anxiety disorders** are an example of this
> misnomer.

**a. Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs):**
- Includes: **fluoxetine, paroxetine, sertraline, fluvoxamine, citalopram & escitalopram**.
- Used for **all types** of anxiety.
- **Side effects: nausea, headache,** initial **irritability, insomnia** or somnolence, & **sexual
  side effects**.

**b. Other Antidepressants:**
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs):** e.g., **clomipramine** (for **OCD**).
- **Serotonin Noradrenaline Reuptake Inhibitors (SNRIs):** e.g., **venlafaxine**.
- **Trazodone:** Used for treatment of **insomnia & initial irritability** at the start of SSRIs.

#### 2. Other medications:
- **Benzodiazepines** (minor tranquilizers): Used for **NOT more than 1 month**.
- **Propranolol:**
  - Used for **social anxiety** occurring only in **definite situations** (e.g., exams).
  - Given **one hour before** the situation.
  - **Contraindicated** in conditions like **bronchial asthma**.

** ⚠️ NO DOSE, NO UNIT, NO ROUTE and NO mg-per-day figure is printed for ANY drug on this page.
The only quantitative prescribing instructions in the whole chapter are TIMING ones:
benzodiazepines "NOT more than 1 month"; propranolol "one hour before the situation"; treatment
duration "12 months"; onset "2-8 weeks (4-12 weeks for OCD)". **

### Hospitalization: Usually **not needed** except in situations such as:
1. **Suicidal trials**.
2. Severe **panic attacks**.
3. **Observation & investigations** of differential diagnosis (e.g., **substance use disorder**).
4. Presence of **comorbid conditions** (e.g., severe **comorbid depression**).

### ECT (Electroconvulsive therapy):
- Generally, the response of anxiety & related disorders is **poor** for ECT.
- However, ECT can be used for treating **suicidal trials** or **severe comorbid depression**.
