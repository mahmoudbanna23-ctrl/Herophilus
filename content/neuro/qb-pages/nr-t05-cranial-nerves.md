### Printed boxes, verbatim

**69.** This is the classical history of a post-viral Bell's palsy. There is some evidence to support a short course of steroids and acyclovir. You need to be confident that; there are no features of Guillain-Barre (test reflexes), or brainstem vascular disease or space occupying lesion. The neurological examination of the cranial nerves thus needs to be completed with care.

**78.** The Abducent nerve has a long, relatively unprotected course, especially in the subarachnoid space. Because of this, it is particularly vulnerable to compression

**79.** In spite of other risk factors in this patient (DM, HTN) but acute onset and symptoms of ICP (headache nausea vomiting) support diagnosis

## Defects and notes recorded

1. **⚠️ Q67 — the correct answer is not offered.** `L8) Cranial nerves` states the duration of a TN
   paroxysm as *"several **seconds**"*. The shortest option printed is **one minute**. Keyed A as the
   only choice within range; the entry says plainly the fact to carry is **seconds**.
2. **Q70 option c** ("Response to gabapentin therapy") is a fair distractor but gabapentin *is* on
   `L8`'s TN drug list, so the option is defensible-looking. Key **D** is right — a treatment
   response is not a diagnostic criterion. Noted in the entry.
3. **Q68** — `L8`'s differential list prints *middle-ear infection / cholesteatoma* **before**
   *diabetes mellitus*, but presents them as a differential, **not a ranking**. The lecture never
   states which is second commonest. Bank's key (DM) is standard teaching; noted.
4. **Q84's stem describes two complications** — synkinesis ("asymmetry, especially with smiling")
   *and* crocodile tearing. Only the tearing is diagnostic of one thing, so key **B** stands. Noted.
5. **Q75 vs Q79 are a deliberate trap pair.** Q75 keys *hypertension and diabetes* for an **isolated**
   palsy; Q79 gives a **diabetic hypertensive** patient and keys **raised ICP**, because that stem
   adds headache, nausea and vomiting. Cross-referenced in both entries.
6. **Q80 vs Q83 look contradictory and are not.** Eye care is the *primary concern*; steroids are the
   *first-line treatment*. Preventing the worst outcome ≠ treating the disease. Explained in both.
7. **Q85 vs Q86** — most **dangerous early** (corneal ulcer) vs most **common late** (contracture).
   Two different rankings on two different timescales.
8. **Q87 caption discipline.** `imgAlt` reads only *"Clinical photograph of a patient's face"*.
   Naming the side or the LMN pattern would answer the question — the caption renders as a visible
   `<figcaption>` **above the options**.
