### Printed boxes, verbatim

**55.** Hemorrhage or blood is hyperdense on CT, and does not need contrats to be visualized. MRI is not the best choice here; however, it is best used to identify "ischemic" stroke as early as 30 minutes from symptom onset.

**56.** Epidural hemorrhage is bleeding in the space between the dura and the skull caused by injury to the middle meningeal artery. For that artery to leak, it needs to be injured by a head trauma.
Subdural hemorrhage, on the other hand, is caused by bleeding of bridging cortical veins into the space between the dura and the arachnoid matter. Risk factors for this types of hemorrhage include alcoholic and elderly, since they have shrunken brain matter, leading to a stretch on these veins. Another risk factor is anticoagulation. In absence of thes risk factors, a subdural bleed is unlikely.
Subarachnoid hemorrhage is mainly caused by rupture of aneurysms of the circle of willis in the subarachnoid space, the space between the arachnoid and pia matter. Risk factors include HTN, marfan's syndrome, autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease, and Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome

**57.** Signs of meningeal irritation include photophobia, nuchal rigidity, and severe thunderclap headache. This is because it occurs in the subarachnoid space where CSF circulates. This space is rich in pain-sensitive structures, including blood vessels, meninges, and cranial nerves. Blood in this space irritates these structures, causing an intense and sudden "thunderclap headache," often described as the **worst headache of a person's life**. Additionally, in SAH, blood in the subarachnoid space mixes with CSF, spreading widely and irritating the meninges throughout the brain and spinal cord. This diffuse irritation leads to an overwhelming and widespread pain response.
Epidural hemorrhage, on the other hand, Occurs between the skull and dura mater. This space is less pain-sensitive because the dura mater itself has limited innervation. While the expanding hematoma can cause headache due to increased intracranial pressure, it is generally less abrupt and less severe than the headache from SAH. Additionally, in an epidural hemorrhage, the bleeding is localized, and the pain is typically due to localized compression of the dura and adjacent brain tissue. This pain may be severe but is not as sudden or intense as in SAH

**58.** As mentioned before, confirming the diagnosis of an intracranial hemorrhage can be made via non-contrast CT. Once the diagnosis is made, efforts should be made to figure out the etiology, which includes aneurysms and AVF rupture and neoplastic hemorrhage. The latter two can be visualized via an MRI, and the first one via a CT angiography

**59.** While a noncontrast CT is the best at detecting SAH, such a diagnosis may not always show on the CT. In presence of high index of suspicion, a lumbar puncture (LP) should be done. RBCs from the leaking vessel go to the CSF, where their hemoglobin will be broken down, giving off a yellow colour called: xanthochromia. This finding is diagnostic of a bleeding the subarachnoid space. Presence of RBCs or consistently hemorrhagic CSF are other findings consistent with the diagnosis.

**60.** Once a diagnosis of SAH has been made, it's pointless trying to prove the same diagnosis using another investigation. This excludes LP. CT angiography will be needed to identify the etiology. While an MRI angiography would yield results similar to that of CT angiography, it's pointless to go for a more expensive investigation, when a cheaper and less time-consuming one can do the job for you.

**61.** The answer to this question is pretty straightforward. Choices A and B are excluded because anticoagulation and thrombolytic therapy should neber be prescribed to patients with history of intracranial hemorrhage, let alone it being a current presentation.
Immediate treatment of SAH entails surgery, where the vessel that ruptured is coiled or dealt with somehow. Later, to prevent future vasospam in the cerebral blood vessels, which is bound to happen in any SAH, nimodipine, a calcium channel blocker should be prescribed. It is important to note that before surgery, patients will need bed rest, analgesia and hydration.

1. **⚠️⚠️ Q58 — the printed key names an option that is NOT on the list.**
   The key reads **"E. C & D. MRI Brain and CT Angiography."** But option **c** is *ECG* and option
   **d** is *CT Angiography*, so option **e ("C and D")** means **ECG + CTA**. The two investigations
   the box actually argues for — **MRI brain and CT angiography** — are **b + d**, a combination the
   question never offers. Keyed **E** as printed; the entry says plainly that the answer to carry is
   MRI + CTA. Verified at 200 dpi on both pages.

   positive within minutes), yet Q50 is keyed the other way. Recorded in **both** entries per the
   standing ruling; neither key altered.
